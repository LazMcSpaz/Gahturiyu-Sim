## What the reviewers agree on
- **Creativity is at target** (4.3). The spines themselves read at about 4 on coherence (reviewer A).
- **The dealt stories score 3.6** because of the roller and placement layer, plus a few one-clause gaps. No spine needs rewriting.

## What remains (ranked; all narrow)
1. **Made-on-demand people need a home slot** bounded like a place. Five cases, e.g. a Wayhouse scribe "buying paper" 6.5 days away, and a watch sergeant asking questions 3.6 days from his town.
2. **Bars and lordship checks on every slot,** including doors and secondary slots. Kotqot is barred by W1-A's own text but was rolled; a Free Lord's men seized papers in the Binders' town.
3. **Every noun the crisis rests on appears on an earlier card,** and every proof or tell the player holds is answered in *Held*.
4. **Turns leaned on but never shown,** and legal rules first stated at the crisis: one clause each, planted a chapter earlier.
5. **Variety at set level:** *Copy it before it burns* turns up in 6 of 8 stories and "Burn your copy" in 5 (cap it across the whole story, not only routines); towns and the leader recur.
6. **A routine-card swap can drop a party** a later card needs (the elder pupil in 7602).
7. **Readability:** one long sentence per card buries the logic; allow two short ones.
