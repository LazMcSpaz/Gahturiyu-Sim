# Story-Quality Rubric for Generated Quests (v1, 2026-10-26)

How tests judge a generated world beyond "the rules pass". This is built from well-worn storytelling and quest-design principles (sources at the end). Reviewers score each section **1–5**, cite card ids as evidence, and name the cheapest rule change that would raise the score.

---

## A. Coherence: does it hang together?
*From story-coherence research: a coherent story is one where readers agree on what happened and why.*

| Check | Pass looks like |
|---|---|
| **Causal chain (but / therefore)** | in every chain (main, faction, linked pair, fallout), each card follows from the one before: *therefore*, or *but*, never just *and then*. A reader can answer "why is this happening here, now?" from the card and what came before. |
| **No loose threads** | every plant is paid off or deliberately seeds something (a minor quest, an echo). Every revelation is published. Every move reaches the world, through its chapters or its fallout. Every odd job's loose thread is tied by the reveal. |
| **World consistency** | no person, place or thing contradicts itself across cards: states, deaths, owners, who knows what, standing, where things are. |
| **Transitional sense** | each step between story beats is believable given what came before: no teleporting motives, no villain doing something their want doesn't explain. |
| **No conflicts** | reuse caps, secrets and the separation of stories all hold. Nobody is in two places at once, and nobody is dead on one card and asking for help on another. |

## B. Scale and span: is the whole map alive?
*From open-world design: avoid a single "guide rope" through an empty map. Scatter points of interest so players make their own routes, and give each place its own pull.*

| Check | Pass looks like |
|---|---|
| **Every place has something** | every settlement has at least one quest. Every region has several, including one worth going out of the way for. |
| **Tied to the place** | a quest uses what makes its location itself: its landmark, trade, hook or faction presence. A quest that could happen anywhere is a weak quest. |
| **Not one guide rope** | the main path isn't the only content. Its journeys cross regions full of side content, and side quests point onward to neighbours. |
| **Faction presence is felt** | each guild and minor faction shows up in several places, not only at its headquarters. |
| **Even-handed density** | no region is starved while another is crammed. Remote places have fewer quests, but stranger ones. |

## C. Depth: are some quests rich?
| Depth level | What it is |
|---|---|
| 1 | a standalone card |
| 2 | a card with a twist, a secret truth, or a real dilemma |
| 3 | a card connected to others: a linked pair, an echo, fallout, a minor quest |
| 4 | part of a story arc (main or faction) |

- **Pass:** every region has at least one level 3+ thread. Every great town has a level 4 presence.
- **Mix:** most side quests are level 1–2, and that's correct: depth needs contrast.

## D. Each unit works as a story
*The five commandments of storytelling, applied to every unit: a card, a chapter, a whole story.*

| Beat | In our cards |
|---|---|
| **Inciting incident** | the premise: something has happened that won't fix itself |
| **Progressive complications** | the hard parts, the twist, the next card in a chain. Each one **raises** the pressure; repeating the same level is a fault. |
| **Crisis** | a *best bad choice* or *irreconcilable goods*: no obvious right answer. The card's ways and endings must make the player pause. |
| **Climax** | the ways through: the player acts |
| **Resolution** | the endings. In a chain, the resolution creates the next pressure. |

**Also checked:**
- **Stakes are set before the choice:** the player knows what's at risk before deciding.
- **Curiosity:** information is withheld and paid out (secrets, quiet plants, revelations), so the player *wants* the next piece.
- **Personal over spectacle:** the pressure lands on a named person the player can care about (the heart, a requester, a Low or Middle character with a want).
- **No easy moral labels:** the other side often has a point, and villains want something comprehensible.

## E. The whole: is it enjoyable?
| Check | Pass looks like |
|---|---|
| **The main story has an arc** | it escalates, has a midpoint turn and a personal cost, and its climax pays off what was planted. The Return shows a changed world. |
| **Faction stories feel distinct** | read side by side, the five guild stories aren't the same story renamed. |
| **Variety** | families, premises, twists, ways and villains aren't repetitive across the world. |
| **Memorable people** | several characters a player would remember by name and want: not just roles. |
| **Familiar and new** | grounded in recognizable situations, with enough twists to surprise. |
| **Side quests know their place** | none overshadows the main story's stakes. |
| **A sense of theme** | the world's canon tensions (old houses vs upstarts, bondage and freedom, the green, belonging) recur, so the world feels like it is *about* something. |

## Sources
- [Story Grid: The Five Commandments and phere](https://storygrid.com/5-commandments-and-pheres/): inciting incident, progressive complications, crisis (best bad choice / irreconcilable goods), climax, resolution, at every unit of story
- [The But & Therefore Rule (Parker and Stone)](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/): causality between beats
- [Fabula Entropy Indexing: Objective Measures of Story Coherence](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.07472): coherence as reader agreement; transitional and world coherence
- [10 key takeaways from the quest design of Cyberpunk 2077 and The Witcher 3 (Paweł Sasko)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/10-key-takeaways-from-the-quest-design-of-cyberpunk-2077-and-the-witcher-3): curiosity, emotion over spectacle, real dilemmas, stakes before choices, novelty with familiarity
- [The Witcher 3 story director on great quests (Marcin Blacha)](https://www.notebookcheck.net/The-Witcher-3-story-director-explains-what-makes-a-truly-great-quest.1400273.0.html): no obvious answer; the player should pause
- [Good Side Quest Design (Chris Avellone)](https://gamebanshee.com/k4wqq): tie quests to their location; side quests never overshadow the main story
- [How Nintendo Solved Zelda's Open World Problem (GMTK)](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/how-nintendo-solved-zeldas-open-world): no guide rope; scattered points of interest; each place gives a reason to go
