# Family: Contest — Draft (v3.3)

## 1. Situation
**A matter has been sworn to an agreed contest (a bout, a race, a match of skill), and what rides on it makes winning hard, or makes winning wrong.**
- **vs. Dispute:** in Dispute the stake is still argued, by truth, a deal, or taking a side. In Contest, **both sides have already sworn**, publicly, to let the contest decide. A Contest card and a Dispute card may never share a stake.
- **vs. Reckoning:** Reckoning answers a **known wrong** (its *contest* ways are one road to justice). Contest has no acknowledged wrong; the contest is the settlement, or the sport.
- **vs. Threat:** there's no danger to a community, only what rides on the contest.
- **Culture-free in shape.** Custom-specific terms (a Qotiro proving circle, a Highfolk guest-bout) are the story layer's wording, never a shape rule. Rites and Trials stay parked.

## 2. Card fields
- **Requester**
- **Principals:** the two sides the matter is between. The requester is usually one of them.
- **Champion:** who competes for the requester's side. The player by default, a named person the player backs or prepares, or **the requester in person** (marked *champion: requester*; §8's standing rule still applies).
- **Opponent:** who competes for the other side (the other principal, or their champion).
- **Opposing slot:** the **other principal** (assembly §5).
- **Ring-keeper:** the judge, starter or ring master (the arbiter). May be *absent* only for *First blood or last*.
- **What rides on it:** a thing, a right, a ladder place, a title, a ring honor, a prize, or a reputation fact.
- **Contest kind:** *bout*, *race*, or *match of skill* (a ploughing match, a swim, a shoot).
- **Terms** (bouts only): first touch, yield, ring-out, or blood.
- **The ground:** a place.
- **Truth:** what the other side is really fighting for (§5b). Every Contest card rolls one (rulebook, *No blank card*).
- **The oath:** a recorded public past-event fact that both principals swore to abide by the result. *The ladder* uses the contest's open rules instead.
- **Optional slots:** sponsor, fixer, paid fighter, trainer, sparring partner, plus any person a way names.
- **Premise, ways through, twist, possible endings.**

## 3. Kinds of ways through
- **Prepare:** train, learn the opponent, get the right gear.
- **Rules:** use, bend or change the terms; work through the ring-keeper.
- **Edge:** an advantage outside the contest itself: nerve, scouting, turning someone.
- **Stakes:** change what rides on it, or who's watching.

## 4. Premises

| Premise | Requirements | Ways through (roll 2–3, ≥2 kinds) | Separation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Champion's bout:** the two principals have sworn to settle a matter by a contest, and the requester needs a champion | the oath; the matter is a title, a ring honor, a ladder place, or anything already sworn to the ring (never a debt or a quarrel still being argued: that's Dispute); terms not *blood*; a ring-keeper | train with someone who has faced the opponent and lost (prepare; fills trainer); get the opponent's old sparring partner to say what he's been working on (edge; fills sparring partner); argue for terms that suit your champion (rules; *removed if the other principal outranks the ring-keeper*); put the contest in front of the people whose good opinion the other principal needs, so a dirty win costs him more than a loss (stakes) | n/a |
| **The fix:** the requester knows the contest is rigged, and needs it run straight, or won anyway | a **fixer**, known to the requester (an unknown fixer is Investigate). The fixer **may be the other principal, or the other side's champion**: one entry in two roles, an explicit exception to rulebook *One entry, one role per card*; a ring-keeper who isn't the fixer; a **fix type**: *bought judge*, *paid fighter*, or *loaded gear* | win anyway, against everything the fix arranged (prepare); make the fixer's money ride the other way (stakes; *removed if the fixer is Top*); expose the fix in front of the crowd, mid-contest (rules); **plus one by fix type:** get the ring-keeper to swap the bought judge (rules; *bought judge*; *removed if the fixer outranks the ring-keeper*), turn the paid fighter (edge; *paid fighter*; fills them), or get the gear tested before the ring-keeper (rules; *loaded gear*) | exempt (crime) |

Wrongs that are published or recorded elsewhere (a sabotage, an earlier death) don't count, as long as the challenge itself is about something else, such as a ring's honor. | get the terms changed to yield before the day (rules; *removed if the challenger outranks the ring-keeper*); find the old custom that lets the challenged withdraw with honor (rules); win without killing, and make it plain you could have (prepare); get the challenger's own people to call it off (stakes); learn what the challenger is really fighting for (edge) | exempt (public offense) |
| **The ladder:** an open contest (a tournament, a circuit, a town's ladder, a fair's race) where entry has a price, and the prize changes a life | an open contest with an entry price (a sponsor, a token, a fee); at least one rival entrant; a ring-keeper; **the prize changes a life:** a lord's guard post, a purse that clears a named debt, a stall licence | find a sponsor, and accept what they'll want (stakes; fills sponsor; names the price); buy a ladder place from someone who wants out, and learn why they want out (stakes; fills that entrant); challenge your way onto the ladder by the old rule (rules); study the field, and who's carrying an injury (edge); pair up with a rival for the early rounds (edge; fills that rival) | n/a |

**Standing:**
- **Principals and champions** (amends rulebook §2.3):
  - Contest's 2+ tiers test applies to **principals, not champions**. If the principals are 2+ tiers apart, both must compete through champions.
  - *The ladder* is exempt, because an open contest takes all comers.
- **Every premise** keeps at least 3 ways across at least 2 kinds after its own filters.

## 5. Lists
- **Requester reasons** (rows of the shared list, rulebook §7; v7). Every card fills `reasons.requester`:
  - **honor:** a name to keep
  - **standing:** a ladder place, or a guild rank
  - **peace:** settle it without blood
  - **a promise:** they swore they'd see it through
  - **proof:** that a school, a style or a teacher is worth something
  - **need:** the prize clears a debt or feeds a household
- **Opponent and challenger reasons:**
  - pride
  - money
  - ambition
  - desperation
  - **revenge** (*requires* a past-event fact naming the requester's side; it's a grievance, not an acknowledged wrong)
- **Fixer motives:** the shared want list.

## 5b. Truth: what the other side is really fighting for
The other side is the other principal (on *The ladder*, the main rival entrant; on *The fix*, the fixer). The truth is a secret fact on them. Each value is a truth row, with its shape beside it (rulebook, *Shapes*). *A way out* is one row, though two premises use it.

| Premise | Truth values | Ways that reach it |
|---|---|---|
| Champion's bout | *the matter itself* (the title or honor, no more) *(confirming)*; *what it brings* (the win opens something else: a post, a marriage, a debt cleared; names it) `hidden-aim`; *to keep it from the requester* (a grudge; needs a past-event fact) `old-grudge` | the sparring-partner way (edge) |
| The fix | *the money* (the fixer's own bets) *(confirming)*; *someone above* (the fixer was told to; names who) `someone-above`; *to protect someone* (only with *The fix protects someone*) `protects-someone` | expose the fix mid-contest (rules); turn the paid fighter (edge) |
| First blood or last | *the stated reason* (it really is about the slight) *(confirming)*; *a cover* (the bout hides another aim: the challenged's post, their silence, an inheritance) `hidden-aim`; *a way out* (the challenger wants to be stopped, with honor) `wants-out` | learn what the challenger is really fighting for (edge); get the challenger's own people to call it off (stakes) |
| The ladder | *the prize* (the same need as the requester's) *(confirming)*; *the sponsor's price* (fighting to pay off what a sponsor wants) `owes-a-backer`; *a way out* (wants off the ladder) `wants-out` | study the field (edge); buy a ladder place from someone who wants out (stakes) |
| The teaching bout (Dueling Society signature) | *the ladder place* *(confirming)*; *to see the fighter break* (needs the old killing's past event) `old-grudge`; *a way out* (the challenger wants to lose to the fighter, with honor) `wants-out`. Homed in `quest-guild-kits.md` | learn what the dead man's family wants from this bout (edge); match them against someone who can take it (rules) |

- **Twists that fix the truth:** *Needs it more* takes *what it brings*, *the prize* or (on *The teaching bout*) *the ladder place*; *Old blood* takes *to keep it from the requester*, or *to see the fighter break* on *The teaching bout*; *The fix protects someone* takes *to protect someone*. These twists are rolled before the truth (rulebook, *Roll order*), and the card counts the row's shape once.
- **The teaching bout** is homed in `quest-guild-kits.md` (Dueling Society); its row is listed here so every Contest premise has one. The two must match.
- **The truth is reachable:** at least one rolled way reaches it (rulebook, *The truth is reachable*).

## 6. Twists
- **None.**
- **Needs it more:** the opponent's side has more riding on it than the requester's: a family's living, a last chance.
  - *Requires:* a dependent or family link on the opponent's side.
  - Group: **the other side is right**.
- **The fix protects someone:** the rigging is there to keep someone from being hurt. A worse fighter was being steered away from a killer, or a judge was bought to stop a blood match.
  - *Requires:* a person the fix protects (a secret fact).
  - Group: **the other side is right**.
- **Old blood:** the challenger's grievance is real. The requester's side did something to them long ago, never named as a wrong.
  - *Requires:* a past-event fact.
  - **It hides behind another name.** Kin share a family name (rulebook, *Name check*), so a challenger who carried his dead father's name would give the twist away. He goes by his mother's line or a byname instead, and the kin link is a secret fact.
  - Group: **you started it**.
- **The champion was promised something:** the requester's champion (not the player) is competing for a reward the requester can't or won't give (freedom, a waived debt, a marriage), and winning will expose it.
  - *Requires:* the champion isn't the player, plus a creditor, employer or dependent link from the champion to the requester.
  - Group: **innocents pay**.

| Twist | Champion's bout | The fix | First blood or last | The ladder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Needs it more | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ |
| The fix protects someone | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ |
| Old blood | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ |
| The champion was promised something | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ |

- **Tells** (rulebook rule 8, v7): a card whose truth is non-confirming names one public fact that doesn't fit the told story in `details.tell` (the challenger has paid his debts this week). *Needs it more*, *The fix protects someone*, *Old blood* and *The champion was promised something* show theirs in the pitch, the place, or every rolled way.

**Debatable outcome:** with *Needs it more*, both winning and settling for a draw-down (*called off*, *settled before the contest*) are fair choices.

## 7. Possible endings
Each with its default **`costs`**, who loses by it (rulebook rule 7, v7). What failure endings write is mechanics §4.
- won (costs: the other principal; the one ending that costs only the other side)
- won, terms bent (costs: the requester's side, whose name carries it)
- lost (costs: the requester)
- thrown (lost on purpose) (costs: the requester, or the champion who threw it)
- settled before the contest (costs: both principals, each giving part)
- called off (costs: the side that withdrew)
- turned deadly (bouts only: on *blood* terms, when *First blood or last* fails, with a twist that makes it so, or on a premise built on that fear, such as the Dueling Society's *The teaching bout*) (costs: the fallen and their kin)
- fix exposed (*The fix* only) (costs: the fixer and the requester: the contest is voided, so nothing is won; with *The fix protects someone*, the one it protected)

## 8. Standing effects
- **Ring-keeper outranked:** "work through the ring-keeper" ways are removed when the party they'd rule against outranks the ring-keeper. The table marks each one.
- **High or Top principal:** a High or Top principal never competes in person, unless they have the trait *known fighter* (a dueling-society leader, a war-chief). Otherwise their side always uses a champion. A Low or Middle requester may always compete in person.
- **Bribe-style ways** are removed when the fixer is Top (rulebook §4).

## 9. Registry interactions
- **Creates:** opponent, ring-keeper, the ground, sponsor, fixer, paid fighter, trainer, sparring partner.
- **The ground** is a place:
  - kinds: dueling ring, fair circle, tavern yard, courtyard, standing stones, a race course, a field;
  - it is often a faction presence (`faction-places.md` §5b: Dueling Society rings and challenge stones; the Last Wave's Circle).
- **What rides on it** is a registry entry: a thing, a right, a reputation fact, or a title.
- **The oath** is a public past-event fact on both principals.
- **Secrets:** the truth (§5b) is a secret fact on the other side. The person the fix protects is a secret fact. *Old blood's* old deed is a public past event that nobody connects, because the challenger goes by another name (§6).

## 10. Main-story and faction-story use
- **Main story:**
  - allowed on **opening and middle cards** of any chapter;
  - **never a turn card,** including the hook's turn card. This amends assembly §4 (Trials: "any family") and main-quest §9 (the hook turn card).
- **Faction stories:** allowed in any phase, and as a crisis. Contest's opposing slot is the other principal, so the enemy fills that.
- **Side quests:** normal.

## 11. Test rolls
*(Illustrative.)*

1. **Champion's bout.** Two smiths have sworn before the town's ring-keeper that a bout decides who holds the title "Master of the Forge Fair". Both are Middle. Terms: yield. Ways: train with the smith who lost to the opponent last year (prepare); bring the bout before the guild elders whose trust the opponent needs (stakes). **Holds.**
2. **The fix (paid fighter) + The fix protects someone.** A fair's bookmaker (Middle, the fixer) has paid the reigning champion's next opponent to fall. The fix keeps that champion, who has killed twice in "friendly" bouts, away from the requester's son. The ring-keeper (Middle) knows nothing. Ways: turn the paid fighter (edge); expose the fix mid-contest (rules). **Holds.** The protected son is a secret fact.
3. **First blood or last, sympathetic High challenger.** Lady Imre (High) has challenged a tutor (Low), through her champion, on blood terms over a slight to her late son. That's two tiers apart, so both sides use champions; the player stands for the tutor. Requester: the tutor's sister. Old blood: the tutor once kept the son's drinking from his mother, and that is a past event. Ways: find the custom that lets him withdraw with honor (rules); learn what she's really fighting for (edge). **Holds.**
4. **The ladder + The champion was promised something.** A mill girl climbs the Ring's ladder as a moneylender's champion. The prize clears her family's debt to him. He promised to forgive the rest if she wins, and he can't, because he sold the debt on. Ways: find a sponsor (stakes); study the field (edge). **Holds.**
5. **Champion's bout + Needs it more.** A Banner Lord's steward and a tanner have sworn that a race to the river ford decides which of them keeps a ferry licence. The steward's runner is a bondsman promised his freedom if he wins; he is the father of two (a dependent link). Ways: argue for the course (rules; the steward doesn't outrank the ring-keeper); get his old training partner to talk (edge). **Holds.**
6. **The fix (bought judge), the fixer is the other principal, the requester competes in person.** A drover (Middle) and a horse-dealer (Middle) have sworn that a race at the spring fair decides who keeps the fair's grazing right. The dealer has bought the course judge. The drover rides herself (*champion: requester*). Truth: *someone above*, since the dealer owes the fair's lord, who wants the grazing for his own herd. Ways: get the ring-keeper to swap the bought judge (rules); expose the fix mid-contest (rules; reaches the truth); win anyway (prepare). **Holds.**

## 12. Decisions made without Laz
1. **Contest's ways** are about the situation around the contest. The contest itself is the player's to fight, which is mechanical-layer work.
2. **Champions** let parties far apart in standing still meet.
3. **The oath requirement** separates Contest from Dispute: no card argues a stake that's already sworn to a contest.
4. **New twists** were placed in existing groups:
   - *Needs it more* and *The fix protects someone* in **the other side is right**;
   - *Old blood* in **you started it**;
   - *The champion was promised something* in **innocents pay**.
5. **Main-story use is limited to non-turn cards** for now.
6. **Flagged for Laz:** the logic reviewer still sees some overlap with Dispute. The oath rule is the line drawn. If you'd rather, Contest could stay faction-only (Dueling Society and Last Wave) instead.
