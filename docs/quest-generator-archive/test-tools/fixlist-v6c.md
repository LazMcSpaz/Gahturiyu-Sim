# Rule changes v6c: wider guild kits that still make sense (Laz, 27 Oct)

**Why.** Four rolled worlds (`D/narratives/guild-narratives.md`) showed that guild stories vary *between* guilds but not *within* one. Dueling told the same plot three times out of four, the Writers repeated one crisis three times, and Lantern's C1 comes up 70% of the time. Endings, chapter 1's move and the odd job's hint never vary. Laz: "apply your fixes, run it again … Assembling a random kit of parts will not always make a story that makes sense unless we're very careful to make sure that it does."

So there are **two goals, equally weighted:** more distinct stories per guild, and every assembled story holding together as cause and effect. Wider pools without a coherence rule would trade repetition for nonsense.

## 1. The throughline: how parts are made to fit (Home: faction-stories, new rule *The throughline*)
Every enemy entry says what the enemy **wants** and what they can **use**. Every other part says what it **needs** from the enemy, and what it **hits**. A part can be rolled only when its needs are met. Then the story must pass the **throughline test** before any card is made.

**Means tags** (what an enemy can use; an enemy has 2–3):
- `paper`: writs, contracts, grants, records, the law's own instruments;
- `coin`: buying people and debts;
- `men`: armed force of their own or a lord's;
- `inside`: people inside the guild who answer to them;
- `name`: rumour, standing, the power to disgrace;
- `faith`: the house's authority, penance, heresy charges;
- `kin`: family ties, marriages, heirs;
- `craft`: a skill the guild also sells, such as forgery, fighting, copying or escort.

**Hit tags** (what a part damages): `place` (a hall, a yard, a crossing), `thing` (a record, a relic, a contract), `name` (the guild's or someone's standing), `people` (members, the heart, the player), `trade` (the guild's living, its routes and clients).

**Each part carries:**
- **Enemy:** `want` (one of the canon wants: greed, revenge, preservation, desperation, cover-up, ambition, faith), `means` (2–3 tags), `reach` (local / region / realm), and a one-line **scheme**: what they are doing and why, in the kit's words.
- **Stake:** its `hit` tag, plus `needs` (the means that can threaten it).
- **Chapter-1 move** (new table, §4 below): `needs` means, `hits`.
- **Chapter-2 blow** (target + event): `needs` means. For example: arrested needs `paper` or `men`; outlawed needs `paper` and region reach; framed needs `inside` or `name`; members taken needs `men`, `paper` or `coin` (a debt); hall seized needs `men` or `paper`; leader struck down needs `men` or `inside`; heart captive needs `men`, `coin` or `paper`; disgraced needs `name` or `inside`; estranged needs `kin` or `name`.
- **Crisis:** `needs` means, the `hits` it brings to a head, and **no fixed enemy list**. Kit-specific exceptions stay only where the crisis is really about one enemy (the paired truce; a Schism needs a Within enemy).
- **Ending:** `fits` (the hits or crisis kinds it can close).

**The throughline test** (checked by the planner; the roller rerolls on a fail):
1. The enemy's want explains the scheme.
2. The chapter-1 move, the blow and the crisis each use **at least one of the enemy's means**.
3. The move, the blow and the crisis all **hit the stake or people tied to it** (the heart, the guild's members, the player), so the story is about one thing.
4. **They climb:** the move is public, the blow is personal, and the crisis decides the stake.
5. **The reveal's kind** (hidden hand, the cost, the old debt) matches the enemy entry.
6. **One sentence** can be written: "Because [enemy] wants [want], they [scheme]; they [move], then [blow], and it comes to a head when [crisis]." The roll records it as the story's `throughline`. If the sentence needs an "and also", the roll fails.

## 2. Wider kits (Home: guild-kits; rules in faction-stories §11)
Every kit, including the Holy House, gets:
- **about 8 enemies:** the existing ones plus new entries, spread over Within / Above / Beside and over wants and means, so no two enemies share a want **and** a means set;
- **about 7 crises,** each with `needs` and `hits` instead of an enemy list;
- **about 6 chapter-1 moves** (the new table);
- **an ending pool of 5–6:** 3 are rolled to fit the stake and crisis, plus the seat ending when it applies;
- **about 5 odd jobs** whose loose threads differ in **kind**, not only in premise:
  - a person who answers to someone else;
  - a thing that shouldn't exist (a forged seal, a second copy);
  - a place that's wrong (an empty house, a fresh grave);
  - a pattern (the third time this month);
  - a lie in a record;
- **hearts with 4–5 types,** unchanged or slightly widened.

Variants (a/b/c) stay, changing who, why and how inside an entry. New entries follow the canon in `world-factions.md` and `leaders.md`, and the existing kits' tone. Anything invented is marked *(Claude)*. Every twist and premise named must already exist in rulebook *Twist groups* and the family docs, so **no new families or premises**. Signature premises stay as they are.

## 3. Rolling fairly (Home: faction-stories §9 and §12)
- **The roll order is "fewest legal options first":** before each guild's roll, count each unrolled guild's legal enemy+crisis pairs under the caps and roll the smallest next. No guild gets the leftovers.
- **Pairing the syndicates:** about **1 in 3** worlds, down from about half (§10).
- **Writ fatigue:** across a world's guild stories, at most **2** chapter-1 moves, blows or crises whose main means is `paper` aimed at the guild (writs, licences, outlawing). This adds to the lever caps.
- **The heart's captivity** is already spread by dealing the targets; keep it.

## 4. Chapter-1 moves (Home: guild-kits, per kit; the rule in faction-stories §6 phase 3)
Each kit has a table of about 6 open moves, each a public act with `needs` and `hits`. Examples: a rival hall opened next door, clients poached, a route closed, a member bought publicly, a record called in by a court, a public challenge. The roll picks one that passes the throughline test. Moves still obey the *Event ceiling* and the lever caps.

## 5. Not repeating the last game (Home: mechanics, new rule *Fresh stories*; the roller takes it as an input)
The game keeps a small file between playthroughs: the kit entries each finished or abandoned world used (enemy, crisis and odd job per guild, plus the main arc and shape). A new world's roller treats the last **two** worlds' entries as **unavailable** unless nothing else fits. Nothing else carries over. The shape layer just reads a list; saving it is mechanics.

## Numbers to check after the change (the sample rerun)
Four fresh worlds, rolled with no "recent" list so the base variety shows:
- no guild repeats an enemy entry or a crisis entry in more than 2 of 4 worlds;
- no single crisis entry above about 35% over 400 seeds;
- every story passes the throughline test;
- an independent reviewer judges every summary for sense and for creativity.
