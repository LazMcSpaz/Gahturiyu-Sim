# v7: authored spines, starting with the Writers (Laz, 27 Oct)

**Laz's decisions:**
- Guild stories are built from **authored spines**, not assembled from parts.
- **Work on one guild first, the Writers, and get it right before spreading out.**
- Apply the book's scope-safe proposals (A1–A11, B1, B5, B7).
- Add three new features: **spy, scout and survey quests** (C1), **a closing account** (C2) and **the world remembers how you solved it** (C5).

**Why spines.** The independent review of 24 rolled guild stories (`D/narratives/v6d/review-v6d.md`) found the parts approach "converging on legality, not sense":
- coherence stayed at 3.0 after every join was fixed, because breaks moved down to the next untagged detail;
- every story that scored 4 came from an enemy whose kit entry already spelled out its spine.

So we write the spine whole, and the generator varies everything **around** it.

## 1. What a spine is
A spine is one complete guild plot in outline, written by hand, with slots where the world fills in people, places and things. One enemy has 2 spines (aim: 16 for the Writers, 2 per enemy W1–W8). The generator picks a spine; it never picks the parts of one separately.

**A spine carries:**
1. **id, title, enemy** (an existing kit enemy, keeping its want, means, reach, shape, reveal kind and side).
2. **The scheme:** 1–2 sentences of what the enemy is doing and why, and its **payload**, the concrete thing the scheme achieves if nobody stops it. One of the three acts (move, blow, crisis) must **be** the payload attempted. A scheme is never announced and then left unspent.
3. **Stake:** one kit stake, or two to choose from, with the reading the spine gives it.
4. **Heart:** the requirements, such as member or outsider, kit types allowed, and anything the plot takes for granted (has a family at the dig; can read the old hand). It also carries a **want** and a **past** (B1). The want shows on the heart's routine card and once more later. It never decides a way.
5. **Routine (3–4 cards):** profile-rolled jobs. Each carries a **quiet plant** (A3), concrete, in the card's text, written as a noun phrase the reveal will repeat. **At least one plant is an absence**: a missing name, an empty shelf, a gap in a roll.
6. **The odd job:** which kit odd job, plus **one sentence saying how the Hand's hidden role serves the scheme**. If you can't write it, the odd job doesn't belong in this spine.
7. **The reveal:** what is published, by whom, and the plant phrases it repeats.
8. **Chapter 1, the open move:** the public act, the scheme in motion. It has 2–3 cards, chained (*therefore* or *but*) and climbing, with families suggested. The turn card plants the ally.
9. **Chapter 2, the blow:** 2–3 authored options, each a target (heart, player or guild) + event + door card, each fitting this scheme. The world's deal picks among them (faction-stories §12; each target+event once per world). Also, one card offers a **lesser code-break** of the crisis's kind as an extra way (B5); the crisis never assumes the player took it.
10. **The crisis:** family + premise, giver (with their reason), the choice, a **code-break way**, and an **ending pool** of 4–5. Each ending names **who pays** (`costs`; A4); at most one costs only the other side. The pool includes a selfish or wrong-guess ending where the family allows it (B7).
11. **Closing account (C2):** for each ending, three short lines: the heart, the place, and the other side, a month on.
12. **Truths and tells (A2):** every hidden truth in the spine (the odd job's, the door's, the crisis's) has a **tell**: one public detail on that card that doesn't fit the told story. It points, never states.
13. **Hall-job hooks (A6, A7):** two hall jobs this spine's moves create (a middle-band one and a late-band one; no story cast, no secrets), plus one innocent appearance of the enemy's side on a side card before the reveal.
14. **What varies (colour):** the slots and their constraints: places (kit sites by kind), people (cast by casting), objects, and the routine jobs' families. Plus 2–3 **flavour variants** that change *who*, *why* or *how* but **never a fact another part relies on**.
15. **Presuppositions (the class-ending rule):** a list of every person, place, thing or past event the spine's text takes for granted. Each is either a slot the generator fills (with its constraint) or a fact the spine itself establishes on an earlier card. **Nothing in a spine may assume what isn't on this list.** This is the rule that ends the "heart's village that doesn't exist" breaks.
16. **Survey, scout and spy cards (C1):** where the plot naturally sends the player to look, watch or take readings, use one. For the Writers that's surveys, digs and boundary readings. These premises are being added to the family docs; see §3.

## 2. Variety across a guild's spines
- No two spines share the same crisis family + premise **and** the same blow target set.
- No two spines' payloads are alike.
- Spines span the enemies' wants and shapes, and the canon tensions of the guild.
- *Fresh stories* (mechanics) records the spine id, so the last two worlds' spines are unavailable.

## 3. Book rules applied to every doc now (any guild, any card)
These are global rules. They're written into the docs now, and tested first on the Writers' story and its hall jobs:
- **A1** spacing along the road;
- **A2** tells (rulebook rule 8);
- **A4** who pays (rulebook rule 7);
- **A5** no empty failure (mechanics §4);
- **A6/A7** hall jobs from moves; the enemy seen in passing (locations);
- **A8** one shared reason list (rulebook §7);
- **A9** the town's own features first (locations);
- **A10** the share band (faction-stories §11: report only);
- **A11** examples show shape, not wording;
- **B7** the new endings;
- **C1:** spy, scout and survey premises. These are new premises in existing families, so no new family: Investigate *Watch them* (spying with no crime yet); Threat or Passage *Go and look* (scout a dangerous place and report); Acquire or Restore *Take the readings* (field work with an instrument or survey). Each gets ways, truths with shapes and tells, and twists that already exist.
- **C5:** an ending records **how** it was won (the kind of the deciding way: bribe, force, stealth, law, persuasion); the grudge loop reads it (mechanics §14).

**Held until we spread out:** B2–B4, B6, B8–B10 and A12–A14.

## 4. The test for the Writers
1. Write the 16 spines (guild-kits, Writers, *Spines*).
2. A spine roller picks spines and colour for 8 sample worlds; the writer summarises 8 Writers stories.
3. **An independent reviewer** scores coherence, creativity and variety.
4. Fix and repeat until coherence and creativity average **4+** and variety is **4+**.
5. Then one Writers story is generated **in full cards** (routine, hall jobs, reveal, chapters, crisis: about 20 quests) inside World D's map and main story, and reviewed by fresh readers: coherence, story and a playthrough.
