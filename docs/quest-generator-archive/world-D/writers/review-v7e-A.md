# Review v7e · reviewer A (coherence and creativity)

Read: `samples-v7e.md`, `spines.md` (the eight spines dealt, the slot vocabulary, *Placing a spine*, *Rendering*), `rolls-7601-7608.txt`, `map-D.json`, `world-factions.md`, `leaders.md`. Nothing else under `test/world/`. Distances below are my own from the map (straight line, or the road where one runs).

Anchors: 5 = would hold as a hand-written guild questline; 3 = works but feels assembled; 1 = doesn't make sense.

## The strongest case against high scores (written before scoring)

1. **Every climax is procedural.** Eight stories, eight hearings, rulings, lodgings, certifications or seals. The player is always a reader standing at a court. The variety is in the scheme, not in what the player does at the end.
2. **The law is invented one sentence before it decides the crisis.** "A lord's court takes no written oath about where a thing was dug" (6), "the house holds a reader only on the page itself" (2), "a ruling reads the roll's page and nobody's memory" (8), "a copyist answers for every line in her hand" (3), "a commission sealed to a house bought in a false name is void" (7). Each is marked *(Claude)* in the spine and each is load-bearing. A hand-written RPG would plant these rules two chapters earlier so the player can see the trap close; here they arrive on the door or the crisis card as explanation.
3. **People are placed without homes.** The map is 11 days across, and several spines assume a person is "at the market" or "on the road out" when the roll has put that market days from where the person lives. The roller checks places against bounds; it does not check people.
4. **Dense one-sentence cards.** Several crisis cards run to 90+ words with four subordinate clauses; the *Held* lines are harder still. Coherent on a second read, not a first.

With that said, here is what I found story by story.

## 1 · The Wall Through the Spring (7601, W8-A)

**Coherence 4 · Creativity 4 · Emotional pull 4**

What holds: the scheme (a moved cairn, a sister's false signature, a hall checker in the captain's pay) is fully mechanical and every plant pays — the staff with no notch, the lichen scraped off, the timber hauled for the wrong line. The reveal's *laid side by side* genuinely lays three found things beside three plants. The clock (3 days: Ashring→Ninth Ring 1.63, up to 2, +1) is right.

Breaks and soft spots:
- **(write-up) The heart's turn is never shown.** The door has his son call him "the fool who'd leave the village open," which only makes sense if the carter has come out against the wall. Every dealt card before the door shows him hauling the wall's timber for pay; his doubt is inferred from the village quarrel (C1-2), never stated. At the crisis he arrives "with the village's petition and the last load of timber" — a fine irony, but the card doesn't own it as irony, so it reads as a contradiction.
- **(spine/canon) The captain hides from a fort 0.8 days away.** The Green Wall's holders "never lie to recruits" and the wife "scouts in the green" (`leaders.md`). The newest cairn (0.7 from the Green Wall) is moved and a ditch is dug half a day inside the real edge on their own stretch, with their own cairn-keeper complicit, and they know nothing. The player even visits the fort on R3. Not impossible; not defended.
- **(spine) The hall master certifies against his own guild head's letter.** *Held* says the leader has "written him what she read and left it his," then he certifies from the checked sheets anyway. The card makes him the one man who can refuse; it doesn't say why a Writer would ignore the leader's written word unless he doubts her, which nothing sets up.
- **(write-up) The chain-boy's rescue (C1-3) has no outcome anywhere later.** Minor.

Pull: the son on the ditch and the father bringing the last load alone is the best image in the set after story 6's.

## 2 · The Last Reader (7602, W5-B)

**Coherence 4 · Creativity 4 · Emotional pull 4**

What holds: the chain of plants (unclean → burned day-book → blank lodging line) is clean. The digger who can't read and stayed to hear a roll read once, and the tell — the rope sent back with a request for paper — are hand-written quality. The clock is right (3 days).

Breaks and soft spots:
- **(placement/write-up) "Through the Holy House's land."** R2 runs Elderhollow→the Ninth Ring. Both are First Peoples towns on a First Peoples road; the Holy House holds the Mount only. The card's smuggling premise ("where sealed letters from the other side are searched") has no Holy House land to cross — at most a wayside shrine. The spine wrote `{reader_lord}` free within 3.0 and the card assumed the road would cross the house's ground.
- **(spine) The sister knows the leader's movements.** The Mount's novice-mistress "adds that the leader is riding to claim the roll for the Wayhouse." The reveal rule is *shows, then adds with a source*; no source is given for a Holy House novice-mistress knowing where the Writers' head rides.
- **(spine) A warning nobody acts on.** The door brings word that the house means the heart to recant at the feast; the heart then stays at his lesson in the Ninth Ring until taken. *Held* says the house holds a reader "in its own land," but the Ninth Ring is Tuhuḍo's, not the Mount's. The Held line covers the clock, not the inaction.
- **(write-up) "Whose archive is read to him"** is the blind archivist-lord's defining trait (`leaders.md`), here hung on Roduhuli of Elderhollow because the Ninth Ring's holder was taken for `{dig_lord}`. Harmless, but it makes a Middle town's head read like the Alliance's leader.
- **(spine) R1 swap.** The pupil's family cries the hall "for teaching from a grandmother's prayer-book" — whose, and why the hall teaches from a family's book, is not set up in the swap the way the base R1 sets it up.
- **(canon nit)** E2 has the heart "banished from First Peoples land" by a Holy House proceeding; the Alliance would do the banishing.

## 3 · The Apprentice's Hand (7603, W7-B)

**Coherence 3 · Creativity 4 · Emotional pull 4**

What holds: the mechanism ("never mend an exemplar") and the revenge that exposes itself are excellent; "my hand" is the best line in the set. The three endings genuinely differ, and E3 (she goes up the hill to her teacher; her mother will not climb it) is a real ending.

Breaks:
- **(placement) The Wayhouse's old hearing-scribe is buying paper in Reefhold.** Reefhold is 6.5 days from Highcrossing by the roller's own count. The spine's C1-3 ("has come to buy paper") assumes the reading room's market is a day or so from the Wayhouse; `{scribe}` has no bound in field 17, so the placer put the centre on the far coast and the card kept the sentence. The 13-day round trip for paper is the kind of thing a player notices. Source: spine (unbounded person) + placement.
- **(spine) The reveal does the move's job early.** The master, laying the boy's sheet beside the hall's old roll, "knows who would write so … from the hall's copy of the Wayhouse's striking-off roll." A striking-off roll records names and charges, not hands. The move then says the list is "in a hand the Wayhouse's old hearing-scribe will know" — that is the right knower; the master shouldn't already have done it.
- **(spine) A Writers' reading room buying unsigned exemplars from a paper-stall agent** for years, under a master "who checks everything twice," who also never noticed a foreign watermark on paper he bought. The unsigned sheets are used as the ∅ plant, which half-acknowledges it, but the premise still asks a careful man to have been careless for a long time.
- **(write-up nit)** A watermark comes from the mould, not a press ("the tower press's watermark"); the rolled tower look has no press.
- **(spine) *Held* skips the player's own eyes.** The player saw false lines drying at the tower (C1-1). *Held* covers the watermark, the copy, the master's finding and the draft; it never says why the player's sworn witness doesn't count. The rule stated ("a copyist answers for every line in her hand") does cover it by implication; one clause would make that explicit.

## 4 · The Archive Nobody Copied (7604, W6-B)

**Coherence 4 · Creativity 5 · Emotional pull 4**

What holds: the guild's own head as the enemy by omission; a letter sealed in the strongroom "to be opened if the archive is lost," dated the week before the fire; the old reader who taught her her letters made to read it aloud. The oiled stair is planted on C1-1 and burns at the crisis. The leader's reason (the weaker heir would sell to the Sharp Hands) is given with its source (what she wrote her tutor). Oathford's map landmark is "a lord's tower," so the archive tower sits well. The STANDING line does its job.

Soft spots (none structural):
- **(write-up) "The commission's seal" in a courier's hands.** The commission was never granted ("held"), so what seal the leader's courier carries to Oathford's hall, and why it is there rather than with the leader, is abstract. One clause ("the halls' copying seal, which the courier carries with her letters") would fix it.
- **(spine) How the leader knows the player copied the letter-book** (branch b) is inferable through the courier, never said.
- **(write-up) C2-0's ward** is moved and never heard of again.
- **(spine) The leader's "held" by letter for three seasons** — a Banner Lord's steward asks three times and never once rides to the hall master in person? He does ride in at the reveal, so the question is only why not sooner. Minor.

## 5 · The Stalls' Oath (7605, W4-A)

**Coherence 3 · Creativity 4 · Emotional pull 4**

What holds: letters home as burglary intelligence is a strong, simple scheme; the painted-out oath line is a good absence; the tube written on the back of a spoiled oath sheet ties the plants physically; the heart's own letter about where the chest is hidden is what loses her the chest.

Breaks:
- **(placement/spine) The Oathford watch sergeant asks on the road out of Carter's Rest.** Field 17 puts `{ally}` "in the heart's town"; C1-3 puts his ask at `{fence_town}`. With Carter's Rest 2.2 from Southroad and about 3.6 from Oathford, the sergeant has no reason to be there, and the card gives none.
- **(placement) The fence is 3.6 days from the houses he robs.** Crews walking Oathford's streets and robbing on fair night from a shop in Carter's Rest is a long raid for a hidden-hand enemy who is otherwise local. Not impossible; not said. (The added fence's shop also lands in the Lantern Company's HQ town, which the Lanterns might mind.)
- **(write-up) How the clerk comes to be "called to account before Oathford."** The crisis opens with him answering the town; nothing dealt shows who accused him or how word reached Oathford, especially with the player framed as the one who talked. A clause ("on the leader's summons, who had come to read the stalls' books") would do.
- **(spine, TELL phrasing)** "The paint over the board's last line is newer than the rest, *for* the oath's first wording survives in the founding book" — the second half doesn't explain the first.
- **(flavour nit)** "The salt fair, at the Mount": a salt fair at the Holy House's inland shrine-town, with Saltmarket on the map.

## 6 · The Last of the Four (7606, W2-A)

**Coherence 4 · Creativity 4 · Emotional pull 5**

What holds: this is the one that reads like a hand-written questline. The heart who can't read sent his son to dig so the boy would learn letters, told him "sell to the halls, not the foreman; the halls keep our grant," and that word is now what puts the boy at risk; the boy's hidden early roll; "marked by the lord's men, he can never go home." The dead digger's letter (a digger who learned letters at a Writers' dig — consistent) as the reveal with nobody explaining is the right call. The Lantern sergeant paid to be elsewhere and hating it matches the Company's code exactly.

Breaks:
- **(placement/spine) The Free Lord of Bogford's men seize a hall in the Binders' head's town.** The door is at `{lodge_hall}` = the Chainmarket, whose holder is Paqax. *Placing a spine* 11 says a lord's writ binds only his own lordship; field 17 bounds `{lodge_hall}` by distance only. The Binders are the Free Lords' suppliers, so a sentence of leave would fix it; the card says "a lord's men at a hall's door are the lord's business," which is wrong for that door.
- **(spine) The lord judges his own claim.** The archivist files the claim for his lord, in his lord's court, and E1 has the claim fail there. Only workable if the lord is honest and his archivist is the ambitious one — which field 14 wants ("a lord who doesn't see his archivist's knives") but no card shows.
- **(write-up nit)** "A neighbour" — the heart farms the valleys, the diggers live in the hamlet; both are 0.25 from the dig, so fine, but the card could say so.

## 7 · The Copy House Across the Street (7607, W3-B)

**Coherence 4 · Creativity 4 · Emotional pull 4**

What holds: the margins ("who owes, who is weak") as the real value of the rolls; the altered bill that makes the court open bids; the half-blind master who knows the rolls by ear so the player must read to him; the court that cried him down sending clerks to read his concordance. E1 (the halls swear in a forger) is a brave ending with a real cost.

Breaks:
- **(spine) The forger's own stock in a pawn shop.** The reveal has "a woman of the Market" pawn court-roll entries with the halls' margins as "spoiled sheets" for a month's rent — the most valuable paper she has, pawned where the one man in town who knows the entries by ear could find it. The door later says she "has never been out of debt in her life," which is a character reason, but the card doesn't connect them, and it still asks the scheme's whole secret to be left in a pawnbroker's box.
- **(write-up) The heart's open-court statement** (branch a's cause) happens on no card; the door asserts it. Acceptable as the blow's event, but a reader will look for the card where he said it.
- **(placement) The house of the Dusk Road, of Wainyard** — a Low cart village's head as the creditor of the Binders' head's steward and the house "the rolls shame most." The slot allows any d-l0 house in 3.0; the fit is thin.
- **(spine)** The forger "means to go straight at last after this one last sale" — the tell gives the sale (the first year's copies pledged to the Dusk Road). Fine, but it takes the tell to make the crisis card parse.

## 8 · The Truce Held for a Debt (7608, W1-A)

**Coherence 3 · Creativity 5 · Emotional pull 4**

What holds: the best scheme in the set — a hall master who has kept two lords at feud for twenty years by leaking each one's true debt to the other at the moment either could win, and calls it balance; the leader's "I'd have done it too"; a famine-year seed loan cleared by seven years' free grinding; a key-book entry that gives away that the strongroom holds only a copy. The heart's past (she copied the ford-mill line as a girl) is leaned on exactly where it should be.

Breaks:
- **(placement, hard) `{lord_a}` = Kotqot Wall-breaker.** He is the Free Lords' faction leader, and W1-A's own field 14 bars him by name ("the paranoid Free Lord would trace a letter's road"). The roller printed "Free Lord" and passed him. `{lord_b}` is Shahayuh, a Banner Lord faction leader (allowed, with a reason). Two faction leaders in one ford feud, one of them barred.
- **(placement) The lenders' hall is in a rival faction's town.** Field 17: "in the lords' own faction's town or a neutral market's, never a rival faction's." Carter's Rest is Open Hand territory, Road Crews' town. A crossroads waystation is arguably "a neutral market," but the Open Hand is the Great House's rival, and the roller didn't say which reading it took.
- **(placement/spine) The Hand "rides the branch's post out each market eve" to Southroad's gatehouse** — 3.0 days by road each way from Carter's Rest, weekly. The spine assumed the lords' seats near the hall; the slot allows 2.8 straight.
- **(write-up) "The miller brings the hall his father's mill tally" at Bogford.** Bogford has no Writers' presence. The slot says "any settlement"; the card says "the hall."
- **(spine, clarity) The mechanism is under-explained in the cards.** The letters show Breakwall's debts sent to Southroad when Southroad grew strong. Why learning a rival's debt stops a strong lord's campaign (it turns every moment of decision into a debt squabble and an objection — exactly what the crisis does) is never said; the reader has to work it out from the crisis.
- **(spine) The objecting steward takes the sealed original away.** Consistent with the master wanting the truce to lapse, and E2 relies on it, but a hall letting the objector walk off with the lodged truce is strange enough that the card should own it.

## Set

**Variety: 4.** Settings spread well (edge, dig, coast, lords' seats, markets, a border mill); enemies and hearts are all different; endings shown all differ; three reveal givers, four forms. Against it: **Acquire *Copy it before it burns* appears in 6 of 8 stories** (routine in 2, 3, 4, 5, 8; chapter 2 in 7) — the cap of five is on routines only, and the beat ("X means to burn Y; the player copies it first") is the same each time. "The oldest herder/local walks the old line" appears verbatim in 1 and 2. Three hearts are old clerks or masters who know their rolls by heart or ear (4, 7, 8). Two stories hinge on a keeper paid to leave (5's loft keeper, 6's dig reader). And, as above, all eight end at a court, hearing, lodging or certification.

**Averages:** coherence **3.6** (4, 4, 3, 4, 3, 4, 4, 3) · creativity **4.25** (4, 4, 4, 5, 4, 4, 4, 5) · emotional pull **4.1** (4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 5, 4, 4).

**Best three:** 6 *The Last of the Four* (the heart's word to his son is the whole story), 4 *The Archive Nobody Copied* (the guild's head as the enemy by a sealed letter), 2 *The Last Reader* (the rope sent back with a request for paper).
**Weakest three:** 5 *The Stalls' Oath* (a sergeant and a fence placed days from where the story needs them), 3 *The Apprentice's Hand* (the Wayhouse's scribe shopping on the far coast; the reveal pre-empting the move), 8 *The Truce Held for a Debt* (the best scheme, the worst placement: a barred faction leader and a rival faction's hall).

## Failure patterns (count · cheapest fix)

1. **People placed without homes · 5.** W7-B's hearing-scribe in Reefhold (6.5 days from his post); W4-A's Oathford sergeant on the road out of Carter's Rest (~3.6) and a fence 3.6 from the robbed streets; W1-A's sorter riding 3.0 each way weekly; W5-B's "through the house's land" on a road that never leaves First Peoples ground. *Fix:* every made-on-demand person gets a home slot in field 17, and a card they appear on carries a bound against it exactly as places do (≤1.25, stretch 1.6 with a printed reason). Where a spine wants a far person present, the card must say why in one clause.
2. **Bars and jurisdiction not checked on secondary slots · 3.** Kotqot as `{lord_a}` against W1-A's own bar; Carter's Rest as the lenders' hall against "never a rival faction's"; Bogford's men seizing a hall in Paqax's town against rule 11. *Fix:* the roller resolves `leaders_ok` bars to registry ids and refuses them; door places get the rule-11 lordship check the move's places already get; the "neutral market" reading is printed when taken.
3. **A turn the write-up leans on but no card shows · 4.** The carter's doubt (1), the heart's open-court statement (7), how the clerk comes to be called to account (5), why the forger's stock is in a pawnbroker's box (7). *Fix:* a clause on the card where it happens or on the door that uses it; the pairwise `presumes`/`shows` check could be extended to door causes.
4. **Knowledge without a source, or in the wrong hands · 3.** The novice-mistress on the leader's ride (2); the reading-room master identifying a hand from a striking-off roll, doing the hearing-scribe's job before the move (3); the blind lord's trait on a non-leader (2). *Fix:* the *Adds* rule already demands a source; enforce it on every *Adds* clause, and move the hand-identification in W7-B to C1-3 where the scribe is.
5. **Repetition across the set · 1 structural.** *Copy it before it burns* 6/8; "the oldest X walks the old line" ×2; three old clerks who know rolls by heart. *Fix:* count chapter cards toward the copy-it cap and set it at 3 of 8; add a phrase-level repeat check for plants ("walks the old line").
6. **Load-bearing rules introduced at the crisis · 5.** The one-sentence *(Claude)* legal rules in 2, 3, 6, 7, 8 are each stated first on the door or crisis card. *Fix:* plant each rule once on a routine or chapter-1 card in the mouth of someone who'd know it (the miller already does this for the hold rule in 8 — that is the model).

## Judgment

**Creativity is at 4+ (4.25) and the spines themselves are 4+ on coherence.** Six of eight stories have schemes I'd be glad to find hand-written; two (6, 8) are better than that. **Coherence of the dealt stories is 3.6, not 4**, and the shortfall is almost entirely in the layer between the spine and the page: people placed by distance-free slots, leader bars and lordship checks not applied to secondary slots, and three or four one-clause gaps where a card assumes a turn no card shows. None of these needs a spine rewritten; they are roller rules (patterns 1, 2, 5) and single clauses (3, 4, 6). **Variety is 4** and would be a firm 4 once *Copy it before it burns* is capped across chapters, not routines only.

What remains is **structural but cheap**: bound people as places are bound, enforce the bars the spines already write, and plant the invented legal rules a chapter earlier. With those, I'd expect the next roll to sit at coherence 4+ without touching creativity.
