# Review of the Writers guild samples, v7d (spines draft 4, rolls 7501–7508)

Independent read of `samples-v7d.md` against `spines.md` (the eight spines dealt and the placing and rendering rules), `rolls-7501-7508.txt`, `map-D.json`, `world-factions.md` and `leaders.md`. No earlier review or sample was read.

Scores: **5** would hold up as a hand-written guild questline in a good story-driven RPG; **3** works but feels assembled; **1** doesn't make sense. Each break is tagged **spine**, **roll/fill**, **placement** or **write-up**. Before each story's scores is the strongest case against a high score; the scores answer it.

Map distances below are straight-line days from `map-D.json` (roads where they exist), checked against the roller's figures; the roller's figures hold.

---

## 1. The Stone That Walked (7501 · W1-B · Reefhold)

**The case against a high score.** The border is between the Ninth Ring and Elderhollow, two days inland; the survey hall that steers it, and where the story's crisis falls, is Reefhold, a Horaro stilt town on the far coast, 2.6 days from one lord and 2 from the border. A burned village walks two days to the sea to knock on the halls' door, past the Mount and the Ninth Ring, both under a day away. And lord_a is Tuhuḍo of the Ninth-Ring House: the Seated Houses' faction leader, the blind archivist-lord who "remembers every slight, debt and name, for generations", cast here as a generic border lord who has been steered for ten years by unsigned paper out of his own court rolls, which is the one thing that man would notice.

**Coherence breaks.**
1. **(placement)** The survey hall at Reefhold: 2.64 from lord_a, 2.03 from the border. The spine's bound (≤2.8 from both seats, neither's seat) is met, but the fiction strains at the crisis, where the heart's mother and her neighbours are "driven with the village to the survey hall's door" two days away. The crisis card gives no reason they went there and not to the Mount (0.95) or the Ninth Ring's hall (0.8).
2. **(placement / canon)** lord_a is a faction leader with a defining trait (total memory of records) that the plot's premise ignores. The lord slot excludes faction leaders only in W6-B; it should here too, or the spine should use him.
3. **(spine)** The scheme sends each contrary reading to "the *other* lord", but the retired surveyor of lord_a keeps readings "that undid his stones" on loft paper with loft numbers. Those readings went to Elderhollow; how copies reached the Ninth Ring's surveyor is never said.
4. **(spine)** The payload reverses the scheme's direction: "one reading too many" goes to lord_a's own steward and moves lord_a's stone *further*, onto the village. It serves "the border stays open", but the write-up never bridges the flip; a reader who followed the reveal expects the fatal reading to go to Elderhollow.
5. **(write-up)** C1-1: the masons "set out a line no survey hall sent them", yet the crisis says the reading "was the halls'". The spine means "officially"; the sentence says the opposite.
6. **(write-up)** Two unnamed "copyists": the Hand is "the survey hall's copyist" and the chapter-1 move dismisses "the copyist who asked". The reader will take them for one man until C1-3.
7. **(roll / fill, minor)** The heart hand-carries a *relay loft*'s night sheets 2.6 days to the Ninth Ring. A pigeon loft's sheets travel by pigeon; a line saying why these went by hand would do.

What works: the loft numbers as the heart's fingerprints on every reading; the tithe book as the one record outside the halls; the shared-blame tell (the fatal sheet in her own hand); the code-bending way that changes who is standing at the door.

**Coherence 3 · Creativity 4 · Emotional pull 4.**

## 2. Every Family Its Own File (7502 · W3-A · Openharbour)

**The case against a high score.** The whole story sits in one town and a barn; the enemy is Middle, the stake local, and the ending shown says the carter's "wife is won back" when nothing in this roll took her from him.

**Coherence breaks.**
1. **(spine)** The closing account for *Wrongdoer ruined* says "his wife is won back". The only card that estranges the wife is door option a, which was not dealt (blow c was). The ending's month-on line assumes a blow that didn't fall.
2. **(spine)** Scheme: the copyist makes her *fair copies* of the files. C2-0: a widow holds her file "not knowing it is the halls' *original*". One of these is wrong, and if originals are leaving the copy room nightly the hall would miss them.
3. **(spine / write-up)** C2-0 says the file was "bought at the booth", but the booth is the crisis, a card later. It can be read as her everyday night-market stall, but the door's plant makes "the booth" a specific thing; the word collides.
4. **(spine)** *Wrongdoer ruined*: "the Market is told how she paid her bond, and takes her back". Paying one's bond is not an offence; the story never says what rule of the Market she broke (running a racket off its book, say). One clause fixes it.
5. **(write-up)** C1-2: carters who bought their "forgeries" "cry the reading room thieves". If they believe the forgery story the reading room isn't the thief; if they've seen their own words (the spine's tell: the loudest crier quotes his own letter), say so, since that is the turn.
6. **(spine, minor)** The night porter watched a copyist carry bundles to a woman paying in Market notes, did nothing, and later also knows *why* she wanted the money ("what the woman told him she was buying"). A forger confiding her motive to a porter is convenient; the C1-3 tell makes him half-complicit, which the write-up could use.

What works: the premise (selling families their own secrets as "forgeries", and the one true warning being the guild's own shame); a carter who can't write, a wife who can't read, and a letter he once asked kept back; the heart paying his cart savings for his own words; the tell of the overloaded cart. Geography is tight and every card sits where its people are.

**Coherence 4 · Creativity 5 · Emotional pull 4.**

## 3. The Last of the Four (7503 · W2-A · Breakwall)

**The case against a high score.** Blow b throws the player into Kotqot's cells at Breakwall and the door is the player's flight from his men. The crisis then asks the player to escort a frightened boy *into Kotqot's court* three days later, with the archivist's men in pursuit, and the shown ending "Delivered" is "won by law" there. The player is a fugitive from the very court the plot turns on, and the spine has no Standing line for it. Underneath that, the legal logic is tangled: the archivist calls the rolls "stolen from the lord's land", the boy fears hanging "as a thief of his lord's ground", and the answer is a digger swearing where he dug them, which confirms they came out of the lord's ground.

**Coherence breaks.**
1. **(spine)** Blow b (arrested in the lord's cells, door: flight) and a crisis whose destination is that lord's court. W2-A has no **Standing** line for blow b; the crisis should say on what footing a fugitive stands at Breakwall (the Wayhouse clerk's party, a Lantern escort's, the heart's household), or blow b should be barred when the crisis is at the court.
2. **(spine)** The claim's logic. "Stolen from the lord's land" is defeated by an oath about *which trench* the rolls came from only if the court already accepts that finds from the Writers' dig were the Writers' to sell. That premise is never stated; the move ("the ruin being his lord's by the old rolls") implies the opposite. The sergeant's line about living witnesses is good but rests on nothing the player has been told.
3. **(spine)** R3's missing reader. The dig's reader is taken by the foreman the night she comes to lodge her catalogue, and is never seen, found, mourned or mentioned again on any later card or ending. A kidnapped woman is a plant, not a person.
4. **(write-up)** The door: of the four, "one is bonded or gone". That is the spine's hedge across door options; in this roll (blow b) it should resolve to one or the other.
5. **(placement, minor)** The dig's finds are catalogued and its dead's goods pawned at Longwall, the fortress of Haʻahoqo, a Banner Lord and rival sub-faction, 1.4 days off and across the Great House border; the roller marks it "stretched". The spine's own nearest-hall rule forces it; it reads oddly for a Free Lord's march dig but is not a contradiction.
6. **(placement / canon, minor)** The lord is Kotqot Wall-breaker, the Free Lords' leader, "secretly principled" and "paranoid, often right". His archivist runs a land grab with a hired blade under his nose and the lord never appears as himself.

What works: the letter in the tool-roll handle that names the plants in its own words (the best reveal form in the set); the filled, turfed trench; a father who can't read sending his son to dig for the halls so he would learn, and that word putting the boy in danger; the boy who won't let anyone carry his pack. The farm-or-son choice is real.

**Coherence 3 · Creativity 4 · Emotional pull 4.**

## 4. The Court's New Clerk (7504 · W7-A · Southroad)

**The case against a high score.** The heart is the enemy's oldest friend and wants her name restored. The enemy is the court's clerk in the heart's own town, left-handed as she always was, and the heart copies the lord's rolls that the clerk is falsifying, at a table a street away. Nothing on any card says whether the heart knows her friend is there, why the friend has hidden from her, or why the friend's revenge on the master lands on the one person in the hall who loves her. The crisis's "in her own name at last" implies the heart didn't know. The whole emotional engine of the story is a knot the cards never touch.

**Coherence breaks.**
1. **(spine)** The heart–enemy relation in one town is unaddressed (above). One sentence at R1 ("she has not seen her friend in twenty years and does not know the court's clerk") and one at the crisis (the clerk's face when she names the heart's copy) would carry it; without them the accusation reads as the generator not noticing who it is accusing.
2. **(roll / fill vs. placement)** Flavour c makes the master's son a ferryman whose trade "takes him past the court every day"; `son_town` is the Silver Road Counting-House, a fortified Sharp Hands counting-house 1.2 days from Southroad. A ferryman 1.2 days away does not pass the court daily. The flavour binds the son to the court's town; the slot let him live elsewhere.
3. **(write-up)** The reveal: "the line she was struck off for was a debt, so the line was his father's". The inference the spine means (father took the book home that night → the false line appeared → his) is misordered into a non sequitur.
4. **(spine)** The son knows the court's clerk is the struck-off copyist "from his trade". Passing a court shows nobody a clerk's handedness. He would have known her as a boy at the Wayhouse's tables; say so.
5. **(spine, minor)** The lord is Shahayuh, the Ṭaḍoro-born Banner Lord and a faction co-leader; the enemy hides under "a Ṭaḍoro road-name" at his court. Not a break (it's a nice resonance), but the roller has now put faction leaders in three of eight lord slots without any spine asking for one.

What works: the day-book entry "late, unsealed" as the mechanism that gets her alone with the copies; the smuggled fresh copy that then becomes the stain on the player ("carries copies into a court by night"), the cleanest cause-and-effect chain in the set; the stripped player standing as the tenant's advocate; the master's face going still; the shown ending, where the guild restores a woman who falsified three lines and lets a tenant's field stay moved, which is a genuinely uncomfortable outcome.

**Coherence 3 · Creativity 5 · Emotional pull 4.**

## 5. Who Is Poor (7505 · W4-B · the Mount)

**The case against a high score.** The story's title and central plant are "no debt bought from anyone who can write": the clerk preys on the illiterate who beg through the stalls. The heart is a copyist's son who copies by night, wrote his first begging letter himself at fourteen, and yet begs "through the stall" and is on the list. The story's rule excludes its own heart. Draft 4 made this heart (it replaced a lost surveyor's child to free the once-a-set motif) and the change broke the premise.

**Coherence breaks.**
1. **(spine)** The literate heart on the illiterates' list, using a letter-writer though he is a copyist. The reveal's own closing sentence ("no debt bought from anyone who can write, and none from anyone who hadn't begged") says his debt should never have been bought. The door's plant ("he kept that one for himself") hints the clerk made an exception for a copyist's family, but nothing says why, and the write-up still has the heart paying a stall to write letters he could write.
2. **(spine)** The heart's want is to pay "the last of it by midsummer"; the crisis's tell is that "his own tally of payments adds up to the debt's face, for his copying has paid it". A man who keeps a tally knows when it reaches the face. Either the want is "to be told it is paid" or the tally is the old creditor's, not his.
3. **(write-up / fill)** Two binderies at the Mount: the father's debt was "for the presses of a bindery that failed" (flavour c) and the heart copies at "the bookbinder's widow's bindery" (R1). Both are "the bindery" in the write-up and read as one.
4. **(placement)** R3 at Reefhold, about 2.8 days from the heart's town on the far coast, for a routine watch-the-clerk job, while R2 is at Oathford. The free-card bound is from the centre (the Ninth Ring); the story's people are at the Mount.
5. **(spine)** The Oathford forgiven-debts thread (R2, the odd job) is laid beside the live debts at the reveal and then never cashed: no bought debt in the chapters is one the old lord forgave.
6. **(write-up)** "the hall's debt sales" (R3): what a reading room sells is unclear; the spine means the hall's *record* of debt sales.
7. **(canon, minor)** A girl held as surety at a Writer's rooms in the Ninth Ring. First Peoples custom allows bondage of Qotiro only (Ṭaḍoro rarely); the card calls the debts "lawfully bought" without saying whose law lets him hold her.

What works: the patience list; begging letters as the thing worth more than the debts in them; the uncle who tells the town what the boy begged, adding only the shame; the sister at the clerk's copy table; the old creditor shamed as a usurer before she can swear.

**Coherence 2 · Creativity 4 · Emotional pull 4.**

## 6. The Wall Through the Spring (7506 · W8-A · Ashring)

**The case against a high score.** The leader herself gives the reveal: at the camp she reads the sister's private field book and knows the readings are false and that her own hall's checker is marking unwalked readings as walked. Three cards and a blow later, the crisis says the one man who can refuse is the Ninth Ring's hall master, her subordinate, who "certifies from the halls' own checked sheets". The head of the guild has known for a chapter and done nothing, and the card never says why.

**Coherence breaks.**
1. **(spine)** The leader as knower undercuts the crisis (above). The *Held* line speaks of the levy and the certification but not of her. If her balance wants the Wardens funded, or she will not overrule a hall master from the road, a clause must say it.
2. **(spine)** The sister's daughter lends the leader her mother's incriminating book "for one reading" with no motive given. She is the enemy's niece and the forger's daughter; the card should say what moved her (the chain-boy, her uncle's cry, her own name on the sheets).
3. **(spine / placement)** Blow b arrests the player "at the Green Wall as green-touched", with "the captain's men" in pursuit. The Green Wall is Haʻo's fort, and the spine's premise is that the captain acts behind the Green Wall's back and its holder "knows nothing of it". A player in Haʻo's cells for a night tells Haʻo; the blow sits at the one place the enemy is supposed to be hiding from.
4. **(placement / scale)** "A mile inside the real edge." On this map a day is twenty-odd miles; Ashring is 0.6–1.0 days south of the cairns and the edge. For the false line's ditch to cut Ashring's spring meadow, the meadow must lie within a mile of the Overgrowth, a day's walk from the village. Possible (herders' summer pasture up by the edge), but the cards never place the meadow, and the title rests on it.
5. **(spine, minor)** The wall's timber is being hauled (R1), its yard hiring (C2-0) and its ditch pegged (C1-2) before the levy that pays for it is voted (crisis). Readable as the captain building on spec; one word ("against the levy") would say so.

What works: the newest cairn scraped clean of lichen; the widow's notched staff with no notch for where the stone stands now; the old certification as the guild's own debt; the headman who sold the strip and won't stand on the meadow; *Bargain struck* paying for the right wall with the Wayhouse's old shame. The heart is the thinnest of the eight: a debt, a pasture, a father's cart, and a son whose turn (door a) wasn't dealt.

**Coherence 3 · Creativity 4 · Emotional pull 3.**

## 7. The Archive Nobody Copied (7507 · W6-B · Bogford)

**The case against a high score.** The dying lord's seat is Bogford: on the map a bog village, size "village", standing Low, quota one or two, no presences, hook "a slave-bought field". The story gives it an archive tower with a lamp-lit stair and oil in every niche, a seat's hall, a steward, a ward, and two heirs each with armed men and a locksmith. The registry may call its holder High and old; the map calls the place a bog hamlet. And the tutor who gives the reveal holds the leader's own letter saying why she held the commission, so the crisis's "who is blamed" is settled before it is asked.

**Coherence breaks.**
1. **(placement)** Bogford as a seat with an archive and a hall. The slot checks the registry's holder (old, High, has an archive) and not the map's place (village, Low). W5-B was passed over twice in this set for "no Seated House with an archive within three days", so the roller does test archives, but it tests them on the holder, not the ground.
2. **(spine)** Blow c and the crisis's Standing both rest on "the player carried the steward's letters". No dealt card has the player carry a letter. R2 has the player *copy* the steward's letter-book before it burns; the reveal has the steward *show* his copies. The write-up should say "copied", and the stain should be worded to match.
3. **(spine)** The tutor holds the leader's own written reason (the reveal's *Adds*), and the heart holds the copying-book page in her own hand with the "held" letters pinned to it (the door). The crisis's choice is real as politics (exposing the leader splits the hall masters) but the write-up frames it as "who is blamed", which these two documents already answer. Say the cost, not the doubt.
4. **(spine)** "Without the commission's seal no Writer may copy a lord's archive": a guild rule. The lord's own steward wants it copied and could set any pen to it. Why it must be the halls (the halls' copy is what a court honours) is a sentence the spine never spends.
5. **(write-up)** Three threes: the steward's three letters, the heart's three letters to the leader, the leader's three "held" answers. In one card the reader cannot tell whose three is which.

What works: the leader as the enemy and the seat ending offered as a fourth way; the scapegoat letter sealed in the strongroom "dated the week before the fire"; the courier's saddlebag of wrappers; the ward who won't be moved; and the shown ending, where the heart "copies the archive's charred remains, which is her want, smaller", the best single closing line in the set.

**Coherence 3 · Creativity 4 · Emotional pull 4.**

## 8. The Letters Never Collected (7508 · W5-A · Highcrossing)

**The case against a high score.** At R2 the player copies the dead copyist's letters before they burn, and the spine says those letters "name the pigeonholes where the drafters' letters wait". The player then rides 3.4 days to a branch to be handed "the old index of the drafters' pigeonholes" on the door, and is offered, as the crisis's ally way, the widow's copies "which say which holes hold what". The player has held that information since the second card. Then the door falls at the Ninth Ring, 3.4 days from Highcrossing, and the crisis is a burning at dawn in the Wayhouse's yard with no card between to carry the player there.

**Coherence breaks.**
1. **(spine)** The R2 copy makes the door's recited index and the ally's copies redundant. Either R2's letters name something else (the drafters' *decade*, not the holes), or the door and ally ways go.
2. **(spine)** How a recently dead copyist, a surveyor's companion on an edge survey, came to know the century-old pigeonholes; and why a branch 3.4 days from the Wayhouse keeps "the old index" of the Wayhouse's oldest wall. Both are load-bearing and neither is said.
3. **(spine)** R2's "so the inquisitor now knows exactly where to look" against a chapter in which he searches a branch for an index and arrests its clerks to get it.
4. **(placement)** The door (the branch's clerks' rescue) at the Ninth Ring, 3.40 from the crisis at Highcrossing, with dawn as the pressure. The Wayhouse exemption (Placing 4) waives the bound but not the clock; nothing says how the player is in the yard. The inquisitors' camp, "on the road *beyond* the branch", is placed at (2.42, 4.27), between the branch and Highcrossing on the Mount road.
5. **(spine)** The drafters' letters themselves: written to whom, left for which "readers", and why uncollected for two centuries, is never said. The tell (they name the inquisitor's own family as the grants' payers) tells what they contain, not what they were.

What works: everything the heart touches. A son writing monthly for nine years to a father in the green, letters filed unopened; the hole inherited from his grandfather's grandfather among letters two hundred years uncollected; the writ that names one decade only; the cook's "they sleep at an inn by the gate of the town they burn in, and burn at dawn"; the dead copyist who walked with the father on his last survey; and the shown ending, "the heart stops writing". The Wall's promise (a letter waits for its reader) as the thing the copy way breaks is the best *Held* line in the set.

**Coherence 3 · Creativity 5 · Emotional pull 5.**

## The set

### The case against a high score for the set

Read in a row, the eight are one machine wearing eight coats. Every routine has a quoted plant; every reveal repeats three of them in a closing sentence of one of four shapes; every chapter 1 meets an ally with a price; every crisis has a code-break way, an ally way and a third; every ending names who pays. Four of eight odd jobs are "the Hand told someone to burn a record and copied it first". Six of eight chapter-1 moves are either "the master dismisses the copyist who asked" (1, 5, 7) or "has it cried in the town that the Writers' X is false" (2, 4, 6). The Ninth Ring appears in four stories and the leader in three (reveal, judge, enemy). Hearts are strong but the enemies' *people* are thin: five of eight enemies never speak a line until the crisis, and three lord slots are filled by faction leaders whose canon traits the plots ignore. Scene to scene the craft is high; story to story the rhythm shows.

**Variety: 4.** Eight enemies of eight kinds, eight crisis families, eight main towns, hearts from six types; the dilemmas differ (a village or a balance; a boy or a farm; a friend or a guild's name; letters or a promise). What repeats is scaffolding (the odd-job shape, two move templates, the three-plant reveal), not plot.

### Failure patterns, counts, cheapest fix

1. **Cross-field contradictions inside one spine (5 of 8: stories 2, 3, 5, 7, 8).** A want against a tell (5), a blow against the crisis's destination (3), an odd job against a door (8), a closing account against the blow dealt (2), a Standing against the cards (7). The roller checks that parties are *present* (rule 10) and that bounds and caps hold; it never checks that two fields *agree*. Cheapest fix: three one-line checks in `roll_samples.py`: (a) every blow option has a Standing line or is barred when the crisis's place is the blow's captor's; (b) every ending's month-on line names only states a dealt card produced; (c) an odd job that hands the player a copy strikes any later card whose plant is the same information. Then five sentences of spine text.
2. **The knower already holds the answer (3 of 8: stories 6, 7, 8).** The leader reads the proof at the reveal and the crisis pretends only a hall master can act (6); the tutor holds the leader's confession (7); the player copied the pigeonhole list at R2 (8). Cheapest fix: the *Held* line must name every proof the player or a friendly giver holds, not only the player's; where the giver is the leader or holds the enemy's own writing, say why they don't act.
3. **Placement that passes the bounds and fights the fiction (5 of 8: stories 1, 3, 4, 7, 8).** A seat checked on its registry holder, not its map size (Bogford); faction leaders in generic lord slots (Tuhuḍo, Kotqot, Shahayuh); a flavour that binds a person to a place ("passes the court every day") rolled with a slot that put him 1.2 days off; a crisis 3.4 days from its door with dawn as the pressure; a two-day walk to the crisis for a burned village. Cheapest fix: (a) seat slots check the map place's size and standing too; (b) lord slots default to *not a faction leader* unless the spine names one (W6-B's rule, made general); (c) a flavour that names a daily habit at a place pins its person's slot to that place; (d) the Wayhouse exemption keeps a clock: the last chapter-2 card must be within `{days}` of Highcrossing, or the crisis says how word reached the player.
4. **People who act without a motive, or vanish (3 of 8: stories 2, 3, 6).** The porter who watches a theft and later knows the thief's reason; the kidnapped reader never seen again; the forger's daughter who hands over her mother's book. Cheapest fix: one clause in field 6 or 7 each; and a roller rule that a person a routine card loses (a disappearance) appears on a later card or an ending.
5. **Write-up slips (5 of 8: stories 1, 2, 3, 4, 5).** A hedge left unresolved ("bonded or gone"); two unnamed roles in one word ("the copyist"); an inference misordered ("a debt, so his father's"); two binderies as one; a crowd crying "thieves" at the people they think forged. All renderer fixes: resolve hedges to the dealt branch, give the Hand a distinguishing word on first mention, and keep "so" out of sentences whose cause is on another card.

### Best and weakest

**Best three.** **8, The Letters Never Collected**: the heart and his father's hole carry the story over its logic gaps; the most feeling in the set. **4, The Court's New Clerk**: the tightest cause-and-effect (the smuggled copy becomes the stain), the most adult ending, and the richest knot, even unplayed. **2, Every Family Its Own File**: the best premise and the only story whose every card sits where its people live.

**Weakest three.** **5, Who Is Poor**: the heart contradicts the story's rule; a draft-4 fix broke the premise. **6, The Wall Through the Spring**: the leader knows and doesn't act, the arrest happens at the one place the enemy hides from, and the heart is thin. **3, The Last of the Four**: a fugitive escorted into the court he fled, on legal ground the cards never lay. (1 is close behind for its sprawl: a border two days inland judged in a stilt town on the coast.)

### Judgment

**Creativity is at 4+ (4.4) and variety is at 4, both met. Coherence is at 3.0, not 4.** No story is at 4 on coherence but one, and none at 5; every story has two to seven breaks a player would notice, and the breaks are not slips.

What remains is **structural**, and it recurs for three reasons:

- **The checks test presence, not agreement.** Draft 3 and 4 made the roller verify that every party is set up, every bound holds, every cap is kept. The breaks that survive are between fields the checker reads one at a time: a want and a tell, a blow and a crisis, an odd job and a door. Pattern 1 (five stories) is this. It will recur with every new spine until the checker reads pairs.
- **The reveal's giver can know too much.** Drafts 2–4 varied the reveal's giver to break the leader's monopoly; the leader, an elder who taught her, and a found document all turn out to *hold the proof*, and the *Held* line was written for the player's hand only. Pattern 2 (three stories) will recur as long as *Held* ignores the giver.
- **Placement passes on distance and registry, not on the ground.** The roller knows a holder's age and kind and a place's coordinates; it does not read the map's size, standing or kind when filling a seat, nor the canon traits of a faction leader, nor whether a flavour pins a person to a place. Pattern 3 (five stories) is this.

The write-up slips (pattern 5) and the motive gaps (pattern 4) are **one-offs**, fixable in the renderer and in a clause each.

Story 5 is the warning: the draft-4 heart change was made to free a variety cap and nobody re-read the spine's plants against the new heart. Any further spine edit should be followed by a read of that spine's quoted plants and tells against its heart, want, blows and endings, which is exactly the pairwise check the roller lacks.

The guild is close. With the three roller checks in pattern 1, the *Held* widening in pattern 2 and the four placement rules in pattern 3, I would expect these same eight seeds to roll at 4 on coherence. The emotional and inventive work is already there.
