# W5-A · The Letters Never Collected

Every branch × ending combination of `spines.md` draft 5, rendered by `roll_samples.py --render-all`. Slots are shown as `{slot}`, flavour as its own slot; one sentence per line. The routine's swaps are not multiplied out (each is one card's other premise, printed in the spine's field 18).

**6 combinations:** 2 branch(es) × 3 endings.

### Branch a × E1 · A copy in every hall

*Blow a: guild · members taken · arrested on a charge (`taken-arrested`) → door Rescue Imprisoned unjustly. Ending shown: A copy in every hall.*

- **R1 · `{wayhouse}`.** At the Wayhouse the heart disputes the pigeonhole fee with the Wall's clerk, who says the heart's father's hole is overfull and nine years unpaid (Dispute *Broken agreement*), and the hole, inherited from his grandfather's grandfather, is in the Wall's oldest section, among {wall_look}, while the heart asks everyone, the player too, whether they knew his father, because he wants to find someone who walked with him on his last survey (his want), and says he has written to him every month for nine years, of late about his own child, born this year, letters the clerk files unopened (his past), and around the hole are "the Wall's oldest pigeonholes, sealed letters two hundred years uncollected".
- **R2 · `{widow_hall}`.** At `{widow_hall}` a dead copyist's widow means to burn his letters, and the player copies them before they burn (Acquire *Copy it before it burns*), "a dead copyist's letters, burned 'for the house's mercy'", and they tell how as a young Wall clerk at the Wayhouse he re-sorted its oldest wall and found the decade the drafters' letters lie in, while in secret a pious copyist in the inquisitor's pay (the Hand) told the widow they were heresy and the house would be merciful if they burned, and copied them first, so the inquisitor now knows which decade to burn.
- **R3 · `{tithe_hall}`.** At `{tithe_hall}` a tithe-house book copied at the hall has been altered by the tithe-keeper for himself (Investigate *The ledger doesn't add up*), and laid beside the old grant charter the hall also keeps it shows ∅ "the grant charter with no copyist's name at its foot".
- **Reveal · `{tithe_hall}`.** Also at `{tithe_hall}`, an old woman's copying desk, her grandfather's, holds in its drawer the drafters' own copy of their covering letter to the Mount, unread for a century (Acquire *Unknown worth*), and the player reads that the Mount's grants were drawn by Ṭaḍoro copyists of a Writers' drafting house, for a fee, and signed by none of them, and that the drafters have left letters of their own at the Wall for their heirs, "to be collected when the grants are safe to own", while the old woman adds that a pious copyist came last month offering to buy the desk for the house, "to be kept shut, for the house's mercy", so the inquisitor's burnings, which the parish talks of, are for silence.
  The letter names two of the plants in its own words, the grants "signed by no copyist of ours", as on "the grant charter with no copyist's name at its foot", and the drafters' letters waiting "in the Wall's oldest pigeonholes" for their heirs, and the old woman's buyer said the third: "for the house's mercy".
- **Chapter 1's move.** By the house's word, not a writ, the inquisitor has the Wall branch at `{branch_hall}`, in First Peoples land where the house's word holds and where the far copy of the Wayhouse's oldest wall's index is kept against fire, searched for heresy and its pigeonholes sealed for a week.
- **C1-1 · `{branch_hall}`.** The player rides to the sealed branch at `{branch_hall}` to carry the carters' ordinary letters out past the inquisitor's guard (Passage *Smuggling*), while the branch's old Wall clerk, who keeps its index, hands out what letters he dares and hears "the inquisitor's clerk asking which letters came from the Wayhouse's old wall", and the dead copyist's widow, come for her husband's last letters, says he walked with the heart's father on his last survey, and will help for her husband's name read at the Wayhouse.
- **C1-2 · `{inq_camp}`.** Therefore the player goes out to the inquisitors' camp on the Mount road from the branch's town to see what it has burned (Threat *Go and look*), finds "a burned page with a Ṭaḍoro copyist's mark", and hears from the camp's cook that before any burning "they sleep at an inn by the gate of the town they burn in, and burn at dawn".
- **C1-3 · `{mount}`.** But at the letter-writer's stall at the Mount the pious copyist (the Hand) holds the dead copyist's last letter to the house, begging its mercy, which the widow wants back, and won't sell it to the halls (Acquire *Won't sell*), and he has told her the halls will burn her husband's name with the rest, so the ally wavers, and will give the house's receipt for the drafters' fee, which her husband found loose in the old wall and kept, only once his name has been read.
- **C2-0 · `{branch_hall}`.** At pressure two, in the branch's town, the branch's letter-carriers are out of work while it is sealed (Restore *Lost livelihood*).
- **Blow and door (a) · `{branch_hall}`.** On the inquisitor's charge the house arrests the branch's Wall clerks, because they keep the far copy of the oldest wall's index he wants, and he wants them quiet, so the door is their rescue (Rescue *Imprisoned unjustly*), and on it the branch's old Wall clerk recites from the index which pigeonholes hold the drafters' decade: "the old index of the drafters' pigeonholes".
- **Code-bending way.** The door carries one extra way that destroys a text: burn the branch's old index, so the inquisitor can't turn the decade into pigeonholes.
- **Crisis · at `{wayhouse}`.** The inquisitor, {inquisitor_look}, has a writ from Highcrossing's magistrate on his heresy charge, the story's one writ, granted because the magistrate sits by the Throne's leave and the Throne keeps the Mount's goodwill, and the pigeonholes of the drafters' decade are to be opened and burned in the Wayhouse's yard at dawn, `{days}` days after the door, the heart's father's hole among them with nine years of letters, so the heart, in the yard as he comes every month, gives the card at the Wall where his letters wait (Acquire *Copy it before it burns*, the inquisitor holding the writ, dawn the pressure): copy them, keep them, or let them burn, and so decide whether the Writers' oldest compromise is read.
  - *How it reads blow a:* If the branch's Wall clerks were got out, the freed clerks ride to the Wayhouse with the player and stand at the Wall to say which holes their index names, and if not, the old clerk's recital is all the player brings.
  - *What the player holds:* The player has known since the reveal that the drafters' letters are the inquisitor's target, knows their decade from the dead copyist's letters and their holes from the branch's index, and the widow holds her husband's copy of the house's receipt, but no Writer may open or move a sealed letter before its reader comes, which is the Wall's whole worth, the receipt stays in the widow's hands until her husband's name is read, and so the halls could only guard the Wall until the writ came, and the copy way breaks that promise, which is its cost.
  - *The tell:* The writ names one decade's pigeonholes only, which no heresy charge would care about, for the letters name the inquisitor's own family as the grants' payers.
  - *The ways:* The ways are burning them tonight so no one reads them (the code-break), the widow, come to the Wayhouse for her husband's name, giving up the house's receipt her husband kept, naming the inquisitor's family as the grants' payers, once his name is read at the Wayhouse, copying in secret while the inquisitors sleep at `{lodging}`, or trading the inquisitor the Wayhouse's silence on the grants for ever.
- **Endings.**
  - *A copy in every hall* (every drafter's letter copied before dawn and lodged at each hall): won by stealth; who pays: the guild's name and the Mount's (the old debt is out) · the heart (marked by the inquisitor as the one who held the lamp). ← shown
  - *Trade what we know* (the letters stay sealed and unburned, for the Wayhouse's silence): won by bribe; who pays: the guild's freedom (an inquisitor holds its promise) · the Mount's tenants (the grants stand unexamined).
  - *Left with the holder* (they burn at dawn): won by nobody; who pays: the heart (his letters too) · the record.
- **A month on (A copy in every hall).** Goes on writing to his father, and the hole is empty but his / the grants are argued in every hall, the branch's clerks' word beside them / the inquisitor is recalled to the Mount, and blamed.
