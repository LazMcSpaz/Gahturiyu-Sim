### Branch a × E1 · Amends made

*Blow a: heart · estranged · her kin turned (`kin-turned`) → door Restore Estrangement. Ending shown: Amends made.*

- **R1 · `{reading_room}`.** In the market of `{reading_room}` the heart's old sign-painter master has lost his eyes and his trade with them (Restore *Lost livelihood*), and the heart, a letter-writer's apprentice who copies for the reading room by the sheet, tells the player she wants to be sworn a copyist at the Wayhouse (her want), that no hall would take her and a struck-off copyist found her copying shop signs and taught her letters and the halls' hand for nothing (her past), and that her mother, a market woman of `{mother_town}`, has never asked who taught her, while the reading room's master says of her copies that hers is "a hand the halls can't tell from their own".
- **R2 · `{reading_room}`.** At `{reading_room}`, which buys its exemplars from the tower, a checker means to burn a batch's exemplar sheets as spoiled, and the player copies them before they burn (Acquire *Copy it before it burns*), "exemplar sheets burned as spoiled", while in secret the tower's agent (the Hand), who sells the exemplars to the reading room, told the checker they were spoiled, because the exemplars are the only proof that the false lines came from the sheet and not the hand that copied it.
- **R3 · `{r3_hall}`.** At `{r3_hall}` a contract copy has been altered by its clerk for himself (Investigate *The ledger doesn't add up*), and the bought exemplars beside it show an absence: ∅ "no name on the exemplars: every sheet unsigned".
- **Reveal · `{reading_room}`.** At `{reading_room}` the shepherd boy who carries the hall's exemplar sheets down from the tower won't go back up the hill, and the reading room's master, a man who checks everything twice, wants him brought round (Passage *Unwilling passenger*), and the boy says a woman in the tower writes the sheets, dries them on a line, and pays him to bring them down to the agent at the paper stall, while the master adds, laying one of the boy's sheets beside the hall's old roll, that it has a line set in that the roll never had, and, from the hall's copy of the Wayhouse's striking-off roll, who would write so, the copyist struck off twenty years ago, and which master brought the charge.
  "No name on the exemplars" leads up the hill to the tower, the tower's sheets explain "exemplar sheets burned as spoiled", since they were the proof, and those explain "a hand the halls can't tell from their own", the apprentice's, which copied every line exactly as the sheet set it.
- **Chapter 1's move.** The struck-off copyist nails to the door of `{reading_room}` a list of twelve Writers' copies, each false line marked, unsigned, in a hand the Wayhouse's old hearing-scribe will know.
- **C1-1 · `{tower}`.** The player goes up to the tower, {tower_look}, to see what she does there (Threat *Go and look*), and finds "exemplars drying on the line, the false lines already in".
- **C1-2 · `{reading_room}`.** Therefore in the market of `{reading_room}` a merchant whose copy is on the list, its false line changing a sum, cries the reading room cheats (Dispute *Public offense*), and the twelve copies turn out to be "twelve copies in one hand", the apprentice's.
- **C1-3 · `{reading_room}`.** But at the market's paper stall the tower's agent (the Hand) holds the next batch of exemplars as his own stock and won't sell it to the halls (Acquire *Won't sell*), while the Wayhouse's old hearing-scribe, who wrote down the striking-off twenty years ago and kept his draft against the rule, has come to buy paper, knows the hand on the batch, and will help for his post kept, though the draft breaks the rule.
- **C2-0 · `{reading_room}`.** At pressure two, at `{reading_room}`, the twelve copies' owners hold the reading room to its oath that a copy stands, and its master, a man who checked everything twice, must answer them (Dispute *Broken agreement*).
- **Blow and door (a) · `{mother_town}`.** At her mother's stall in `{mother_town}` the heart's mother, told her girl copies for a struck-off forger, sides with the halls and will not have her home, and the door is mending it (Restore *Estrangement*, cause *a side taken*), and on it the old hearing-scribe, who has bought the halls' paper for forty years, holds a sheet to the light, for the exemplars' paper carries the tower press's watermark and the halls' paper doesn't: "the tower press's watermark".
- **Code-bending way.** The door carries one extra way that destroys a text: burn one of the twelve copies, so its owner can't take it to court.
- **Crisis · at `{wayhouse}`.** The Wayhouse's hearing, `{days}` days after the summons, has barred the apprentice's hand from every hall for the twelve false lines, and that night the struck-off copyist's letter is read at the Wall, saying the lines were hers, as the master's line was his, and calling the girl "my hand", so the eldest copyist, {eldest_manner}, who read it, gives the card (Reckoning *Ruin from above*, the old hearing-scribe acting for the apprentice): ruin the teacher, or answer her, and say whether the Wayhouse owns two wrongs.
  - *How it reads blow a:* With her mother sided with the halls, the apprentice rides up to the hearing with nobody of her own but the hearing-scribe, and stands alone before it as her teacher once did.
  - *What the player holds:* Before the hearing the player holds the watermark finding and the copy of the burned exemplars, the reading room's master found a line in an exemplar that the hall's roll never had, and the hearing-scribe keeps his draft of the old striking-off, but the hearing sits on the halls' rule that a copyist answers for every line in her hand, a watermark shows where a sheet came from and not who set the line, a copy of an exemplar proves nothing of where its lines began, the master's finding is a reading room's and not the Wayhouse's, and the scribe may not bring a hearing the draft its rule forbids, so the bar lands, and the crisis reads the finding: with it in hand, the ally's way stands on the scribe's sworn word on the paper as well as on his draft.
  - *The tell:* She still copies in the hand her teacher gave her, and won't change it, for she will not see her teacher ruined.
  - *The ways:* The ways are destroying what the teacher built, every copy made from her sheets burned (the code-break), the hearing-scribe reading his old draft, which names the master's book that night, so the Wayhouse's own record answers the teacher's letter (his price: his post kept), or following her to her tower and deciding there.

- **R1 · `{reading_room}`.** In the market of `{reading_room}` the heart's old sign-painter master has lost his eyes and his trade with them (Restore *Lost livelihood*), and the heart, a letter-writer's apprentice who copies for the reading room by the sheet, tells the player she wants to be sworn a copyist at the Wayhouse (her want), that no hall would take her and a struck-off copyist found her copying shop signs and taught her letters and the halls' hand for nothing (her past), and that her mother, a market woman of `{mother_town}`, has never asked who taught her, while the reading room's master says of her copies that hers is "a hand the halls can't tell from their own".
- **R2 · `{reading_room}`.** At `{reading_room}`, which buys its exemplars from the tower, a checker means to burn a batch's exemplar sheets as spoiled, and the player copies them before they burn (Acquire *Copy it before it burns*), "exemplar sheets burned as spoiled", while in secret the tower's agent (the Hand), who sells the exemplars to the reading room, told the checker they were spoiled, because the exemplars are the only proof that the false lines came from the sheet and not the hand that copied it.
- **R3 · `{r3_hall}`.** At `{r3_hall}` a contract copy has been altered by its clerk for himself (Investigate *The ledger doesn't add up*), and the bought exemplars beside it show an absence: ∅ "no name on the exemplars: every sheet unsigned".
- **Reveal · `{reading_room}`.** At `{reading_room}` the shepherd boy who carries the hall's exemplar sheets down from the tower won't go back up the hill, and the reading room's master, a man who checks everything twice, wants him brought round (Passage *Unwilling passenger*), and the boy says a woman in the tower writes the sheets, dries them on a line, and pays him to bring them down to the agent at the paper stall, while the master adds, laying one of the boy's sheets beside the hall's old roll, that it has a line set in that the roll never had, and, from the hall's copy of the Wayhouse's striking-off roll, who would write so, the copyist struck off twenty years ago, and which master brought the charge.
  "No name on the exemplars" leads up the hill to the tower, the tower's sheets explain "exemplar sheets burned as spoiled", since they were the proof, and those explain "a hand the halls can't tell from their own", the apprentice's, which copied every line exactly as the sheet set it.
- **Chapter 1's move.** The struck-off copyist nails to the door of `{reading_room}` a list of twelve Writers' copies, each false line marked, unsigned, in a hand the Wayhouse's old hearing-scribe will know.
- **C1-1 · `{tower}`.** The player goes up to the tower, {tower_look}, to see what she does there (Threat *Go and look*), and finds "exemplars drying on the line, the false lines already in".
- **C1-2 · `{reading_room}`.** Therefore in the market of `{reading_room}` a merchant whose copy is on the list, its false line changing a sum, cries the reading room cheats (Dispute *Public offense*), and the twelve copies turn out to be "twelve copies in one hand", the apprentice's.
- **C1-3 · `{reading_room}`.** But at the market's paper stall the tower's agent (the Hand) holds the next batch of exemplars as his own stock and won't sell it to the halls (Acquire *Won't sell*), while the Wayhouse's old hearing-scribe, who wrote down the striking-off twenty years ago and kept his draft against the rule, has come to buy paper, knows the hand on the batch, and will help for his post kept, though the draft breaks the rule.
- **C2-0 · `{reading_room}`.** At pressure two, at `{reading_room}`, the twelve copies' owners hold the reading room to its oath that a copy stands, and its master, a man who checked everything twice, must answer them (Dispute *Broken agreement*).
- **Blow and door (b) · `{reading_room}`.** At `{reading_room}` the false lines are found scraped off three of the twelve copies and the player is framed for scraping the false lines away to hide them, and must find who did (Investigate *Sabotage*), and on the door the old hearing-scribe, who has bought the halls' paper for forty years, holds a sheet to the light, for the exemplars' paper carries the tower press's watermark and the halls' paper doesn't: "the tower press's watermark".
- **Code-bending way.** The door carries one extra way that destroys a text: burn one of the twelve copies, so its owner can't take it to court.
- **Crisis · at `{wayhouse}`.** The Wayhouse's hearing, `{days}` days after the summons, has barred the apprentice's hand from every hall for the twelve false lines, and that night the struck-off copyist's letter is read at the Wall, saying the lines were hers, as the master's line was his, and calling the girl "my hand", so the eldest copyist, {eldest_manner}, who read it, gives the card (Reckoning *Ruin from above*, the old hearing-scribe acting for the apprentice): ruin the teacher, or answer her, and say whether the Wayhouse owns two wrongs.
  - *How it reads blow b:* With the player framed, the scraped places count at the hearing against the apprentice's side, as though her friends had tried to hide her lines.
  - *Standing:* Framed and not yet cleared, the player keeps rank and may sit at the Wayhouse's hearing as a Writer under a charge, heard last and sworn on nothing.
  - *What the player holds:* Before the hearing the player holds the watermark finding and the copy of the burned exemplars, the reading room's master found a line in an exemplar that the hall's roll never had, and the hearing-scribe keeps his draft of the old striking-off, but the hearing sits on the halls' rule that a copyist answers for every line in her hand, a watermark shows where a sheet came from and not who set the line, a copy of an exemplar proves nothing of where its lines began, the master's finding is a reading room's and not the Wayhouse's, and the scribe may not bring a hearing the draft its rule forbids, so the bar lands, and the crisis reads the finding: with it in hand, the ally's way stands on the scribe's sworn word on the paper as well as on his draft.
  - *The tell:* She still copies in the hand her teacher gave her, and won't change it, for she will not see her teacher ruined.
  - *The ways:* The ways are destroying what the teacher built, every copy made from her sheets burned (the code-break), the hearing-scribe reading his old draft, which names the master's book that night, so the Wayhouse's own record answers the teacher's letter (his price: his post kept), or following her to her tower and deciding there.
- **Endings.**
  - *Amends made* (the teacher confesses at the Wall; the apprentice's bar lifted and the teacher's name restored to the roll, the master's line owned): won by persuasion; who pays: the old hall master (struck off at last) · the guild's name (two wrong judgments owned).
  - *Burn your copy* (code-break: every copy made from the teacher's sheets burned): won by stealth; who pays: the twelve copies' owners (their contracts burned) · the halls' records. ← shown
  - *The requester forgives* (the apprentice goes to the tower to copy with her teacher): won by persuasion; who pays: the heart (barred from every hall for good) · the halls (they lose the best hand they had).
- **A month on (Burn your copy).** The bar lifted, with no lines left to judge, and her teacher's work is ash / the market has no copies to doubt / the teacher's revenge is half undone, and half done.
