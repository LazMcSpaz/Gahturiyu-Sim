# W3-B · The Copy House Across the Street

Every branch × ending combination of `spines.md` draft 5, rendered by `roll_samples.py --render-all`. Slots are shown as `{slot}`, flavour as its own slot; one sentence per line. The routine's swaps are not multiplied out (each is one card's other premise, printed in the spine's field 18).

**6 combinations:** 2 branch(es) × 3 endings.

### Branch a × E1 · Obtained and handed over

*Blow a: heart · disgraced · cried down for taking a side in public (`cried-down`) → door Dispute Public offense. Ending shown: Obtained and handed over.*

- **R1 · `{reading_room}`.** At `{reading_room}` two heirs dispute one farm whose roll entry decides it (Dispute *Contested inheritance*), and the heart, the half-blind reading master who knows the entry by ear, asks the player to read to her for her concordance of the court rolls, every name to its page, which she wants finished before her eyes go (her want), for she lost most of her sight copying these rolls by lamplight in the winter of the great lawsuit (her past), while the heirs' quarrel turns up "a lawyer's clerk quoting rolls the halls never copied for him".
- **R2 · `{lord}`.** In the counting room of the court of `{lord}`, where the court pays the halls' copying bill, the halls' bill as it stands in the counting room's book has been altered upward (Investigate *The ledger doesn't add up*), showing "a line for copies the halls never made", while in secret the copyist's sister (the Hand), now a clerk in that counting room, altered it so the court thinks the halls overcharge and opens the commission to bids.
- **R3 · `{bindery_hall}`.** At `{bindery_hall}` an old bindery's trade has failed, its bookbinder having bound for the reading room of `{reading_room}` for thirty years (Restore *Lost livelihood*), and its press-room stands empty, ∅ "the bindery's empty press-room", its presses and cords sold to "a buyer from the court's town".
- **Reveal · `{pawn_town}`.** At `{pawn_town}`, where the heart has come with the player to read, a pawnbroker holds a box of "spoiled sheets" a woman of the Market pawned from her back room for a first month's rent, not knowing they are court-roll entries with the halls' margin notes (Acquire *Unknown worth*), and as the player reads them aloud the heart knows the entries by ear and the notes as her own hall's, who owes and who is weak, and adds, from her copying book, which says who read which entries back to her this season, that they are her copyist's copies.
  The heart asks the player three things the routine answered, who quoted rolls the halls never copied for him, "a lawyer's clerk quoting rolls", who billed the court for "a line for copies the halls never made", and who bought an old bindery's presses, "the bindery's empty press-room", and says it is one house, and it hasn't opened yet.
- **Chapter 1's move.** The forger opens a copy house across the street from `{reading_room}`, {house_look}, bidding half the halls' price for the court rolls.
- **C1-1 · `{record_house}`.** Out at the court's record house outside the town walls the court's steward disputes the halls' account with them, under the old commission by which the halls keep the rolls "while the halls are true" (Dispute *Broken agreement*).
- **C1-2 · `{reading_room}`.** Therefore the player watches the copy house's lane at night (Investigate *Watch them*) and sees the copyist's sister carry bundles to "a second door onto the lane".
- **C1-3 · `{reading_room}`.** But at the copy house the forger has staffed it with young copyists bound for Market debts, whom her door-keeper keeps (Rescue *Bound by debt*), and the old bookbinder whose presses she bought, come to see them at work in her press-room, who sold them under a threat from the Night Market and bound the heart's rolls for thirty years, will help for the halls buying his presses back.
- **C2-0 · `{record_house}`.** At pressure two, in the court's archive room at the record house, the steward means to burn the halls' old copies once the new house delivers, because one of them carries "a margin note on the steward's own debt", owed to `{house}`, and the player copies them before they burn (Acquire *Copy it before it burns*).
- **Blow and door (a) · `{record_house}`.** At the court's record house the heart told the steward in open court that the new house's copies are stolen, and the forger has her cried down for it, a keeper who took sides against a lawful bidder, before the court she spoke to (Dispute *Public offense*), and on the door the old bookbinder, who knows whose money bought his presses, says the copy house's rent is owed to the Market and she has never been out of debt in her life: "the copy house's rent, owed to the Market".
- **Code-bending way.** The door carries one extra way that destroys a text: burn the stolen bundle of margins found at the lane door.
- **Crisis · at `{reading_room}`.** The court of `{lord}` has sealed its copying commission to the copy house across the street for the year and sent the heart a letter ending the halls' keeping, and the forger, {forger_look}, won't sell it back, for she means to go straight at last after this one last sale, so at the reading room, the court's letter in her hand, the heart gives the card because her keeping has ended (Acquire *Won't sell*): win the rolls back, and say at what price to the forger and to the halls' secrets.
  - *How it reads blow a:* Cried down for taking sides against a lawful bidder, the heart has already said in open court what she knows, so when the seal comes her word is the halls' grievance, and only the bookbinder's oath or a deal can carry what she heard.
  - *What the player holds:* The player knows from the pawned sheets and the lane's second door that the copy house's stock is stolen, the heart knows the entries by ear, and the player holds the halls' copy of the steward's margin note, but the court seals to the lower bid under the old commission's own words, "while the halls are true", the altered account says the halls are not, and the halls' own account against the counting room's book is the halls' word against the court's own clerk, the stolen sheets can't be shown to the court without showing it the halls' margins, a keeper's ear is no proof in a court, and the steward's note shames the steward and not the forger, so the seal lands, and the crisis is the chance to undo it.
  - *The tell:* The carriage of `{house}` waits at the lane door on court days, for the first year's copies are pledged to that house.
  - *The ways:* The ways are burning the commission deed in her strongroom (the code-break), the old bookbinder swearing his presses were bought under a false name, for a commission sealed to a house bought in a false name is void (his price: the presses back), or giving her what the house does for her another way, the halls taking her on as a sworn copyist with her bond paid and the young copyists bound to her with her.
- **Endings.**
  - *Obtained and handed over* (she sells it back for a place on the halls' roll and her bond paid, and the young copyists she bound are sworn with her): won by bribe; who pays: the halls' oath (a forger is sworn in) · `{house}` (loses the rolls it was promised, and will come for her). ← shown
  - *Burn your copy* (code-break: the deed burned; the court must seal again, to whoever brings the oldest copies): won by stealth; who pays: the forger (her house fails; back into the Market's bond) · the halls (it's known they burned a deed).
  - *Trade what we know* (the steward bought with the margin note on his own debt; he voids her seal): won by bribe; who pays: `{house}`, the steward's creditor (the note names it) · the halls (now the steward knows what the margins hold).
- **A month on (Obtained and handed over).** Finishes her concordance, and the court that heard her cried down sends its clerks to read it / the rolls come back to the reading room, copied by the young copyists the Market had bound / she copies beside the heart, and is watched.
