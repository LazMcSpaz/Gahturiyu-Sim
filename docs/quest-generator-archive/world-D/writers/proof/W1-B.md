# W1-B · The Stone That Walked

Every branch × ending combination of `spines.md` draft 5, rendered by `roll_samples.py --render-all`. Slots are shown as `{slot}`, flavour as its own slot; one sentence per line. The routine's swaps are not multiplied out (each is one card's other premise, printed in the spine's field 18).

**6 combinations:** 2 branch(es) × 3 endings.

### Branch a × E1 · Open the ledgers

*Blow a: heart · disgraced · cried down for taking a side in public (`cried-down`) → door Restore Lost good name. Ending shown: Open the ledgers.*

- **R1 · `{free_hall}`.** At `{free_hall}` a survey roll has been altered to give a miller his neighbour's strip, and comparing it with another hall's copy shows who did it for himself (Investigate *The ledger doesn't add up*), while the other hall's copy carries the margin note "two readings for one stone, ten years apart, both in the halls' hand".
- **R2 · `{reading_room}`.** At the relay loft beside the survey hall in `{reading_room}`, {loft_look}, the loft keeper docks the heart's pay for blotted sheets (Dispute *Broken agreement*), and the heart, a letter-writer's apprentice who copies the survey hall's outgoing sheets onto loft paper by number at night, tells the player she is saving her night coin to bring her mother, who still farms a strip in the border village, to live in town before winter (her want), because the village headman sent her to the halls at twelve "to learn the letters for us" (her past), and the loft's book holds ∅ "sheets logged by number, with no name and no sender".
- **R3 · `{border_village}`.** At the border village's parish chapel the priest means to burn his tithe book, and the player copies it before it burns (Acquire *Copy it before it burns*), keeping "the tithe book's last page: which farms paid to which chapel", while in secret the survey hall's chain-book copyist (the Hand) told the priest that the halls already hold a copy and that the book would draw the lords' quarrel into his chapel, because it is the one record outside the halls of where the border ran, and its burning leaves the halls' readings the only word on it.
- **Reveal · `{lord_a}`.** At the seat of `{lord_a}`, where the heart carries the loft's night sheets by hand for the seat's steward, who takes court-roll sheets only sealed and from a hand he knows, never by pigeon, she brings the player to the lord's retired surveyor, shunned as "the man who lost the border" (Restore *Lost good name*), whose kept contrary readings show that each reached his master's court as the other lord's objection within the month of a stone he set, on loft paper with a loft number and no name, numbers the heart knows as her own because she copied every one at night, and the heart adds, from the loft's night book, where every sheet she copies is the survey hall's outgoing post signed out by its master, that the master has been releasing old readings out of the court rolls the halls keep for `{lord_a}`, and so the halls have been steering the border.
  Her own number on his reading leads to "sheets logged by number, with no name and no sender", those lead to the other hall's margin, "two readings for one stone, ten years apart", and that leads to the one record of the border the halls never held, "the tithe book's last page", which is why it had to burn.
- **Chapter 1's move.** Before the whole survey hall the master dismisses a junior copyist who asked who has been sending border readings out of the rolls of `{lord_a}`, silencing him by naming an old fault of his.
- **C1-1 · `{border_village}`.** With the halls' steering known, the player rides out to the border by the village to watch the masons of `{lord_a}` set out a line from a fresh reading their steward calls his own survey (Investigate *Watch them*), with the survey hall's eldest surveyor, who swore every stone he ever set, beside the player, and across the line the surveyor of `{lord_b}`, born on the border and wanting the line fixed, watches the same masons and will help for the halls surveying his master's line too, free, while the masons' foreman works from "a fresh reading in the steward's hand".
- **C1-2 · `{border_village}`.** Therefore the border village quarrels with the masons over the strip the fresh reading claims, which takes in the heart's mother's strip (Dispute *Shared resource*), while "a new stone cut and waiting on the cart" stands by.
- **C1-3 · `{reading_room}`.** But at `{reading_room}` the survey hall's chain-book copyist (the Hand) holds his own chain-book of every stone and every reading sent and won't lend it (Acquire *Won't sell*), and he has shown the steward of `{lord_b}` the ally's name in it beside a Writer's, so the surveyor of `{lord_b}` is ordered off the border, and can still swear the true line in person before a court but can never walk it with the player again.
- **C2-0 · `{border_village}`.** At pressure two the player goes out to the border at night to count the men of `{lord_b}` camped across the line (Threat *Go and look*).
- **Blow and door (a) · `{reading_room}`.** At `{reading_room}` the heart is cried down for taking a side, because she warned her own village that "the stone will walk" and the master has it said she backed a lord (Restore *Lost good name*, cause *exaggerated*), and on the door the eldest surveyor says he will stand against the master if a village pays for the master's readings.
- **Code-bending way.** The door carries one extra way that breaks the halls' rule against taking a side: tell the border village's headman, privately, which reading is the true line.
- **Crisis · at `{border_village}`.** One reading too many, sent unsigned to the steward of `{lord_b}` the week the masons of `{lord_a}` set their new stone, has put the border village's fields on his side, his men have come to pull the stone down, the men of `{lord_a}` have answered, and the village has burned in a fire spreading from the night's fighting, so among the ashes the heart's mother gives the card for her burned house to the leader, who has come off the road to see what the halls' readings did and judges there in person whether the guild keeps its steering or owns it (Reckoning *Schism*, the eldest surveyor as its fault line), and the master of the survey hall at `{reading_room}`, ridden out to her, {master_manner}, says plainly that "the border is safer open".
  - *How it reads blow a:* Cried down, the heart is believed in her own village and nowhere in the hall, so at the judging her number in the night book is her word against the master's, and the leader must weigh a disgraced apprentice's copying against a hall master's name.
  - *What the player holds:* Before the stone moved the player knew the masons worked from a fresh reading, and from the reveal that the halls have been steering the border, the player holds the copied tithe page, and the retired surveyor holds the contrary readings, but no Writer can stop a lord's masons or his neighbour's men, the old readings show the halls' hand and not which line is true, a lord's court reads a priest's own book and not a Writer's copy of it, and the halls' rule forbids telling either lord which reading is true, so the fire lands, and if the player told the headman on the door the village got its old people out before it, and the heart's mother stands before the leader with her neighbours, not alone.
  - *The tell:* The loft's night book, brought out to the village by the eldest surveyor, shows the heart's own number against that week's sheet to the steward of `{lord_b}`, for the reading that sent his men was copied in her hand.
  - *The ways:* The ways are the halls standing before the court of `{lord_a}` for the village's line (the code-break, taking a side in public), the surveyor of `{lord_b}` swearing the true line in person (his price: the halls survey his master's line too, free), or bringing it to the leader's own judgment, with the eldest surveyor speaking.
- **Endings.**
  - *Open the ledgers* (every reading she sent, and her name, published, with the true line sworn beside them): won by law; who pays: the guild's name (both lords learn the halls steered them) · the master (struck off). ← shown
  - *Keep the balance* (the leader keeps the master; the village is paid for): won by persuasion; who pays: the heart (the village rebuilds on the moved line; her mother's strip is lost) · `{fault}` (he leaves).
  - *Take a side at last* (code-break: the halls stand for the village before `{lord_a}`'s court): won by law; who pays: the guild's neutrality (`{lord_a}` takes his rolls from the halls; S7 lost).
- **A month on (Open the ledgers).** Her mother lives in town at last, and the village that heard her cried down knows she warned it / the village rebuilds on the old stone / the master keeps a tower and her readings.
