### Branch a × E1 · Amends made

*Blow a: heart · estranged · his kin turned (`kin-turned`) → door Restore Estrangement. Ending shown: Amends made.*

- **R1 · `{reading_room}`.** At the Wall of `{reading_room}` the heart, a carter who can't write and whose wife can't read, disputes with his master the price of buying the cart he drives (Dispute *Broken agreement*), and the hall reads the hire contract to both at the reading-out bench, where the Wall clerk's reading-out book lies open at his name, while the heart wants to buy his own cart and stop hauling for a master (his want), and the book shows that once, years ago, he asked the clerk not to read his wife the letter that says he sends money from the cart savings to a child elsewhere, still in his file (his past): ∅ "one letter in the reading-out book marked 'kept back'".
- **R2 · `{barn}`.** The player carries a sealed "love letter" waiting at the Wall of `{reading_room}` to a sweetheart a day out of town, past the gate where the watch has begun opening sealed post (Passage *Smuggling*), and it is "a love letter sealed with the copy room's own wax", while in secret the sweetheart is the forger's runner (the Hand), and the letter is a bundle of file copies the copyist sends her through the Wall's own post under the copy room's wax so the sorters pass it unopened, so the player has carried the forger's stock to her.
- **R3 · `{widow_town}`.** At `{widow_town}`, whose letters all go through the Wall, a widow means to burn her husband's letters (Acquire *Copy it before it burns*), and as the player copies them she lets fall "four silver paid to a market woman for 'the only copy'".
- **Reveal · `{reading_room}`.** At the reading room of `{reading_room}` the Wall clerk who reads letters out to those who can't, met here first, asks the player who has been taking from the copy room at night (Investigate *Theft*), and the night porter, who took a woman's coin to look away and is ashamed of it, shows what he saw with his own eyes, that he let a copyist of the hall into the copy room after the bell and watched him carry file bundles out to the woman, who waits at the lane end and pays in Market notes, while the Wall clerk adds, from her own reading-out book, that the halls keep a file on every family whose letters go through the Wall and that the families she reads to have begun asking her whether a market woman's "only copy" is theirs, and the porter adds what the woman told him the night she first paid him, that she was buying her daughter's release from a Night Market bond (the syndicate's own servitude).
  The files are why a family would pay "four silver for 'the only copy'", a family's file is how a market woman could know of "one letter marked 'kept back'", and the copy room's own seal is how the bundles left by the Wall unopened, "a love letter sealed with the copy room's own wax".
- **Chapter 1's move.** The forger has it cried in the market square of `{reading_room}` that forged Writers' files on the town's families are going about, and that she can get a family its own out of circulation.
- **C1-1 · `{barn}`.** Following the cry to the market it came from, the player goes into the night market in its barn a day out, {barn_look}, to see what she sells and who brings it (Threat *Go and look*), and finds "files bound with the copy room's cord".
- **C1-2 · `{reading_room}`.** But the cry bites in town: in the carters' yard, carters who bought their files and found their own letters in them, cried up by the forger's crier, cry the reading room thieves of their letters and shout down the halls' copyist who came to deny it (Dispute *Public offense*), and they take up "a collection for 'clean copies'".
- **C1-3 · `{reading_room}`.** Therefore the shouted-down copyist, the one who lets the forger in and one she has an old false line on, so she has no tie to him she'd spare, fears the Market and runs, and the runner (the Hand) hunts him on the forger's orders because a copyist caught by the halls would lead them to her (Passage *Fugitive*), while the Wall clerk, who knows every family who bought, asks the player to bring the copyist back to the hall alive so the families hear from his own mouth who sold them, and her price, for later, is the reading-out book kept from the hall master.
- **C2-0 · `{reading_room}`.** At pressure two, at the reading room of `{reading_room}`, a carter's widow holds her family's file, bought from the forger at the night market, not knowing that its copy-room hand is the proof the "forgeries" are the halls' own (Acquire *Unknown worth*).
- **Blow and door (a) · `{reading_room}`.** Because the heart has begun gathering the carters against her, the forger has his kept-back letter read to his wife in the market, where every family sees what the forger holds, so his wife learns of the money sent to a child elsewhere and means to leave for her sister's at the season's end, and the door is the heart's making it right with her (Restore *Estrangement*, cause `secret-told`), and on it the Wall clerk, who hears every family's business at the reading-out bench, says the forger has hired the big booth for the market's last day and a cart for the night after: "a booth for the last day, a cart for the night after".
- **Code-bending way.** The door carries one extra way that destroys a text: burn one family's bought file, at their begging.
- **Crisis · at `{reading_room}`.** On the market's last day the forger, {forger_manner}, has sold the town its files at the booth, the heart has paid his cart savings for his own and his wife's, and she has paid her bond and gone by night with the rest of her stock to a far hall town under a new name, so at the Wall where every letter he sent went through the heart gives the card because he paid for what the hall let out (Reckoning *Swindle*): go after her for the families' coin, for her stock, or let her keep the freedom she bought.
  - *How it reads blow a:* With his kept-back letter read to his wife in the market and his wife bound for her sister's, the heart pays his cart savings at the booth for his own file and hers all the same, so that nothing more of theirs is read out.
  - *What the player holds:* The player knew her trade from the reveal, the porter's own eyes and the Wall clerk's reading-out book, the booth's day from the door, and that the widow's file in the copy room's hand proves the "forgeries" are the halls' own, but the families bought willingly, no law forbids selling a family its own letters, and the one warning that would have stopped them is that proof said aloud, which is the halls' shame, so the sale landed, and the crisis asks who pays for it.
  - *The tell:* The cart she hired was for a heavier load than a woman and a chest, for she took the unsold files to sell to the houses they shame.
  - *The ways:* The ways are destroying her stock of copies and the halls' family files they were made from (the code-break), the Wall clerk naming every family who bought so that they act together (her price: the reading-out book kept from the hall master), or following her to her new life and deciding there.
