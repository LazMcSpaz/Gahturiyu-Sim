# W1-A · The Truce Held for a Debt

Every branch × ending combination of `spines.md` draft 5, rendered by `roll_samples.py --render-all`. Slots are shown as `{slot}`, flavour as its own slot; one sentence per line. The routine's swaps are not multiplied out (each is one card's other premise, printed in the spine's field 18).

**6 combinations:** 2 branch(es) × 3 endings.

### Branch a × E1 · Lodged on the ruling

*Blow a: heart · disgraced · cried down for taking a side in public (`cried-down`) → door Dispute Public offense. Ending shown: Lodged on the ruling.*

- **R1 · `{reading_room}`.** At `{reading_room}` a loan-roll page has been altered for a grain buyer by the clerk who sold it him (Investigate *The ledger doesn't add up*), and the heart, the hall's old clerk who knows the original by heart, asks whether the player has a steady hand, because she wants to train one apprentice to keep the rolls after her (her want), and says she copied half these lines herself as a girl, the ford-mill debt among them (her past), while beside the rolls lie letters for the lords' stewards, chalked by the hall's own sorters, with ∅ "the blank sender column".
- **R2 · `{lord_b}`.** From `{reading_room}`'s Wall branch the player carries a sealed "will" into the lordship of `{lord_b}`, past his toll, where sealed letters from the other side are searched (Passage *Smuggling*), and its seal is "a hall seal no hall cut", while in secret the will is one of the master's debt letters, whose true recipient at the steward's gate is her Wall sorter (the Hand), who rides the branch's post out each market eve and passes it on unsigned, so the player has carried her letter into the house of `{lord_b}` through the guild's own post.
- **R3 · `{mill_market}`.** At `{mill_market}` the ford's miller brings the hall his father's mill tally to be read, not knowing what it is worth to either lord (Acquire *Unknown worth*), and says his mill "has stood in two lords' debt since before I was born": "the ford mill's old debt".
- **Reveal · `{lord_b}`.** At the seat of `{lord_b}` the leader, off the road, asks only to read the bundle of unsigned letters the lord's retired steward keeps and won't part with (Acquire *Won't sell*), and they show twenty years of true debts of the house of `{lord_a}`, each sent the season `{lord_b}` grew strong, in roll wording and a hall sorter's chalk, while the retired steward says, from the house's oldest account book, kept before his time, that the old debt began as a famine year's seed-grain loan, and the leader adds, from knowing every hall's hand, that the chalk is `{reading_room}`'s and so its master's, and, from what the master said to her face at the last hall masters' table, that the master holds balance to be the Writers' oldest duty, and the retired steward adds that a truce is coming, which his old house talks of.
  The leader, who is not sorry ("I'd have done it too"), lays a letter's chalk beside "the blank sender column", its seal beside "a hall seal no hall cut", and, last, the one debt the bundle never used beside the miller's tally, "the ford mill's old debt", the letter the master has kept back.
- **Chapter 1's move.** The master takes the sorting of her hall's Wall branch into her own hands, "since letters have gone astray".
- **C1-1 · `{ford_mill}`.** At the ford mill on the border, {mill_look}, the two lords' tenants have broken the mill race after the latest letter, each carting stones to its own bank (Restore *Broken works*), and the miller and his mother, the old miller's widow who keeps the mill's tally, stand between them, while the miller explains that the lords mean to lodge a truce at `{reading_room}` on "the truce day", `{truce_day}`, that it binds once lodged there, and that a lodging someone objects to is "held until ruled".
- **C1-2 · `{reading_room}`.** Therefore at `{reading_room}` the player watches who carries letters out of the branch now the master sorts it (Investigate *Watch them*), and sees "the back-stair satchel" go out on a market eve, while the sealed truce, agreed at last, has come in through the branch to wait for its lodging day, and the heart has made "the hall's door copy" of it, as the hall copies every lodging.
- **C1-3 · `{lord_b}`.** But at the gatehouse of `{lord_b}`'s steward the steward's clerk says she paid for her lord's reply to the other house's draft to go by the Wall a month before the truce was sealed, and it never came (Dispute *Broken agreement*), the sorter (the Hand) says it was never handed in and the steward takes his side, and the clerk, who lets slip that the carriage was paid from "the steward's grain store, in the roll under his wife's name", wants the truce because her family farms both banks, and will help for her own name kept off every copy of the branch's carriage book.
- **C2-0 · `{ford_mill}`.** At the ford again, with the race broken and both banks' tenants coming to blows over its stones, the miller must be got off the ford to `{mill_market}` before he is hurt, and won't leave the wheel his father kept turning (Passage *Unwilling passenger*).
- **Blow and door (a) · `{reading_room}`.** In `{reading_room}`'s market the heart is cried down for taking a side in public, because, when the steward of `{lord_b}` called her hall the other house's creature, she named aloud, from the roll, his own debt, and he now stands offended before the market (Dispute *Public offense*), and on the door the old miller's widow says her late husband ground the grain of `{lord_b}`'s house free for seven years "to clear the mill": "seven years' free grinding".
- **Code-bending way.** The door carries one extra way that destroys a text: scrape out the market clerk's note of the debt she named aloud, which clears her and breaks the code.
- **Crisis · at `{reading_room}`.** `{days}` days before `{truce_day}` the ford-mill debt reaches the steward of `{lord_b}` unsigned, he rides to `{reading_room}` and lodges a sealed objection with the master, {master_manner}, and she holds the truce in her strongroom "until the debt is ruled", a hold the heart, as the hall's oldest clerk, must write into the key-book against the line she copied as a girl, and so the heart gives the card (Acquire *Under seal*): let the hold stand, or force the truce through before the evening bell ends the lodging day.
  - *How it reads blow a:* Cried down in the market, the heart writes the hold into the key-book all the same, and her word that she copied the line as a girl must now stand against the market's laughter as well as against her own master.
  - *What the player holds:* The player saw the back-stair satchel and knows the hold rule before the last letter goes, the heart holds the hall's door copy of the truce, and the leader has read the retired steward's letters, but the letter is a true debt carried unsigned by the Wall's own post, no rule lets a Writer open a sealed letter or warn one lord of another's debt, the old letters prove the sending and not that the debt is false, a lodging takes the sealed truce and not a clerk's copy, and a ruling reads the roll's page and nobody's memory, so the hold lands, and the crisis is the player's chance against the hold itself.
  - *The tell:* The key-book, in the heart's own hand, reads "one truce, received sealed, in our hand", for what the strongroom holds is the hall's door copy, and the steward took the sealed original away with his objection.
  - *The ways:* The ways are burning the ford-mill page of the loan roll, so there is no debt to rule (the code-break), the steward's clerk swearing to the seven years' grinding before the hall (her price: her name on the copy after all), or holding the ruling before the evening bell, the page read aloud, the heart swearing to the line she copied as a girl and the widow's grinding set against it.
- **Endings.**
  - *Lodged on the ruling* (the ruling clears the debt by the grinding; the steward withdraws, and the truce is lodged): won by law; who pays: the steward's clerk (named; her master turns her off). ← shown
  - *Burn your copy* (code-break: the ford-mill page burned, so the objection has no debt to rest on and the steward must hand the sealed truce back for its lodging): won by stealth; who pays: the heart (the line she copied as a girl is ash, and what she remembers of it rules nothing; she leaves the rolls) · `{lord_b}`'s house (a true claim gone).
  - *Keep the balance* (the hold stands; the lodging day passes): won by nobody; who pays: the ford's tenants (the race is broken again within the season) · the steward's clerk.
- **A month on (Lodged on the ruling).** Keeps the rolls, cried down and proved right, and has taken an apprentice / the mill grinds for both banks / the master writes these lords no more letters, and waits.
