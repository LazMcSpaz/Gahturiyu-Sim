### Branch a × E1 · A deal is struck

*Blow a: player · stripped of rank · (`stripped`) → door Restore Lost good name. Ending shown: A deal is struck.*

- **R1 · `{reading_room}`.** At `{reading_room}` two heirs dispute one copied will (Dispute *Contested inheritance*), and the heart, the copyist they confide in, tells the player that her oldest friend from the Wayhouse's copying tables "never made a false line in her life", that she wants to see her friend's name back on the Wayhouse's roll (her want), that twenty years ago she sat silent at the hearing that struck her friend off and has not seen her since (her past), and that her daughter copies at the same table, while the clue is "a copyist struck off twenty years ago for one false line".
- **R2 · `{lord}`.** In the counting room of the court of `{lord}` the court's copying day-book disagrees with the halls' delivery book (Investigate *The ledger doesn't add up*), showing the halls' "copies entered 'late, unsealed'", while in secret the court's under-clerk (the Hand) made those entries so that the court checks every halls' copy line by line against the original, alone with its clerk, which is when she sets her false lines in.
- **R3 · `{r3_hall}`.** At `{r3_hall}` a bookbinder bought in good faith a leaf of the Wayhouse's old copyists' roll, sold off as the Wayhouse's scrap (Acquire *Passed on*), and on it the player finds an absence, ∅ "a leaf with one name cut out".
- **Reveal · `{son_town}`.** At `{son_town}` the hall master's son, a carter met here first, asks the player to carry a letter to his father, to whom he hasn't spoken since he was a boy (Restore *Estrangement*), and tells why from what he saw with his own eyes, that on the night of the false line twenty years ago he saw his father sign out the copying book and carry it home, and that the next morning the false line was in it, a debt in his father's own favour, and he adds that the court's new clerk, who copies left-handed and goes by a dead aunt's name, is the copyist his father struck off, for he knew her as a boy at the Wayhouse's tables and knew her again at the court's counter when he paid his carting dues there.
  The son asks the player three things: what a copyist "struck off twenty years ago for one false line" does with her hand now, why a court would enter the halls' copies "late, unsealed", and whose name was cut from the Wayhouse's leaf, "a leaf with one name cut out".
- **Chapter 1's move.** Under her court name the clerk has it cried at the court of `{lord}` that the Writers' copies of its rolls are false, and the court's commissioner orders every copy checked.
- **C1-1 · `{tenant_village}`.** Out at `{tenant_village}`, a village of the court's own tenants, two tenants quarrel over a field that the court's checked copy of the rolls now gives to one of them, by a line the halls' copy never had (Dispute *Shared resource*), and the other tenant, whose lease rests on the halls' copy, shows the player "a field moved by one line".
- **C1-2 · `{lord}`.** But the move bites: the commissioner lets no Writer into the check room, so the player must smuggle into the court, past the under-clerk keeping the gate, a fresh copy made at the reading room from the hall's own master roll, which never went through the check room, to lay beside the sealed copies and show which lines changed in the court's keeping (Passage *Smuggling*).
- **C1-3 · `{lord}`.** Therefore, with the fresh copy showing lines changed in the court's keeping, the player watches the check room, {check_room_look}, after the bell (Investigate *Watch them*), and sees "a lamp in the check room after the bell" and "a court clerk who copies left-handed", as the heart's friend did, while the hall master's son, who wants his father's wrong owned, asks the player to keep his father from a public striking-off, and that is his price.
- **C2-0 · `{tenant_village}`.** At pressure two, at the tenants' village, the tenant holds the halls' first copy of his lease, made before any check, not knowing it is the one copy of his entry the court clerk never touched: "a lease copy made before the check" (Acquire *Unknown worth*).
- **Blow and door (a) · `{reading_room}`.** Because the player smuggled the fresh copy in, the clerk's court name tells it at the halls, and the player is stripped of rank, the stain "carries copies into a court by night" (Restore *Lost good name*), and on the door the heart finds on the reading room's shelves the hall's copy of that year's Wayhouse copying book, which shows the master's book signed out that night under his own name: "the night the master signed out the book".
- **Code-bending way.** The door carries one extra way that destroys a text: scrape one of her false lines off a halls' copy.
- **Crisis · at `{lord}`.** At the court of `{lord}` the commissioner lays the halls' copy beside the court's original and three false lines are found, and the court clerk, {clerk_look}, in her own name at last, accuses the hall's copy, until the commissioner names its copyist, the heart, and the clerk, who never knew whose hand she had been falsifying, goes white and presses on, so the tenant, who has come to the reading room to ask the hall for an advocate because his lease and field rest on that copy, gives the card (Dispute *Public offense*, the commissioner as arbiter): save the halls' name by sinking the heart's friend, or tell the old wrong and risk the commission.
  - *How it reads blow a:* With the player stripped for carrying copies into a court by night, the fresh copy comes before the commissioner in a stripped advocate's hands, and must be heard for what it shows, not for who brings it.
  - *Standing:* Stripped of rank, the player stands at the hearing as the tenant's advocate, as any party before a court may name one, and speaks for him and the heart, not for the hall.
  - *What the player holds:* The player knows the court's clerk is the struck-off copyist and holds the fresh copy, the tenant holds a lease copy made before the check, the master's son saw the night of the false line, and the heart holds the year's copying book showing the master's book signed out that night, but the commissioner's comparing is lawful and public, the halls can't accuse the court's own clerk without the old wrong, which the master won't tell and his son will tell only at his price, and the signed-out book shows that the master took the book home and not what he wrote in it, so the crisis reads the fresh copy: with it, the hearing must hear which lines changed in the court's keeping.
  - *The tell:* The clerk names the night and the book, and the master's face goes still, for the lines are hers and the halls struck her off for one that was his.
  - *The ways:* The ways are destroying the disputed copy so nothing can be proved (the code-break), the master's son, come to the hearing, telling the night he saw (his price: his father spared in public, which this may not allow), or a deal that makes mercy cost less: her name restored for her false lines named.
- **Endings.**
  - *A deal is struck* (the Wayhouse restores her name and owns the master's line; she names every false line): won by persuasion; who pays: `{master}` (struck off at last) · the guild's name (it owns a wrong). ← shown
  - *Take a side at last* (the Writers stand with the struck-off copyist against their own master, and let her three false lines go unpunished): won by persuasion; who pays: `{master}` · the commission (the court won't keep a guild that owns a false line) · `{client}` (his field stays moved, by a line nobody will answer for).
  - *The heart wins* (the lines shown to be the clerk's; the court takes her): won by law; who pays: the clerk (held for false copying) · the heart (her friend held on her word).
- **A month on (A deal is struck).** Writes to her friend again, her want met / the court keeps the halls, warily, and the player's stain is lifted with the master's line / her name on the roll, and no hall will seat her.
