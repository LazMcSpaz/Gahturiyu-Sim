# Review v7e-B: Writers samples, seeds 7601–7608 (spines draft 5)

Reviewer B, working alone. I read `samples-v7e.md`, `rolls-7601-7608.txt`, the head of `spines.md` (rules, placing, rendering) and the eight dealt spines in full or in their load-bearing fields, `map-D.json`, `world-factions.md` and `leaders.md`. Distances below are map units (about a day each).

Sources of a break: **spine** (the spine's text or slot table), **roll-slot** (what the roller put in a slot), **placement** (where the placer put a card or site), **write-up** (how the sample renders the spine).

---

## The strongest case against a high score

Every story sits on the same 13-card frame, and every card is a single sentence of 60 to 150 words. The **Held** lines are the worst: they are legal briefs that a player would have to read twice. The frame is new each draft, but the same kinds of break keep turning up:

- **The premise is missing.** In several stories the scheme's key fact appears only in the spine's field 2 and never on a card. Story 2 never says what the roll says, or why the Holy House would burn it. Story 6 never explains the "charter's trench" that the whole crisis rests on. Story 8 never says what the ford-mill debt is, or why it gives the steward grounds to object.
- **The checks miss some slots.** The lordship and barred-leader checks pass while the samples break them. Story 8's lord_a is Kotqot, whom W1-A bars by name. Story 8's "border" mill stands 0.76 from a third lord's seat. Story 6's Free Lord seizes papers in the Binders' head's town.
- **The world gets thin across the set.** Read as eight playthroughs in a row, the set uses:
  - Southroad in four stories, Carter's Rest in four and the Ninth Ring in four;
  - the same Chainmarket pawn house for back-to-back reveals;
  - someone about to burn a document and the player copying it in six stories;
  - "Burn your copy" as a rolled ending in five, and a destroy-a-text bend in four;
  - the leader in five stories (three reveals, one enemy, one wounded).
- **The hearts come in near-pairs by type:**
  - two carters (stories 1 and 5) saving money for a child;
  - two old clerks (4 and 8) who keep a hall's book;
  - two reading masters (2 and 7) with a want about one great reading.

Creativity is real. The coherence and variety are not yet at hand-written level.

## Per story

### 1 · The Wall Through the Spring (W8-A): coherence 4 · creativity 4 · pull 4
A strong core. The false line is drawn for money; the old certification is the guild's own debt; the father's cart once carried stones for the old false line; the son goes over to the Wardens. The tell (the headman sold the strip) is a good turn.

Breaks:
- **RV (spine):** the sister's daughter "keeps" her mother's private field book at her mother's own camp, and nothing says how. Field 12's C1-3 truth says the sister keeps the true journals in a locked chest she sits on, which contradicts this.
- **MOVE (spine):** the captain cries that "the Writers' maps put the green too far off". But the Writers' signed sheets already carry his false, nearer line, and he needs those maps certified. The cry attacks his own instrument. It needs a clause saying it targets last season's maps.
- **Held (spine / write-up):** "and he certifies from the halls' own checked sheets" reads as already done. The CR says he has come to see the line *before* he signs.
- **Placement (minor):** Ashring is 1.08 from the camp at the edge, and the meadow is "a short walk above" Ashring. A line only half a day inside the edge can't reach it, unless the edge near Ashring isn't at the camp. One clause fixes this.
- **Door plant (minor):** "the spring the lowland town drinks from too" names no town and pays off nowhere except E3's "lowland towns pay". In E3 the lords pay the levy, not the towns.

### 2 · The Last Reader (W5-B): coherence 3 · creativity 4 · pull 4
The best tell in the set: the rope sent back with a request for paper. The orphan-school sister and the want ("read one roll whole, once") carry it.

Breaks:
- **Stake content (spine):** no card says what the roll says, or why the Holy House would burn it and hold a man until he recants. The heart has read part of it, but the player never learns a line. Field 2 has only "the drafting roll of the Mount's grants, in a Writers' hand". E2's "argues about the grants" assumes a content that was never given.
- **R1 swap (spine):** the swap drops "her elder pupil carries it to the hall". The crisis giver (the elder pupil) is therefore first met *at* the crisis. The parties-first check didn't cover the swap.
- **RV Adds (spine):** the sister "adds that the leader is riding to claim the roll", with no source. The draft-4 rule requires one.
- **R2 (spine / write-up):** "through the house's land" is rendered "through the Holy House's land". The Holy House holds no territory on map D. The Elderhollow–Ninth Ring road doesn't pass the Mount.
- **Held (spine):** the player's copy of the dig day-book (R3) is not covered, and the spine calls it "the one record that the roll came out of the dig".
- **E3 (spine):** "his page is burned". The card never says who holds the first-page copy: the Holy House, whose charge rests on it, or the heart.
- **DOOR (minor):** the leader is still riding to claim a roll that was seized two cards earlier. She carries the reader-lord's letter, but no card shows how she got it.

### 3 · The Apprentice's Hand (W7-B): coherence 4 · creativity 5 · pull 5
The best story here. The teacher recreates the Wayhouse's old wrong on her own pupil to prove the first one. Three layers make it: "a hand the halls can't tell from their own", the mother turning away, and the forgiving ending. It would stand as a hand-written questline.

Breaks:
- **C1-3 (spine):** the Wayhouse's old hearing-scribe happens to be in Reefhold, about five days from Highcrossing, buying paper on the very day. It needs a reason: the teacher's list drew him, or he buys for the Reef halls.
- **R2 (spine, minor):** the exemplars are "the only proof the false lines came from the sheet". But the boy's sheets, the drying line and the stall's batch all survive.
- **RV (minor):** from a striking-off roll the master infers "who would write so". That is a leap the card doesn't support.
- **Held (write-up):** "the crisis reads the finding: with it in hand, the ally's way stands on…" is close to unreadable.
- **Month-on (minor):** "the teacher has a pupil", but she already had one.

### 4 · The Archive Nobody Copied (W6-B): coherence 4 · creativity 4 · pull 3
The leader as the enemy, with the sealed letter "dated the week before the fire" that scapegoats the heart, is a fine dark turn. The oiled stair is a fair plant.

Breaks:
- **C2-0 (spine):** the old lord's ward who won't be moved never comes back. It is an orphan card.
- **MOVE (spine):** the dismissed copyist "who asked" is never seen again.
- **E3 (spine):** "the heir the steward says the archive favoured". The archive has burned, and no card shows that the steward knows its contents.
- **Tell (placement):** the courier and his saddlebag are at Southroad at the crisis. He was last seen holding the seal at Oathford.
- **Reveal (soft):** the heart keeps the copying book and wrote to the leader twice herself. The "whose letter held them" reveal tells her nothing she didn't know. For the player it lands; for her it is flat.

### 5 · The Stalls' Oath (W4-A): coherence 4 · creativity 4 · pull 3
Good mechanics: pigeon lists, the painted-out oath line, the dowry-chest letter written through the stall. The crisis question (does the stalls' oath bind the halls?) is a real guild question.

Breaks:
- **C1-3 (placement):** the watch sergeant of Oathford asks for help "on the road out of Carter's Rest", more than three days from his town, with no reason given.
- **C1-2 (write-up / spine):** "the Market notes lead to a fence's shop in Carter's Rest" doesn't say how.
- **Heart's past (spine, soft):** it is a plot device (the chest's hiding place), not a past. The heart has less inner life than the others.
- **Held (write-up):** a tangle of four clauses. The reading of a warning given is good, but buried.

### 6 · The Last of the Four (W2-A): coherence 3 · creativity 4 · pull 5
The most human stakes in the set. The father's "sell to the halls, not the foreman" sends his son into danger, and the son ends up "the last of the four".

Breaks:
- **Premise (spine):** "the charter" (what comes out of the charter's trenches is the halls') lives only in field 2. In the sample, "the charter's bounds" and "the charter's trench" arrive unexplained, and the whole crisis turns on them.
- **DOOR a (spine / placement):** the Free Lord of Bogford's men seize the Chainmarket reading room's copies. The Chainmarket is the Binders' head's seat and court, not Bogford's lordship (rule 11). The slot table for `{lodge_hall}` names no lordship.
- **Blow reading (spine):** the seizure changes nothing. The Wayhouse's own record still goes to court, and the sergeant has just said the court takes no written oath anyway.
- **Crisis (spine):** the Free Lord's own court hears the Free Lord's own claim. No card says why it must honour a digger's oath against its master (a charter sworn before the Throne, a Lantern witness, a Wayhouse clerk present).
- **Minor:**
  - the archivist is named only from the door on; the reveal says "the lord's reader";
  - the boy's kept early roll (the tell) is not covered in Held;
  - C1-3 and the door are both Acquire *Under seal*.

### 7 · The Copy House Across the Street (W3-B): coherence 4 · creativity 4 · pull 3
A neat knower's-question reveal ("it is one house, and it hasn't opened yet"), and a good month-on: the court that cried him down sends clerks to read his concordance.

Breaks:
- **RV (spine):** the forger pawns her own stolen stock, halls' margins and all, for rent. The card reads as though the pawnbroker doesn't know what the sheets are. It needs one clause on her carelessness or her need.
- **CR (write-up):** "won't sell it back, for she means to go straight at last after this one last sale". The "last sale" is to the house of the Dusk Road, which only the tell explains.
- **Spine (minor):** the copyist inside the hall, the thief, is never seen or dealt with.
- The forger's real trade (selling each entry to the house it shames) is never stated on a card.

### 8 · The Truce Held for a Debt (W1-A): coherence 3 · creativity 5 · pull 3
The most original premise. A master keeps the peace by sending each lord the other's true debts, and the leader says "I'd have done it too". It is also the hardest to follow.

Breaks:
- **lord_a (roll-slot):** Kotqot Wall-breaker. W1-A's field 14 bars "the paranoid Free Lord [who] would trace a letter's road". That is him, and the roller let him through.
- **`{ford_mill}` (placement):** placed "on the border between the two lordships" at (6.9, 5.1). Bogford, a Free Lord's seat, is 0.76 away, while Breakwall and Southroad are each about 1.39. By rule 11 the mill is in Bogford's lordship, and the two lords don't adjoin.
- **R2 (spine):** the sorter "rides the branch's post out each market eve" to Southroad's gate, about three days each way, yet the player carries the letter. Why the master needs the player isn't said.
- **The objection (spine):** what the ford-mill debt is, and why it lets Southroad's steward stop the truce, is left to inference.
- **Tell (spine):** "the steward took the sealed original away with his objection". How he got it out of a strongroom isn't said, and no way uses it.
- **E1 vs Held (spine):** Held says "a ruling reads the roll's page and nobody's memory", yet E1 clears the debt "by the grinding", which is the widow's memory. The miller's tally (R3) might record it, but no card says so.

- **Places:** Southroad, Carter's Rest and the Ninth Ring each appear in four stories, and the Chainmarket pawn house carries two reveals in a row.
- **Burning:** a document about to burn shows up in six stories, plus five "Burn your copy" endings and four destroy-a-text bends.

## Failure patterns (count · cheapest fix)

1. **Lordship, bar and territory checks don't reach every slot** (5: S8 Kotqot, S8 mill, S6 door, S2 "Holy House's land", S5 sergeant's road). *Fix:* make field-14 bars machine tags the roller enforces. Run the rule-11 lordship check on every card where a lord's men act, the door included. Check a border site against every third seat nearer than the two named.
2. **The premise lives only in field 2** (4: S2 the roll's content, S6 the charter, S8 the debt and objection, S7 the forger's trade). *Fix:* every noun the crisis or Held rests on (charter, roll, debt) must appear on an earlier dealt card; add an `appears` check for terms as well as people. Give the reveal's **Shows** one clause for what the stake *says*.
3. **A proof or tell is left uncovered, or does nothing** (4: S2 day-book copy, S6 boy's roll, S8 original with the steward, S1 Held's tense). *Fix:* auto-tag every *Copy it before it burns* output and every tell object as `proof`, so Held must answer it. A tell that reveals a new fact must map to a way.
4. **Dangling or conveniently placed people** (6: S4 ward, S4 move copyist, S7 inside copyist, S3 scribe in Reefhold, S1 daughter with the field book, S2 the sister's source-less add). *Fix:* run `loses/appears` on every named person, and require a how-there clause for anyone met far from home.
5. **A swap or branch breaks a check the main text passes** (2: S2 swap drops the giver, S6 blow a changes nothing). *Fix:* run the parties and `needs/makes` checks on swap rows. Fail a branch whose READING doesn't change any way or Held.
6. **Readability** (all eight; worst in Held). One-sentence-per-card makes clauses carry the logic, and the logic gets lost. *Fix:* allow two or three sentences per card, or render Held as a short note under the crisis.
7. **Motif saturation** (set). *Fix:*
   - *Copy it before it burns* ≤ 3 per set;
   - destroy-a-text bends ≤ 2;
   - a pawn-house reveal once;
   - any one town on ≤ 3 stories' cards;
   - the leader on ≤ 3 stories in any role;
   - within a heart type, variants must differ in trade and want.

## Best and weakest

- **Best:** 3 *The Apprentice's Hand*, 1 *The Wall Through the Spring*, 6 *The Last of the Four* (its breaks are fixable in the spine; its heart is the set's strongest).
- **Weakest:** 8 *The Truce Held for a Debt* (most breaks, and the hardest to follow, despite the best idea), 2 *The Last Reader* (the stake is never told), 7 *The Copy House* (a muddled crisis motive and the least pull).

## Judgment

Not yet at 4+ on all three:
- **Creativity: 4.3. Passes.** Several premises (3, 8, 4) are better than most hand-written guild lines.
- **Coherence: 3.6. Doesn't pass.** No story fails to make sense, but three (2, 6, 8) rest on something no card sets up, or break the spine's own placing rules.
- **Variety: 3. Doesn't pass.** The plots vary; the towns, the motifs, the heart pairs and the leader's presence don't.

**What remains is mostly structural, but narrow.** Patterns 1–3 are holes in the checks: the roller verifies what each spine tags, and the breaks sit in what isn't tagged (bars, third seats, nouns in field 2, uncovered proofs). Closing those checks and editing W5-B, W2-A and W1-A should lift coherence to about 4. Patterns 4–5 are one-off slips, a clause each. Variety needs set-level caps (pattern 7), not new spines.
