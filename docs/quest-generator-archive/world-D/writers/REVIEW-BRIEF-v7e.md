# Review brief (Writers, draft 5)
You are an independent reviewer for Laz, who is designing a procedural quest generator for his fantasy RPG. Guild stories are built from **authored spines**: hand-written plot outlines with slots the world fills (people, places, one of two blow branches, three fixed endings), placed on the map by rules. Laz wants the Writers guild's stories right before any other guild; he asked for a reviewer checking **coherence and creativity** — he wants to be sure every story makes sense.

**Read:** `/home/claude/quests/test/world/D/writers/samples-v7e.md` (8 rolled Writers stories, one sentence per card), `spines.md` (16 spines + placing/rendering rules), `rolls-7601-7608.txt` (rolls + placement report) — all in `/home/claude/quests/test/world/D/writers/` — `/home/claude/quests/test/world/map-D.json` (1 map unit ≈ a day), canon flavour `/home/claude/quests/world-factions.md`, `leaders.md`. Do NOT read anything else under `test/world/` (no other reviews, samples, proof or logs). Judge on your own.

For each of the 8 stories: **coherence (1–5)** — does each card follow, every role fit, each actor able to do what they do, places/travel make sense, nothing resting on what was never set up? List every break and its source (spine / roll-slot / placement / write-up). **Creativity (1–5)**, **emotional pull (1–5)**.

Then: **variety (1–5)** for the set; **averages**; **failure patterns** with counts and cheapest fix; **3 best, 3 weakest**; and a **judgment**: is the Writers guild at 4+ on coherence, creativity and variety? If not, what remains — structural or one-off slips?

Anchors: 5 = would hold up as a hand-written guild questline in a good story-driven RPG; 3 = works but feels assembled; 1 = doesn't make sense. Before scoring, write the strongest case against a high score.
