# Writers guild samples, v7d (spines draft 4, rolls 7501–7508)

Eight stories as `roll_samples.py` dealt them, one sentence per card. Plants are in quotes; ∅ marks the plant that is an absence. Where a card is a premise, the family and premise follow in brackets.

## 1. The Stone That Walked
<small>(seed 7501 · W1-B · heart H2c, a woman · stake S7 · move M9 · shape 2 · blow c · endings 4, 1, 2 · main town Reefhold)</small>

The survey hall is Reefhold's reading room, and its master (the enemy) keeps the border open between two Seated House heads, the Ninth Ring's and Elderhollow's; the border village between them is added by the placer, with its parish chapel.

- **R1 · the Ninth Ring.** At the Ninth Ring's reading room a miller wants his strip measured again with the hall's chain before the sowing, against a neighbour who won't let the chain onto his side (Acquire *Take the readings*), and the hall's copy of the old survey, laid beside the player's new readings, carries the margin note "two readings for one stone, ten years apart, both in the halls' hand".
- **R2 · Reefhold (the heart's card).** At the relay loft beside Reefhold's survey hall, the loft keeper docks the heart's pay for blotted sheets (Dispute *Broken agreement*), and the heart, an apprentice letter-writer who copies the survey hall's outgoing sheets onto loft paper by number at night, tells the player she is saving her night coin to bring her mother, who still farms a strip in the border village, to live in town before winter (her want), because the village headman sent her to the halls at twelve "to learn the letters for us" (her past); the quiet clue is an absence, ∅ "sheets logged by number, with no name and no sender".
- **R3 · the border village (odd job).** The priest of the border village's chapel means to burn his tithe book, and the player copies it before it burns (Acquire *Copy it before it burns*), keeping "the tithe book's last page: which farms paid to which chapel"; in secret the survey hall's copyist (the Hand) told the priest that the halls already hold a copy and that the book would draw the lords' quarrel into his chapel, because it is the one record outside the halls of where the border ran, and its burning leaves the halls' readings the only word on it.
- **Reveal · the Ninth Ring (given by the heart; a chain).** Having carried the loft's night sheets to the Ninth Ring for its lord's steward, the heart brings the player to the lord's retired surveyor, shunned as "the man who lost the border" (Restore *Lost good name*), whose kept contrary readings show that each came through the Wall within a month of a stone he set, on loft paper, with a loft number and no name, numbers the heart knows as her own because she copied every one at night; and the heart adds, from the loft's night book (every sheet she copies is the survey hall's outgoing post, signed out by its master), that the master has been releasing old readings out of the Ninth Ring lord's court rolls, and that the halls have been steering the border. Her own number on his reading leads to "sheets logged by number, with no name and no sender"; those lead to the other hall's margin, "two readings for one stone, ten years apart"; and that leads to the one record of the border the halls never held, "the tithe book's last page", which is why it had to burn.
- **Chapter 1's move.** Before the whole survey hall, the master dismisses the copyist who asked who has been sending border readings out of the Ninth Ring lord's rolls, silencing him by naming an old fault of his.
- **C1-1 · the border by the village (a ride out).** With the halls' steering known, the player rides out to the border to watch the Ninth Ring lord's masons set out a line no survey hall sent them (Investigate *Watch them*), with the survey hall's eldest surveyor, who swore every stone he ever set, beside the player and Elderhollow's lord's surveyor watching the same masons from across the line; the masons' foreman works from "a fresh reading in the steward's hand", and Elderhollow's surveyor, who wants the line fixed because he was born on the border, will help if the halls survey his master's line too, free.
- **C1-2 · the border village.** Therefore the border village quarrels with the masons over the strip the fresh reading claims, which takes in the heart's mother's strip (Dispute *Shared resource*), while "a new stone cut and waiting on the cart" stands by.
- **C1-3 · Reefhold (the turn).** But at Reefhold the Hand holds his own chain-book of every stone and every reading sent and won't lend it (Acquire *Won't sell*), and he has shown Elderhollow's lord's steward the ally's name in it beside a Writer's, so Elderhollow's surveyor is ordered off the border: he can still swear the true line in person before a court, but can never walk it with the player again.
- **C2-0 · the border at night.** At pressure two the player goes out to the border at night to count Elderhollow's lord's men camped across the line (Threat *Go and look*).
- **Blow and door (c) · Reefhold.** The master shuts the survey hall's copy room by her own order "until the border is settled", so no chain-book or loft sheet leaves it, and the player must get the copy room's keys under the hall's rule (Acquire *Under seal*), while the eldest surveyor says he will stand against the master if a village pays for her readings.
- **Code-bending card.** The door carries one extra way that breaks the halls' rule against taking a side: tell the border village's headman, privately, which reading is the true line.
- **Crisis · at the survey hall's door, Reefhold.** One reading too many, sent to the Ninth Ring lord's steward the week his masons are at the border, moves the stone onto the village's fields; Elderhollow's lord's men pull it down, the Ninth Ring lord's men answer, and Elderhollow's lord's men set the border village on fire. The heart's mother, driven with the village to the survey hall's door because the halls' readings burned it, gives the card for her burned house, and the leader, off the road at the survey hall, judges in person (Reckoning *Schism*, the eldest surveyor as its fault line) whether the guild keeps its steering or owns it, while the master, found out before the leader, says it plainly: "the border is safer open". The player knew before the stone moved that the masons worked from a fresh reading and that the reading was the halls', but no Writer can stop a lord's masons or his neighbour's men, and the halls' rule forbids telling either lord which reading is true; if the player told the headman on the door, the village got its old people out before the fire, and the heart's mother comes to the door with her neighbours, not alone. The loft's night book shows the heart's own number against that week's sheet, for the reading that moved the stone was copied in her hand (the tell). The ways are the code-break of the halls standing before the Ninth Ring lord's court for the village's line, Elderhollow's surveyor swearing the true line for his free survey, or bringing it before the leader's letter with the eldest surveyor speaking.
- **Endings rolled.**
  - *Sides with the wrongdoer*: the player argues the master's case, that without her one lord would have had the border long ago; won by persuasion, and the heart and the village's claim pay.
  - *Open the ledgers*: every reading the master sent, and her name, are published; won by law, and the guild's name (both lords learn the halls steered them) and the master (struck off) pay.
  - *Keep the balance*: the leader's letter keeps the master, and the village is paid for; won by persuasion, and the heart (the village rebuilds on the moved line, and her mother's strip is lost) and the eldest surveyor (he leaves) pay.
- **A month on (Open the ledgers).** The heart's mother lives in town at last; the village rebuilds on the old stone; and the master keeps a tower and her readings.

## 2. Every Family Its Own File
<small>(seed 7502 · W3-A · heart H3c, a man · stake S3 · move M5 · shape 3 · blow c · endings 4, 1, 3 · main town Openharbour)</small>

Openharbour's reading room keeps a private file on every family whose letters pass through its Wall; the enemy is a Night Market forger buying her bond out of the Market, through a copyist of the hall who makes her fair copies of the files.

- **R1 · Openharbour (the heart's card).** At Openharbour's Wall, the heart, a carter who can't write and whose wife can't read, disputes with his master the price of buying the cart he drives (Dispute *Broken agreement*), and the hall reads the hire contract to both at the reading-out bench, where the Wall clerk's reading-out book lies open at his name; the heart wants to buy his own cart and stop hauling for a master (his want), and the book shows that once, years ago, he asked the Wall's clerk not to read his wife one letter, the one that says he sends money from the cart savings to a child elsewhere, and it is still in his file (his past): ∅ "one letter in the reading-out book marked 'kept back'".
- **R2 · Openharbour's Wall to the night market's barn (odd job).** The player carries a sealed "love letter" waiting at Openharbour's Wall to a sweetheart a day out of town, past the gate where the watch has begun opening sealed post (Passage *Smuggling*), and it is "a love letter sealed with the copy room's own wax"; in secret the sweetheart is the forger's runner (the Hand), and the letter is a bundle of file copies the copyist sends her through the Wall's own post under the copy room's wax so the sorters pass it unopened, so the player has carried the forger's stock to her.
- **R3 · Openharbour.** In Openharbour itself, whose letters all go through the Wall, a widow means to burn her husband's letters (Acquire *Copy it before it burns*), and as the player copies them she lets fall "four silver paid to a market woman for 'the only copy'".
- **Reveal · Openharbour (given by the ally; a chain).** At Openharbour's reading room, the Wall clerk who reads letters out to those who can't, met here first, asks the player who has been taking from the copy room at night (Investigate *Theft*), and the night porter shows what he saw with his own eyes, that he let a copyist of the hall into the copy room after the bell and watched him carry file bundles out to a woman who waits at the lane end and pays in Market notes; the Wall clerk adds, from her own reading-out book, that the halls keep a file on every family whose letters go through the Wall, and that the families she reads to have begun asking her whether a market woman's "only copy" is theirs; and the porter adds what the woman told him she was buying, her daughter's release from a Night Market bond (the syndicate's own servitude). The files are why a family would pay "four silver for 'the only copy'"; a family's file is how a market woman could know of "one letter marked 'kept back'"; and the copy room's own seal is how the bundles left by the Wall unopened, "a love letter sealed with the copy room's own wax".
- **Chapter 1's move.** The forger has it cried in Openharbour's market square that forged Writers' files on the town's families are going about, and that she can get a family its own out of circulation.
- **C1-1 · the night market's barn (a journey).** Following the cry to the market it came from, the player goes into the night market in the barn a day out to see what she sells and who brings it (Threat *Go and look*), and finds "files bound with the copy room's cord".
- **C1-2 · Openharbour's carters' yard (the turn).** But the cry bites in town: in the carters' yard, carters who bought their files, cried up by the forger's crier, cry the reading room thieves and shout down the halls' copyist who came to deny it (Dispute *Public offense*), and they take up "a collection for 'clean copies'".
- **C1-3 · the road out of Openharbour.** Therefore the shouted-down copyist, the one who lets the forger in, a copyist she has an old false line on and so has no tie to him she'd spare, fears the Market and runs, and the runner hunts him on the forger's orders because a copyist caught by the halls would lead them to her (Passage *Fugitive*); the Wall clerk, who knows every family who bought, asks the player to bring the copyist back to the hall alive so the families hear from his own mouth who sold them, and her price, for later, is the reading-out book kept from the hall master.
- **C2-0 · Openharbour.** At pressure two, at Openharbour's reading room, a carter's widow holds her family's file, bought at the booth, not knowing it is the halls' original (Acquire *Unknown worth*).
- **Blow and door (c) · Openharbour.** With the families' coin the forger buys two copyists' debts, and they must copy for her until paid, so the door is their rescue from the forger as creditor (Rescue *Bound by debt*); on it the Wall clerk, who hears every family's business at the reading-out bench, says the forger has hired the big booth for the market's last day and a cart for the night after: "a booth for the last day, a cart for the night after".
- **Code-bending card.** The door carries one extra way that destroys a text: burn one family's bought file, at their begging.
- **Crisis · at Openharbour's Wall, the morning after the booth.** On the market's last day the forger sells the town its files at the booth, the heart pays his cart savings for his own and his wife's, and she pays her bond and is gone by night with the rest of her stock to a far hall town, under a new name. At the Wall where every letter he sent went through, the heart gives the card because he paid for what the hall let out (Reckoning *Swindle*), and wants the player to go after her for the families' coin, for her stock, or to let her keep the freedom she bought. The player knew her trade from the reveal and the booth's day from the door, but the families bought willingly, no law forbids selling a family its own letters, and the one warning that would have stopped them (that the "forgeries" are the halls' own files) is the halls' shame said aloud, so the sale landed, and the crisis asks who pays for it. The cart she hired was for a heavier load than a woman and a chest, for she took the unsold files to sell to the houses they shame (the tell). The ways are destroying her stock (the code-break), the Wall clerk naming every family who bought so that they act together (her price, the book kept from the hall master), or following her to her new life and deciding there.
- **Endings rolled.**
  - *Wrongdoer ruined*: the Market is told how she paid her bond, and takes her back; won by stealth, and the forger pays.
  - *Amends made*: she repays before witnesses in her new town; won by persuasion, and the forger (poor again, and known), the copyist (named, struck off) and the heart (a season's hauling lost to the chase; his letter is not read out again) pay.
  - *Burn your copy*: her stock and the halls' family files are both burned; won by stealth, and the halls' records (generations of letters) and the families' coin (she keeps it) pay.
- **A month on (Wrongdoer ruined).** The heart's savings are gone, and his wife is won back; the town's trust is half-mended; and the forger is bonded again.

## 3. The Last of the Four
<small>(seed 7503 · W2-A · heart H6a, a man · stake S5 · move M6 · shape 1 · blow b · endings 1, 5, 4 · autumn · main town Breakwall)</small>

The enemy is the archivist of Breakwall's Free Lord, buying the early imperial survey rolls out of a dig in the lord's march (added at a ruin 0.8 from his seat) through its foreman, so the lord can claim three valleys back; four diggers who wouldn't sell sold the later rolls to the Wayhouse, and they are dying. The heart holds a farm in one of the valleys under a grant the halls keep, and can't read it.

- **R1 · the Chainmarket.** At the Chainmarket's reading room a lodging fee has been altered by the hall's clerk for himself (Investigate *The ledger doesn't add up*), and the year's copy of the Wayhouse's lodging book the player checks it against shows the dig's last entry: "three rolls from the march dig, sworn by four diggers before the eldest copyist".
- **R2 · Breakwall (the heart's card).** At the dig's Lantern contract board in Breakwall, a guard of the dig's contract camp quarrels with his chapter's board over the last pay of a digger killed in a trench collapse, which the chapter owes because the camp pays its diggers through the Lantern contract (Dispute *Broken agreement*), and the heart stands witness for the dead man's widow, a neighbour; the heart tells the player he wants to see his son take the copyists' oath and leave the dig (his want), that he never learned letters and sent the boy to dig for the halls so that he would (his past), and that when the foreman came buying he told the boy "sell to the halls, not the foreman; the halls keep our grant", and the widow's case carries the clue "two diggers dead in one season, both of the crew that sold to the halls".
- **R3 · Longwall (odd job).** At Longwall's reading room, the nearest to the dig, where its finds are catalogued, the player looks for the dig's reader, who came to lodge her finds catalogue and is gone from her room above the reading room with the ink still wet (Investigate *Disappearance*), and finds an absence, ∅ "the catalogue's empty shelf"; in secret the foreman (the Hand) took the reader and her catalogue the night she came, because the catalogue says which trench each roll came out of, and without it only the diggers can swear where the later rolls were found, and the diggers are the ones dying.
- **Reveal · Longwall (a found letter; the thing names them).** At Longwall's pawn house, a pawnbroker has bought a dead digger's tool roll in good faith from his widow (Acquire *Passed on*), and in its handle the player finds the dead man's last letter, never sent, which shows that the foreman was buying the early rolls for "the lord's reader" so that Breakwall's lord could claim three valleys back by them, that he and three others sold the later rolls to the halls instead, that two of them are dead this season, and that he was afraid of the man the foreman brought up from the lord's seat; nobody adds to it but the pawnbroker, who says only that the widow pawned the roll the week he was buried. The letter says the plants in its own words: the four of them "swore to the halls' rolls before the eldest copyist"; "two of us are dead this season, both of the crew that sold to the halls"; and the foreman asked him which trench the reader's catalogue sets his rolls against, "the only book that says where they came out of the ground", the book the player found gone from its shelf.
- **Chapter 1's move.** Breakwall's lord's men close the dig and turn its diggers off, the ruin "being his lord's by the old rolls".
- **C1-1 · the dig (a journey).** With the dig shut, the player slips into it to see what the lord's men are doing (Threat *Go and look*), and finds "the later rolls' trench, filled in and turfed over".
- **C1-2 · the diggers' hamlet.** Therefore, in the hamlet below the dig, the turned-off diggers cry the foreman's clerk a cheat before the hamlet over unpaid wages on the season's wage-roll (Dispute *Public offense*), and the heart's son talks of going over the hills: "a boy who means to run".
- **C1-3 · the Lantern camp at the dig (the turn).** But at the dig's Lantern contract camp, the foreman, now keeping the closed dig's books under the lord's seal, holds the wage-roll with every digger's mark against every trench by day (Acquire *Under seal*), and the camp's Lantern sergeant, who was paid to be elsewhere one of the nights the diggers died and hates it, offers his help for his chapter's dig contract paid in full.
- **C2-0 · the diggers' hamlet.** At pressure two, with the dig's wages gone, the turned-off diggers and the valleys' holders quarrel over the hamlet's grazing (Dispute *Shared resource*), and the lord's crier, come to publish the court's harvest sitting, takes the holders' side.
- **Blow and door (b) · Breakwall.** Because the player went into the closed dig, the lord's men arrest the player in his cells at Breakwall, and the door is the player's flight with his men in pursuit (Passage *Fugitive*); on it the heart, who has counted them, says that of the four who swore two are dead, one is bonded or gone, and only his son's mark is left, "the last of the four", and the sergeant says why that matters: a lord's court hearing a claim on ground takes no written oath about where a thing was dug, so a living digger must swear it there in person.
- **Code-bending card.** The door carries one extra way that destroys a text: rub the son's mark out of the wage-roll, so no one can trace him.
- **Crisis · at the heart's farm in the three valleys; acted at Breakwall.** The archivist's men ride the road to Breakwall's court, the court's harvest sitting is three days off (the valleys to Breakwall is 1.34, rounded up to two, plus one), and the Wayhouse has sent the later rolls down to Breakwall with one of its clerks to be shown at the hearing. At the farm where his son hides and won't go, because the boy expects to hang as a thief of his lord's ground, the heart gives the card for the boy and for his own word that sent him to the halls (Passage *Unwilling passenger*, the archivist's men pursuing): take the boy to swear and risk him, and so keep the family's land, or keep him safe and let the claim take the farm. The player knows the buying and the murders from the reveal and holds the wage-roll's marks, but the lord's court hears a claim on its own day and takes no written oath about where a thing was dug, so nothing the player holds answers the claim but a living digger at the hearing, and the crisis is that chance. The boy won't let anyone carry his pack, for he kept one early roll from the foreman's cart, which proves the buying (the tell). The ways are burning the later rolls the Wayhouse sent down, so that nothing is left to swear to (the code-break), the sergeant riding with them for his contract, or the heart riding too.
- **Endings rolled.**
  - *Delivered*: the boy swears, the later rolls stand, and the claim fails; won by law, and the son (marked by the lord's men, he can never go home) and the sergeant (his chapter loses the dig) pay.
  - *A copy in every hall*: the rolls are copied into every hall and to each valley holder, and the boy rides up to swear before the Wayhouse's eldest copyist, not the court; won by persuasion, and the valleys at Breakwall's court (lost there) and the guild (the lord's enmity) pay.
  - *Handed to the pursuer*: the boy is given up for the archivist's promise to spare him; won by bribe, and the son and the valleys pay.
- **A month on (A copy in every hall).** The boy is safe in Highcrossing, and the farm is lost in the lord's court and kept in every other; the claim wins at Breakwall's court and no other court honours it; and Breakwall's lord bars the halls from his march.

## 4. The Court's New Clerk
<small>(seed 7504 · W7-A · heart H4 (spec), a woman · stake S7 · move M5 · shape 3 · blow a · endings 2, 5, 1 · main town Southroad)</small>

Southroad's court is its Banner Lord's, a toll lord's, and sits in the same town as the reading room whose master once struck a copyist off the Wayhouse's roll; the enemy is that copyist, now the court's clerk under another name. The heart is her oldest friend from the Wayhouse's copying tables, who still copies the lord's rolls at Southroad's reading room.

- **R1 · Southroad (the heart's card).** At Southroad's reading room two heirs dispute one copied will (Dispute *Contested inheritance*), and the heart, the copyist they confide in, tells the player that her oldest friend from the Wayhouse's copying tables "never made a false line in her life", that she wants to see her friend's name back on the Wayhouse's roll (her want), and that twenty years ago she sat silent at the hearing that struck her friend off (her past), while her daughter copies at the same table; the clue is "a copyist struck off twenty years ago for one false line".
- **R2 · Southroad's court (odd job).** In the counting room of Southroad's court, the court's copying day-book disagrees with the halls' delivery book (Investigate *The ledger doesn't add up*), showing the halls' "copies entered 'late, unsealed'"; in secret the court's under-clerk (the Hand) made those entries so that the court checks every halls' copy line by line against the original, alone with its clerk, which is when she sets her false lines in.
- **R3 · Openharbour.** At Openharbour a bookbinder bought in good faith a leaf of the Wayhouse's old copyists' roll, sold off as the Wayhouse's scrap (Acquire *Passed on*), and on it the player finds an absence, ∅ "a leaf with one name cut out".
- **Reveal · the Silver Road Counting-House (given by the ally; the knower's question).** At the Silver Road Counting-House, the hall master's son, a ferryman met here first, asks the player to carry a letter to his father, to whom he hasn't spoken since he was a boy (Restore *Estrangement*), and tells why from what he saw with his own eyes: on the night of the false line twenty years ago he saw his father sign out the copying book and carry it home, and the line she was struck off for was a debt, so the line was his father's; he adds, from his trade, which takes him past the court every day, that the court's new clerk, who copies left-handed, is the copyist his father struck off, going under a Ṭaḍoro road-name, and that her false lines follow from the court's "late, unsealed" copies, which the player has seen. The son asks the player three things: what a copyist "struck off twenty years ago for one false line" does with her hand now, why a court would enter the halls' copies "late, unsealed", and whose name was cut from the Wayhouse's leaf, "a leaf with one name cut out".
- **Chapter 1's move.** Under her court name, the clerk has it cried at Southroad's court that the Writers' copies of its rolls are false, and the lord's commissioner orders every copy checked.
- **C1-1 · the tenants' village (a journey).** Out at a village of the court's own tenants (added in Southroad's lordship), two tenants quarrel over a field that the court's checked copy of the rolls now gives to one of them, by a line the halls' copy never had (Dispute *Shared resource*), and the other tenant, whose lease rests on the halls' copy, shows the player "a field moved by one line".
- **C1-2 · the court's gate (the turn).** But the move bites: the commissioner orders every copy checked and lets no Writer into the check room, so the player must smuggle into the court, past the under-clerk keeping the gate, a fresh copy made at Southroad's reading room from the hall's own master roll, which never went through the check room, to lay beside the sealed copies and show which lines changed in the court's keeping (Passage *Smuggling*).
- **C1-3 · the court's check room.** Therefore, with the fresh copy showing lines changed in the court's keeping, the player watches the check room after the bell (Investigate *Watch them*) and sees "a lamp in the check room after the bell" and "a court clerk who copies left-handed", as the heart's friend did; the hall master's son, who wants his father's wrong owned, asks the player to keep his father from a public striking-off, and that is his price.
- **C2-0 · the tenants' village.** At pressure two the tenant holds the halls' first copy of his lease, made before any check, not knowing it is the one copy of his entry the court clerk never touched: "a lease copy made before the check" (Acquire *Unknown worth*).
- **Blow and door (a) · Southroad.** Because the player smuggled the fresh copy in, the clerk's court name tells it at the halls and the player is stripped of rank, the stain "carries copies into a court by night" (Restore *Lost good name*); on the door the heart finds on the reading room's shelves the hall's copy of that year's Wayhouse copying book, which shows the master's book signed out that night in his own hand: "the night the master signed out the book".
- **Code-bending card.** The door carries one extra way that destroys a text: scrape one of her false lines off a halls' copy.
- **Crisis · at Southroad's court.** The commissioner lays the halls' copy beside the court's original, three false lines are found, and the court clerk, in her own name at last, accuses the heart, whose copy it is, with the commission riding on the hearing. The tenant, who has come to Southroad's reading room to ask the hall for an advocate because his lease and field rest on that copy, gives the card (Dispute *Public offense*, the commissioner as arbiter): save the halls' name by sinking the heart's friend, or tell the old wrong and risk the commission. Stripped of rank, the player stands as the tenant's advocate, as any party before a court may name one, and speaks for him and the heart, not for the hall. The player knows the court clerk is the struck-off copyist and holds the fresh copy, but the commissioner's comparing is lawful and public, and the halls can't accuse the court's own clerk without the old wrong, which the master won't tell; with the fresh copy, though, the hearing must hear which lines changed in the court's keeping. The clerk names the night and the book, and the master's face goes still (the tell). The ways are destroying the disputed copy so nothing can be proved (the code-break), the master's son telling the night he saw (his price, his father spared in public, which this may not allow), or a deal that makes mercy cost less: her name restored for her false lines named.
- **Endings rolled.**
  - *A deal is struck*: the Wayhouse restores her name and owns the master's line, and she names every false line; won by persuasion, and the master (struck off at last) and the guild's name (it owns a wrong) pay.
  - *Take a side at last*: the Writers stand with the struck-off copyist against their own master and let her three false lines go unpunished; won by persuasion, and the master, the commission (the court won't keep a guild that owns a false line) and the tenant (his field stays moved, by a line nobody will answer for) pay.
  - *The heart wins*: the lines are shown to be the clerk's, and the court takes her; won by law, and the clerk (held for false copying) and the heart (her friend held on her word) pay.
- **A month on (Take a side at last).** The heart stands beside her friend before the whole town; the halls lose the rolls and keep their honesty; and the friend is a Writer again, with nothing left to revenge, and three false lines nobody made her answer for.

## 5. Who Is Poor
<small>(seed 7505 · W4-B · heart H5b, a man (the son) · stake S2 · move M9 · shape 2 · blow a · endings 5, 3, 1 · main town the Mount)</small>

The enemy is the clerk of the Ninth Ring's reading room, who runs its district's letter-writers and sells moneylenders the names in the begging letters they write.
