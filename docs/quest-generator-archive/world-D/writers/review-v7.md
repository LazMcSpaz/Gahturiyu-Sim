# Review of the Writers' v7 samples (independent reviewer, 2026-10-27)

Judged from `samples-v7.md`, `spines.md`, `map-D.json` (1 unit ≈ a day), and the Writers' canon in `world-factions.md` and `leaders.md`. Nothing else under `test/world/` was read.

Score anchors: 5 would hold up as a hand-written guild questline in a good story-driven RPG; 3 works but feels assembled; 1 doesn't make sense. Each break is tagged **[spine]** (the plot outline), **[roll]** (a flavour or slot fill), or **[map]** (placement).

## The case against a high score (written first)

- Every one of the 8 stories has at least one card placed days from where its people live: a court's own under-clerk 8 days from the court, a dig's letter-writer 5 days from the dig, a lord's steward's clerk 5 days from the seat. The placer treats the seven large towns as interchangeable, and Reefhold, the far-west town with no lord near it, is drawn in 6 of 8 stories.
- The same villain twice: the Free Lord of Breakwall's archivist is the enemy of stories 3 and 6, both claiming "three valleys", and Breakwall's court sits in 3, 6 and 8. The leader herself is the enemy in 4 and 8. Half the hearts are an old woman clerk at a hall desk (1, 3, 4, 7).
- The prose is telegraphese. "At the Wayhouse three false lines are found, and the clerk accuses her friend" can be read three ways. A player would get the footnote, not the story. Most of the emotional load (the heart's past, the crisis's hidden truth) sits in spine fields that never reach the page: story 5's dowry-chest letter, story 8's family line in the margin grants, story 1's clerk having copied the debt herself as a girl.
- Several shown endings cost the heart for a reason the text never set up (story 1's clerk "swears it against her master" out of nowhere; story 8's player simply keeps the roll).

With that said, the *schemes* are good, and most breaks are not the spines' fault.

## The eight stories

### 1 · The Truce Held for a Debt (W1-A) — Coherence 3 · Creativity 4 · Pull 3

A hall master has fed two feuding lords each other's true debts for twenty years and now stalls their truce by lodging one last debt the night before it binds. Good premise: a peace killed by paperwork, by someone who believes balance is the guild's duty.

Breaks:
- **[roll]** The spine needs the reading room to be "the hall *between* lord_a's and lord_b's lands… a large town whose market both use". The roll put it in Southroad, lord_a's own seat. Oathford's lord paying a hall inside his rival's town to copy his rolls, and that hall posing as neutral, undercuts the whole scheme.
- **[map]** The odd job (carry the "will" into Oathford's lordship) is given at Reefhold, 3.7 days from Oathford and 4.8 from Southroad, when the letter originates in Southroad's own Wall branch 1.4 days from Oathford. The guild's post would never route it there.
- **[map]** Oathford's old steward and the ford's miller are both met at Carter's Rest, 2.2 days from Southroad and further from Oathford. Possible, unmotivated.
- **[roll]** The ally is "the Oathford lord's sister's maid, who farms both banks". A lady's maid who farms a border ford is a flavour pick that doesn't fit its role (flavour b allows "anyone of lord_b's household who farms both banks"; the list needs pruning to people who plausibly farm).
- **[spine]** The master of Southroad holds the truce "in the strongroom", which is the Wayhouse's in Highcrossing. She must send it there with a hold; the text reads as if she holds it herself.
- **[spine, minor]** The ending shown ("a copy in every hall") costs the heart because "she swears it against her master", but nothing in the story has her take a stand; her past (she copied the debt line herself) would carry this and is never used.

Creativity: the blank sender column, "a hall seal no hall cut", the debt's flour barred from both markets. Pull: the stakes are two lords' war or peace, which is abstract; the heart's forty years with the rolls is told, not felt.

### 2 · Every Family Its Own File (W3-A) — Coherence 4 · Creativity 4 · Pull 4

A Night Market forger sells each family the halls' own file on them, as "a forgery going about", to buy the bond she owes for her brother's freedom; the brother is the copyist who lets her in. The con is tight and the hidden side (the halls keep every family's letters) is the Writers' canon flaw made concrete.

Breaks:
- **[map]** The runner receives her stock at Longwall, 4.1 days from Carter's Rest, then must carry it back to a barn "a day out" of Carter's Rest. The spine doesn't bound hall_2's distance; it should.
- **[map]** The knower of the reveal is Carter's Rest's own night porter, but the reveal happens at the Chainmarket, 2.1 days away.
- **[roll, minor]** With the copyist as her brother (flavour a), C1-3 has her own runner hunting the brother she freed. Not impossible (the Market's muscle, not hers), but the text should say so.
- **[spine, minor]** The kept-back letter is read to his wife in chapter 2 (blow a) and the "amends" ending costs him its being "read out among the papers" again: the same wound twice.

Creativity: people who can't write paying to buy back their own words; a love letter as thick as a ledger. Pull: the kept-back letter about his brother's debt, the cart savings spent on the file, "has his savings back, and still no cart", the forger freed and poor. Strong.

### 3 · The Last of the Four (W2-A) — Coherence 3 · Creativity 3 · Pull 4

A Free Lord's archivist buys early survey rolls to "restore" a claim to three valleys and has the diggers who sold the later rolls to the halls killed in staged falls; the heart's grandson is the last who can swear where they came out of the ground.

Breaks:
- **[map]** The heart (the grandmother) is at Reefhold, 7.5 days from Breakwall, where her grandson digs and the court sits. She "taught him his letters by the dig's lamp" from across the entire island.
- **[map]** The lodging line ("three rolls from the dig, sworn by four diggers") is shown at Reefhold; lodgings are at the Wayhouse. The spine allows R1 to be the Wayhouse; the roller should prefer it when the plant is a lodging.
- **[spine]** Under a *hidden hand* enemy, blow a has "the lord's men wound the leader on the road", openly. An archivist who stages accidents would not have his lord's livery attack the head of a guild; the blow should be masked men, or the roll barred for hidden-hand schemes.
- **[spine, soft]** If the lodging line is "sworn by four diggers", the oath is already on record; the spine asserts a court needs living witnesses. State it once (a *Claude* presupposition) or the murders look pointless.
- **[roll]** Duplicate of story 6: same lord, same archivist, same three valleys, in one world. See the set notes.

Creativity: the catalogue's empty shelf and the turfed trench are good; the murder-for-provenance plot is otherwise stock. Pull: a grandmother whose word sent her grandson into a killer's path, the boy who won't let anyone carry his pack, and the shown ending ("told he lives, and not told where") is the bleakest and best line in the set.

### 4 · The Archive Nobody Copied (W6-B) — Coherence 3 · Creativity 4 · Pull 4

The leader has "held" a steward's thrice-made request to copy a dying lord's archive, so it burns uncopied and the halls' roll decides his succession; then her letter blames the old clerk who logged the requests.

Breaks:
- **[map]** The steward's clerk about to burn the letter-book (odd job) is at Reefhold, 4.8 days from Southroad, the seat he serves.
- **[map]** The reveal lays Southroad's copying book beside Southroad's steward's letters at the Ninth Ring, 3.9 days away.
- **[spine]** The heart is cried down for taking a side (blow a, Dispute *Public offense*) and then, in the crisis, blamed by the leader's letter (Reckoning *Betrayal*): two public shamings of the same woman in a row. The spine's blow a should be barred here or the crisis should land on someone else when a is dealt.
- **[spine, minor]** Timing of the leader's letter: the archive burns the night the lord dies, and her letter blaming the clerk is read as if already written. One line ("a letter that had waited, sealed, for this") would make it chilling instead of fuzzy.

Creativity: the lamp-lit stair with oil in every niche, two heirs' locksmiths at one door, the leader's "cost": nobody set the fire. Pull: "copies the archive's charred remains: her want, smaller" is the best closing line in the set.

### 5 · The Stalls' Oath (W4-A) — Coherence 2 · Creativity 4 · Pull 3

The clerk who runs the letter-writers sells fences a list of whose house will stand empty for the fair; fair night, every listed yard is robbed, the heart's dowry chest with them; the question is whether the stalls' oath binds the halls behind them.

Breaks:
- **[map]** The heart is a Saltmarket carter writing through Saltmarket's stall, but his first card (the wage dispute) is at Longwall, 6.0 days away. His nearest hall is Openharbour, 1.4 days.
- **[map]** The fence town is the Ninth Ring (4.1 from Saltmarket), while **Saltmarket itself has "Night Market fence's shop" in its presences list**. The placer rolled past the map's own answer.
- **[map]** The fair is at Oathford, 2.9 days overland from Saltmarket with no road between them; the carter "hauls for the fair" from a town that trades by sea.
- **[roll]** The enemy's own hall is never named. The oath board hangs "over the Wall branch" at Longwall, but who the clerk is and where he sits is left to inference.
- **[roll]** Flavour (a) 2 makes the robbery a carters' yard of laid-up goods, but the heart's dowry chest is, by his past, hidden in his house. The text moves the chest into the yard without saying so.
- **[roll, minor]** The letter that told his daughter where the chest is hidden (his past, and the point of the whole scheme) never appears.

Creativity: letter-writers who read what they write, the oath's last line painted out ten years ago, the founding book's wording. Pull: a dowry and a daughter in service are real, but the carter has three words on the page.

### 6 · The Seal With a Plain Rim (W2-B) — Coherence 2 · Creativity 3 · Pull 3

A Free Lord's archivist buys a dig's whole season under a Wayhouse seal with a plain rim, through bills written by the reading master who recruited the player, so that the halls are named as sellers when he claims three valleys.

Breaks:
- **[map]** The heart's stall is "placed in Reefhold's market", 5.1 days from the Old Road dig whose thumb-signing diggers she writes bills for.
- **[map]** The Old Road dig stands in Crown Crossing, 1.7 days from Highcrossing, yet the bought carts turn off "at the toll" of the Free Lord of Breakwall, 4.2 days away. His toll can't sit on that road.
- **[map]** The plant "the drawn seal in her margin" (her Reefhold day-book) is turned up while freeing the keys of the Chainmarket reading room, 8.0 days from Reefhold.
- **[spine]** The summons is served at the Wayhouse, but the heart quit the halls and keeps a stall elsewhere; nothing puts her in Highcrossing at dawn. The spine never sets up where she is when the crisis falls.
- **[roll]** Duplicate of story 3 (same lord, same archivist, same claim). Two of the eight Writers stories in one world are "Breakwall's archivist wants three valleys".

Creativity: the plain rim versus the halls' knotted rim is a lovely detail; the woman who left when the halls stopped copying for the poor, and took the fee anyway, is a good heart. Everything else is the W2-A plot with the witness running instead of hiding. Pull: her want (to be taken back) and the shown ending (taken back; the lord shuts the halls) land, but her guilt (she guessed the seal was false) never reaches the page.

### 7 · The Court's New Clerk (W7-A) — Coherence 3 · Creativity 4 · Pull 4

A copyist struck off twenty years ago for a false line that was really the hall master's now clerks for the court under another name, setting false lines into the halls' copies; the heart is her oldest friend, who sat silent at the hearing.

Breaks:
- **[map]** The odd job (the court's day-book, altered by the court's own under-clerk) is at Reefhold, 8.0 days from the Chainmarket court. The Hand is that court's under-clerk; the card is unplaceable there.
- **[map]** The reveal (the Chainmarket master's estranged son) is also at Reefhold.
- **[roll]** "The market lord's court at the Chainmarket": the Chainmarket is owned by the Binders, in Free Lords land, and the map gives it no lord. The roll invents one and ignores the Binders entirely; a Binder-run court would have been richer and is what the map offers.
- **[spine, soft]** The commissioner brings the court's original to the Wayhouse, 3.7 days from his own court, to compare copies. The spine pins the payload to the Wayhouse regardless of distance (the authors' own "known soft spot").
- **[render]** "The clerk accuses her friend" is ambiguous: the enemy (now the court clerk) accuses the heart (her friend), whose copy it is.

Creativity: a clerk who copies left-handed, a lamp after the bell, "a leaf with one name cut out", the hall master's own line. Pull: the best personal core in the set: twenty years of silence, a friend's revenge that lands on the one who loved her, and "tells the old wrong anyway, to nobody who listens".

### 8 · The Copies Sent for Mending (W6-A) — Coherence 3 · Creativity 4 · Pull 3

The leader has every outside copy of the Marches' land rolls sent "for mending" and lost into a friend's attic, never burned ("she keeps the code"), so the Wayhouse's roll is the only one and she decides who owns the land; the heart is an apprentice who copies the old hand by shape and wants to read it.

Breaks:
- **[map / roll]** "A lord's archivist won't lend his land rolls" at Openharbour, an Open Hand great port with no lords, 4.6 days from the Marches whose rolls these are.
- **[map]** The mending book and the fallen roll-case (dropped from the courier's mule between the Marches and Highcrossing) are at Reefhold, 8.0 days west of the Chainmarket.
- **[roll]** Where the heart works drifts: she disputes her pay at the Chainmarket, but the spine has her copying the *Wayhouse's* rolls by night, and the crisis needs her and her full copy inside the Wayhouse.
- **[spine, minor]** The W6 reveal is given by hall_3's "eldest reader" and the crisis by the Wayhouse's "eldest copyist": two eldests in one story; a reader will take them for one man.

Creativity: the leader as a hoarder who won't break the letter of the code; the paper carters never searched; the mending book's one ink for a year. Pull: the foundling from the valley that loses is the right heart, and "two people now hold the land rolls, and one of them is the player" is a striking last line, but her own family's line in the margin grants (the spine's hidden truth) never surfaces, so the shown ending is cold rather than cruel.

### Variety: what repeats

- **Enemy and lord.** Breakwall's archivist is the enemy twice (3, 6), both claiming three valleys; Breakwall's court appears three times (3, 6, 8). The leader is the enemy twice (4, 8). Three "within" clerks (1, 5, 7). The 8 feel like five or six stories.
- **Heart.** An old woman clerk at a hall desk in 4 of 8 (1, 3, 4, 7). A carter who can't write in 2 (2, 5).
- **Places.** Reefhold in 6 of 8, Carter's Rest 4, Chainmarket 3, Southroad 3, Ninth Ring 3; Openharbour once. Reefhold is the farthest town from every lord on the map, which is why it causes most of the travel breaks.
- **Beats.** "Carry a sealed thing past a toll" is the odd job in 3 (1, 2, 6). "Lesser code-break: burn X" in 5. "X will help, for a price" in 4. The eldest copyist is the giver in 4 (1, 4, 6, 8). Every crisis closes "X asks: A, or B?"

### The eight unrolled spines

The set is strong. W5-A (letters to a mother lost in the green, nine years uncollected, in the section about to burn) is the best heart in all sixteen; W5-B (a reader who won't be rescued from saying what she read), W7-B (a teacher who loved and used her pupil), W3-B (a half-blind keeper against a cheaper hand; hire the thief?), W4-B (a son's begging letters sold while he pays his lost mother's debt) and W8-B are all distinct and playable. Flags:

- **W2-B is a pale twin of W2-A**: same lord, same valleys, same claim; only the mechanism differs. The weakest spine of the sixteen. Give it a different lord *by rule*, or a different payload (the halls named as sellers is the good part; the valleys are not needed).
- **H5 (the son of a surveyor lost in the green) is the heart of three spines** (W4-B, W5-A, W8-B). Each is good alone; in one world they are the same boy. Cap the heart type at one per world set.
- **"The friend who sat silent at the striking-off"** is the heart of W7-A and the ally of W7-B. Same person. If both spines can be dealt in one world, the generator must make her one character or bar the pair.
- **W1-A** rests on two invented rules (the truce binds only once lodged; an objected lodging is held) and a geography the roller can't deliver (a hall *between* two lords). It is the spine most at the mercy of the placer.
- The authors' own soft spot is real: 13 of 16 open with *The ledger doesn't add up*. Three of the rolled eight did, which read fine; it would not at sixteen.

### Best three

1. **2 · Every Family Its Own File.** The tightest con, the clearest heart, the sharpest cost ("still no cart").
2. **7 · The Court's New Clerk.** The strongest human core and the best last line; only the map hurts it.
3. **4 · The Archive Nobody Copied.** The leader as enemy, done by inaction; "her want, smaller".

### Weakest three

1. **6 · The Seal With a Plain Rim.** Four map breaks and a twin of story 3; the heart is good and never gets to be.
2. **5 · The Stalls' Oath.** A good question ("does the oath bind the halls?") on an unplaceable set of towns, with the enemy's seat never named and the map's own fence's shop passed over.
3. **1 · The Truce Held for a Debt.** The roll violated the spine's one geographic premise (a hall *between*), and the odd job's post runs 4 days the wrong way. (Story 3 is a near fourth: its placement is the worst in the set, but it reads better.)

### Judgment

**Creativity is already there** (3.75, with the unrolled spines at least as strong), and emotional pull is close (3.5), held back mostly by authored material that never reaches the page. **Coherence (2.9) and variety (3) are not spine problems.** Six of the seven failure patterns are placement, slot and render rules; every story's *plot* holds when you ignore where the roller put its people. The spines themselves need two fixes (W2-B's twin-ness; the double-shaming path in W6-B) and one guard (W1-A's "between" hall must be enforced or the spine marked unplaceable on this map).

What stands between this and 4+ on all three: (a) a story centre with a distance bound, and hall picks weighted by it; (b) once-per-world caps on kit enemy, lord and heart type, with more named lords on the map; (c) slot checks against place kind and presences; (d) a render pass that prints the heart's past and the crisis's tell, in full sentences. With (a) and (b) alone I would expect coherence near 4 and variety at 4; (c) and (d) take emotional pull to 4. None of it requires rewriting the sixteen spines.
