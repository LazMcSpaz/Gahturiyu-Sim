# Writers sample stories, v7 (rolled from the authored spines)

Eight Writers stories in World D, one per seed (7201–7208), each rolled with `random.Random(seed)` by `roll_samples.py` in this folder: spine (no spine twice), chapter-2 blow, flavour variant, heart, slot fills from `map-D.json`, three endings from the pool, and the one shown. People are roles. "The player" is the guild member playing; "the leader" is the Writers' head, always on the road. In each story's list of rolled endings, the brackets name who pays.

---

## 1 · The Truce Held for a Debt

At Southroad's reading room the old clerk who has kept its loan rolls forty years catches a grain buyer's altered page; she wants an apprentice with a steady hand. Stewards' letters there show "the blank sender column". At Reefhold the odd job: carry a sealed "will", addressed to a steward, past Oathford's toll under "a hall seal no hall cut". At Carter's Rest a miller speaks of "the ford mill's old debt"; the leader, off the road, reads an old steward's unsigned letters: for twenty years Southroad's master has fed each lord the other's true debts to keep the balance, and a truce is coming.

Chapter 1: the master takes her Wall branch's sorting in hand. Tenants break the mill race; the player watches a back-stair satchel; the truce reply goes missing, and the Oathford lord's sister's maid, who farms both banks, will help if her name stays off every copy.

Chapter 2: the player is stripped of rank for "reading others' letters". Clearing it turns up the miller's widow's "seven years' free grinding", and a lesser code-break: copy the ford-mill page without the debt line.

Crisis: the debt reaches Oathford's steward the night before the lodging day; he objects, and the master holds the sealed truce in the strongroom. The eldest copyist, who keeps its key-book, asks: let the hold stand, or force the truce through? Rolled endings: lodged on the ruling (the maid), a copy in every hall (the old clerk), trade what we know (a bystander).

**A month on: *A copy in every hall***

- The old clerk copies at the Wayhouse, her rolls in other hands.
- The truce is posted in every hall.
- The master keeps her hall and her counsel.

## 2 · Every Family Its Own File

At Carter's Rest the Wall's clerk reads aloud for a carter who can't write and wants his own cart, and to his unlettered wife. Her book shows "one letter marked 'kept back'". At Longwall the odd job: carry a ledger-thick "love letter", "sealed with the copy room's own wax", to a forger's runner. At the Chainmarket a widow burning her husband's letters paid "four silver for 'the only copy'"; there the leader, off the road, learns who robs Carter's Rest's copy room. A Night Market forger, let in by her copyist brother, sells families their own files to pay her bond, owed for his freedom; and the halls keep files on every family.

Chapter 1: she has forged files cried in the market. A night-market barn holds files in copy-room cord; carters cry the reading room thieves; the brother flees the runner. The Wall clerk offers every buyer's name.

Chapter 2: the forger has the carter's kept-back letter read to his wife. Mending them turns up "a booth for the last day, a cart for the night after", and a lesser code-break: burn one family's file at their begging.

Crisis: the town buys its files at the booth, the carter with his cart savings, and she is gone. He asks the Wayhouse: chase her coin, her stock, or let her go? Rolled endings: amends made (the forger, her brother, the carter), open the ledgers (the guild's name, the brother), sides with the wrongdoer (the carter, the families).

**A month on: *Amends made***

- The carter has his savings back, and still no cart.
- The families hold their coin and distrust the halls.
- The forger is a copyist for hire under her own name.

## 3 · The Last of the Four

At Reefhold an old clerk who knows the survey rolls by heart unpicks an altered fee. She wants her grandson off the dig and sworn a copyist, and shows a lodging line: "three rolls from the dig, sworn by four diggers". At Carter's Rest a Lantern guard disputes a dead digger's pay: "two diggers dead in one season". At Southroad the odd job: the dig's reader is gone, leaving "the catalogue's empty shelf"; the foreman took her. At Carter's Rest the leader, off the road, reads a dead digger's last letter: the Free Lord of Breakwall's archivist buys early survey rolls for a "restored" claim to three valleys, and his blade kills the diggers who sold the later rolls to the halls, in falls from the ruin's stair.

Chapter 1: the lord's men close the dig. The later rolls' trench is turfed over; the grandson means to run; the foreman holds the wage-roll under seal; the Lantern sergeant will help, for a price.

Chapter 2: the lord's men wound the leader on the road. Getting her away, the player learns the grandson is "the last of the four", and is offered a lesser code-break: rub his mark from the wage-roll.

Crisis: hidden at the Wayhouse, the boy won't ride to Breakwall's court, expecting to hang. His grandmother asks: take him to swear, or keep him safe? Rolled endings: delivered (the boy, the sergeant), burn your copy (the valleys, the old clerk), handed to the pursuer (the boy, the valleys).

**A month on: *Handed to the pursuer***

- The old clerk is told her grandson lives, and is not told where.
- The claim stands.
- The archivist keeps his promise as far as suits him.

## 4 · The Archive Nobody Copied

At Southroad's reading room the old clerk who keeps its copying book traces an altered will. She longs to copy an archive whole; her book shows the Southroad lord's commission "held, by letter, three seasons running". At Reefhold the odd job: a steward's clerk, urged by the leader's courier, would burn a letter-book with "three letters to the halls in a year". At the Ninth Ring heirs fight over "a will the halls never got round to copying"; the eldest reader sets the copying book beside the steward's letters. The leader held the commission so the archive would go uncopied, leaving the halls to choose the heir.

Chapter 1: the hall master dismisses the copyist who asked. The archive stair has oil in every niche; two heirs' men keep one door; the courier withholds the commission's seal; the old lord's ward offers help.

Chapter 2: the ward won't be moved. The leader's letter cries the clerk down as backing an heir. Answering the offended heir turns up "the page in her own hand", and a lesser code-break: tell one heir privately what the roll says.

Crisis: the old lord dies, the heirs' men fight, a lamp falls, the archive burns, and the leader's letter blames the clerk. The eldest copyist asks: her name or the leader's? Rolled endings, plus the seat ending: open the ledgers (the leader, the split guild), amends made (the leader, the steward), take a side at last (neutrality, the other heir's tenants).

**A month on: *Amends made***

- The old clerk copies the archive's charred remains: her want, smaller.
- The succession is argued from scraps.
- The leader walks on, watched.

## 5 · The Stalls' Oath

At Longwall a carter who can't write disputes a season's pay, saving a dowry to bring his daughter home, writing her through Saltmarket's stall. Over the Wall branch hangs "the oath board, its last line painted out". At Carter's Rest the odd job: find the keeper of "a loft whose pigeons went unfed", paid off by the clerk's list-writer. At the Ninth Ring a merchant "robbed the week his letter said he'd be away" would burn his letter-book. At Carter's Rest the leader, off the road, brings the keeper home: the hall clerk who runs the letter-writers sells fences a list of whose goods, by their letters, will stand empty.

Chapter 1: he pays a sacked letter-writer to read a letter aloud, drunk, in a market. The player clears her ("paid in Market notes"), finds a list in a stall's hand in a Ninth Ring fence's pigeon tube, and Oathford's watch sergeant, his house listed, wants a copy.

Chapter 2: the player scouts Oathford's streets. Riding to read the stalls' books, the leader falls, her girth cut; finding the cutter turns up "painted out at the clerk's order", and a lesser code-break: privately tell one family who sold their letter.

Crisis: fair night robs every listed carters' yard, the dowry chest with them. The carter asks: does the stalls' oath bind the halls? Rolled endings: a deal is struck (the clerk's purse, the daughter), the heart wins (the clerk, the guild's quiet), the clerk wins (the carter, every client).

**A month on: *The heart wins***

- The dowry is half-repaid from the clerk's takings.
- The stalls' board is repainted, whole.
- The clerk sells his list one last time, to anyone.

## 6 · The Seal With a Plain Rim

At Reefhold the reading master who recruited the player, who quit the halls when they stopped copying for the poor, keeps a stall. She witnesses a dispute over one of her "bills of sale for men who sign with a thumb"; the halls are buying all season, and she'd go back if asked. At the Ninth Ring the odd job: carry a packet past the Free Lord of Breakwall's toll under "a Wayhouse seal with a plain rim". At the Wayhouse a hidden fee shows "the lodging book's empty line for the Old Road dig". At the Ninth Ring a dead digger's sons dispute a price "paid by the Wayhouse"; the leader says it bought nothing. The lord's archivist buys under a false seal, to claim three valleys before Highcrossing's magistrate.

Chapter 1: his buyer buys the dig's season, "with the Wayhouse's thanks". Carts turn off at the toll; the headman cries the halls liars; the toll keeper holds their cart; the headman's daughter will help, for a price.

Chapter 2: her trade dies. The lord's men seize the Chainmarket reading room; freeing its keys turns up "the drawn seal in her margin", and a lesser code-break: burn the headman's bill.

Crisis: the magistrate summons her as the seal's forger. The eldest copyist, at whose door it's served, asks: run to an Open Hand haven, or stand and be held? Rolled endings: take a side at last (the lord's halls), handed to the pursuer (she, the valleys), delivered (she).

**A month on: *Take a side at last***

- The reading master is taken back onto the roll.
- The claim is thrown out.
- The lord shuts the halls in his march.

## 7 · The Court's New Clerk

At the Chainmarket a court-roll copyist advises in a will quarrel. She wants her oldest friend, "a copyist struck off twenty years ago for one false line", back on the roll. At Reefhold the odd job: a court day-book enters the halls' sealed deliveries "late, unsealed"; the under-clerk did it. A Highcrossing binder bought "a leaf with one name cut out". At Reefhold the leader, off the road, mends the Chainmarket master's estrangement from his son, who saw his father take the copying book. The struck-off friend, now court clerk under another name, sets false lines in the halls' copies; the line she fell for, over a boundary, was the master's.

Chapter 1: she has the halls' copies cried false at court; all are checked. The court clerk copies left-handed; a lamp burns in the check room after the bell; smuggling in a fresh copy, the player meets the master's son, who'll help if his father is spared.

Chapter 2: the player is blamed for scraping the court's original; clearing it turns up "the night the master signed out the book", and a lesser code-break: scrape one false line off a halls' copy.

Crisis: at the Wayhouse three false lines are found, and the clerk accuses her friend. A tenant whose lease rests on it asks: sink the friend, or tell the old wrong? Rolled endings: burn your copy (the halls, the tenant), take a side at last (the master, the commission), the enemy wins (the guild, the copyist).

**A month on: *The enemy wins***

- The copyist is dismissed, as her friend was.
- The court's rolls leave the halls.
- The struck-off clerk tells the old wrong anyway, to nobody who listens.

## 8 · The Copies Sent for Mending

At Openharbour a lord's archivist won't lend his land rolls: "a copy sent for mending that never came back". At the Chainmarket an apprentice, a foundling from a Breakwall valley who copies the imperial hand by shape, wanting to read it, disputes her pay for "copies made for mending, of rolls that weren't torn". At Reefhold the odd job: the mending book shows "the courier's mark against 'returned', in one ink for a year". There the eldest reader reads a fallen roll-case's mending label, addressed to no hall: the leader herself has had every other copy of the Marches' land rolls lost, so the Writers alone decide who owns the land.

Chapter 1: the Chainmarket's master takes the master copy "for mending". The lost copies lie dry and unread in a Ṭaḍoro friend's attic; the courier, due at the new moon, keeps the copy; the valley's headwoman needs a copy.

Chapter 2: the apprentice is cried down for copying the roll for the claimants; clearing her turns up "the paper carters, never searched", and a lesser code-break: privately vouch for the claimants' copy.

Crisis: the master copy is "lost"; the court sits in three days, and the leader bars copies leaving without her seal. The apprentice holds a full copy. The eldest copyist, hating it, asks: obey, or carry it out? Rolled endings (and the seat ending): kept by the player (the valley, the apprentice), a copy in every hall (the guild's hold, the courier), seized (the valley, the apprentice).

**A month on: *Kept by the player***

- The apprentice asks the player for the copy, and is refused.
- The valley loses.
- Two people now hold the land rolls, and one of them is the player.
