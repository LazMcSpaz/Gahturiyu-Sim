# Review of Writers samples v7b (seeds 7301–7308)

Independent read of `samples-v7b.md` against `spines.md` (draft 2), `rolls-7301-7308.txt`, `map-D.json`, `world-factions.md` and `leaders.md`. No earlier review or sample was read.

Scale: 5 would pass as a hand-written guild questline in a good story-driven RPG; 3 works but feels assembled; 1 doesn't make sense. Each break is tagged **spine**, **roll/slot**, **placement** or **write-up**.

Distances below are straight-line map units (≈ a day) with the road distance in brackets where the roads make it longer. Highcrossing is at (5.4, 4.1).

---

## The case against a high score (written before scoring)

1. **The clock ignores the map.** Three crises say "in three days" or "the night before" while the Wayhouse, which every crisis is pinned to, is 3.4–4.4 units (4–7 road days) from the action: story 1 (the truce sent up "the night before" from 3.4 away), story 3 (hearing in 3 days; Highcrossing to Breakwall is 2.8, 4.1 by road), story 5 (vote in 3 days; the hut is 4.4 from the Wayhouse, ~6.8 by road, through two villages that bar the cargo). A player who reads the map will see these.
2. **The Wayhouse pin taxes every far centre.** Five of eight stories (1, 2, 3, 6, 7) send an old clerk, a half-blind master, a pupil or a tenant 3.4–5 units to Highcrossing and then act 2–5 units back where they started, with none of that travel on a card. Reefhold (5.05), Openharbour (3.93), the Chainmarket (3.68) are the worst.
3. **Spine logic holes that a reader will catch:** burning a loan-roll page while the clerk who knows it by heart stands there to recite it (1); a lord's archivist who holds his own copy of the land rolls on R1 in a story whose whole payload is "the Wayhouse's roll is the last" (4); burning copied pages to void a charge of *reading aloud* (2).
4. **People who arrive unset:** the ford miller's widow (1; the miller is alive one card earlier), the uncle (8), a Seated House court that takes the heart in the shown ending (2; planted only on the undealt door a), the young copyists bound for Market debts (7; introduced on C1-3, never resolved).
5. **Variety is thinner than eight enemies suggests.** Stories 6 and 7 share the stake (S7, a court's copying commission), the blow (player stripped), the ch2 opening (court copyists laid off) and an added "record house" 0.5–0.8 out. Stories 5 and 8 are both the son of a surveyor who walked into the green. Seven of eight routine sets have *The ledger doesn't add up*; three ch2 openings are *Unwelcome cargo* ("the X, now 'unclean/green-touched/the debt's flour', are barred from…"). *Burn your copy* is rolled in five of eight and shown in two.

The case for: nothing is at 1 or 2. Every story has a clear enemy, a clear payload, a heart whose want and past reach the page, and endings that name who pays. Several ideas (selling begging letters to moneylenders; a half-blind master who keeps rolls by ear; smuggling truth out of your own guild past your own head's order; a reader who sends back the rope and asks for paper) are better than most hand-written guild quests.

## 1 · The Truce Held for a Debt (7301, W1-A)

**Coherence 3.** Breaks:
- **(spine + placement)** The master "sends the sealed truce up to the Wayhouse" *the night before the truce day*, and noon is the last hour of the lodging day; the Ninth Ring is 3.4 (≈4 by road) from Highcrossing. The hold can't reach the strongroom in time unless it was already there, which the card doesn't say.
- **(spine)** The code-break "burn the ford-mill page" so there is no debt to rule, while the heart, who has the roll by heart and "rides up to recite its line at any ruling", stands in the same strongroom. The ending even costs him "the line he copied is ash", but the line is in his head. Needs one sentence (the court takes a roll, not a recital; or the heart must be made to hold his tongue).
- **(spine)** The truce's paper trail doesn't add up: C1-2 says the truce "passed through the branch on its way up" and the heart made the door copy; C1-3 says the reply to the *draft* never came; the crisis has a sealed truce going up again with a hold. Draft, reply, sealing and lodging need an order.
- **(spine / write-up)** C1-1 and R3 have the miller alive; the door has "the ford miller's widow" with no death between. If she is the previous miller's widow (the seven years' grinding "to clear the mill" suggests it), say so.
- **(spine)** The lesser code-break (copy the ford-mill page for the steward's clerk with the debt line out) has no visible effect on the door it is attached to (the player framed).
- **(placement)** The two "lords" are a First Peoples Seated House head and a Great House Banner Lord, with Holy House land (the Mount, 0.6 from the added mill) between their seats. A truce across a faction border that "one lord swallowing the other" ended last time is a bigger event than the spine's language of tenants and a mill race, and the write-up never notices. Also a shuttle: Ninth Ring → Oathford (2.5) → Elderhollow → Oathford → mill → Ninth Ring → Oathford → mill → Ninth Ring → Highcrossing; Oathford carries three cards 2.5 out.
- **(write-up)** The leader is "not sorry" and then the master's "shield" on the door; defensible but needs a clause.
- Good: the player's own R2 carriage is what makes blow b stick; "a hall seal no hall cut"; the steward's grain under his wife's name as the bribe lever.

**Creativity 4.** Balance-keeping as a weapon against peace, and an old clerk's childhood line as the pivot, is a strong Writers idea.
**Emotional pull 3.** The heart's want (an apprentice) and past reach the page, but the stakes are two lords we never meet, and the shown ending reads as the heart losing for reasons the card itself undercuts.

## 2 · The Last Reader (7302, W5-B)

**Coherence 4.** Breaks:
- **(spine)** The charge is "reading unclean writing aloud"; the code-break burns "the few pages he copied so there is nothing to hold him for". Those pages were never shown being copied, and burning them doesn't touch the charge. Needs the charge to name the copied pages, or the copying planted on C1-2.
- **(roll / slot)** Shown ending *Handed to another*: a Seated House's court "wants the roll's words for itself". Only door a's tell (the leader carrying a Seated House letter) plants that, and door b was dealt. The ending arrives from nowhere. (It is a lovely ending: the "blind lord in a closed room" is almost certainly Tuhuḍo, the blind archivist-lord; naming him would make it land.)
- **(placement)** Reefhold → Wayhouse (5.05) → the Mount (2.4) before any way is tried; the feast is unset so no hard break, but ~7 days of unshown riding for a pupil.
- **(placement / write-up)** The spine is written for "the Vale" (Vale road, Vale hall, "the Vale hears nothing"); the roll centres it on Reefhold, Reef Coast, with the Mount's drafting house put 0.8 from a Horaro stilt town, 2.8 from the Mount. The write-up swaps "the Reef Coast" in but can't make a Holy House drafting house on the reef feel set up. Why the Mount's grants were drafted there is never asked.
- **(placement)** R2 carries "a crate of dig finds" from the Ninth Ring (2.64) when the dig is 0.8 from Reefhold; generic, but a careful player asks which dig.
- Good: "'unclean', said of a book with a Writers' hand in it" is a plant that becomes the whole story; the rope sent back with a request for paper; a Tide House speaker bending to an inquisitor fits canon (Horaro overlap with the Temple).

**Creativity 4.** **Emotional pull 4.** The sibling reconciliation, and a captive who stays to say what he read, are the strongest hearts in the set after story 5.

## 3 · The Last of the Four (7303, W2-A)

**Coherence 4.** Breaks:
- **(spine + placement)** "The hearing three days off" and the boy hidden at the Wayhouse: Highcrossing to Breakwall is 2.8 straight, 4.1 by road (Crownsgate, Longwall). The crisis as written can't be won by *Delivered*.
- **(spine)** R2: a *Lantern guard* quarrels with his chapter over a *digger's* pay. A guard arguing a labourer's wages with his own chapter is odd; one clause (the camp paid the diggers through the Lantern contract) fixes it. Breakwall has a contract board, not a chapter; "his chapter" is a stretch.
- **(write-up)** The spine's sharpest line, the father's own word "sell to the halls, not the foreman; the halls keep our grant", which is what put the boy on the killer's list, doesn't reach the heart's card. The crisis leans on it ("his own word that sent the boy to the halls") without the player having heard it.
- **(write-up, missed resonance)** The Chainmarket is the Binders' town and blow c bonds diggers for bought debts. That is the Binders' trade in the Binders' seat and nobody says so.
- Good: the catalogue as provenance, the filled trench, "a boy who means to run", the wage-roll as the thing that both proves and endangers. The shown ending (the boy handed over for a promise "kept as far as suits him") is honest and bleak.

**Creativity 3.** A land claim backed by murdered witnesses is the most conventional plot of the eight, well dressed.
**Emotional pull 4.** A father who can't read his own grant, whose son is the last mark; the choice of farm against boy is real.

## 4 · The Copies Sent for Mending (7304, W6-A)

**Coherence 3.** Breaks:
- **(spine)** R1: the Banner Lord's archivist "won't lend his master's copy of the land rolls", and the tell says he is right to keep it. So the lord holds a copy. The payload is "the Wayhouse's roll is the last" and the crisis has the court "ask the Writers for the roll". Either the archivist's copy must be gone (that is what "sent for mending and never came back" could mean, but the card says he still has one and won't lend it) or it lacks the margin grants; the spine must pick.
- **(spine)** C1-1 "find where the lost copies go": the only lead is a roll-case fallen "on the road out". Enough for a hunch, not for a tower 1.8 away in Open Hand land.
- **(write-up)** C1-2 "waits for the courier due at the new moon", C1-3 "the courier has the master copy": the new moon came between cards; say so.
- **(write-up)** *A copy in every hall* costs "the guild's hold on land, since the rolls are now anyone's to buy": copies in Writers' halls are not "anyone's".
- Good: the leader as enemy fits canon exactly (puppeteer, keeps the balance, acts only by letter, never at the Wayhouse); the eldest reading an order he hates; the deaf caretaker's dry shelves; the courier's one ink for a year; the hall master "who'd sooner copy than send" planting the ending. Compact map (everything within 1.8).

**Creativity 4.** Smuggling the truth out of your own headquarters against your own head is the freshest guild premise here.
**Emotional pull 3.** The heart's want (to read what he copies) is good but the hidden truth that gives it teeth (his own family's line is in the margin grants) never surfaces on any card; the player never learns why he asks to carry it.

## 5 · The Mother's Journal (7305, W8-B)

**Coherence 3.** Breaks:
- **(spine + placement)** "The lords vote at the Wayhouse in three days"; the ways run from the hut (1.79, 1.63) to the Wayhouse door: 4.4 straight, ~6.8 by road, through the Green Wall, the Ninth Ring, the Mount and Oathford, past two villages that bar the cargo. Not winnable as written. The door's "month's end" and the crisis's "three days" also disagree in feel.
- **(spine)** "A journal in surveyors' shorthand nobody at the hall can read": the survey camps are the Writers' own, and the Wayhouse checks their journals every season. Make it a dead hand's private shorthand, or the mother's own cipher.
- **(write-up)** The journal is "inside his shirt" on R1 and at the old surveyor's hut in the crisis; the hand-over is never shown.
- **(roll / slot, variety)** Same heart as story 8: a son of a surveyor who walked into the green. H5a and H5b are different variants and the cap wasn't hit, but the player meets two of them in eight stories.
- Good: a straight-edge and camp ink in a tower that never surveys; "the true edge, a mile out"; "the kind Warden who carries the journals"; the last page in a different, hurried hand; a Warden captain faking the green nearer for wall money is the best use of a canon faction's wound ("a cause nobody funds") in the set.

**Creativity 4.** **Emotional pull 5.** A son carrying his mother's last pages through the line of the man who sent her in, read to him at last by someone who walked with her. This is the one a player would remember.

## 6 · The Court's New Clerk (7306, W7-A)

**Coherence 4.** Breaks:
- **(spine + placement)** C1-3: "bring a fresh copy, made at the Wayhouse, into the court". The Wayhouse is 3.68 away (5+ by road); that is a ten-day errand inside one card, and the card never says *why* a fresh copy helps (to compare an untampered copy against the sealed ones, presumably). Then the tenant rides the same road for an advocate while "the commission rides on the hearing".
- **(placement / write-up)** The court is the Binders' head's; the record house is "the lord's old keep". Paqax is not a lord. And in the Binders' town, "a *binder* bought a leaf" (R3) reads as a slaver until the second pass. Write "bookbinder".
- **(write-up)** The door's plant, the Wayhouse's copying book from that night, is seen at the Chainmarket; it lives in Highcrossing.
- **(spine)** Shown ending *Take a side at last*: the Writers stand with a woman who has just put three false lines into their copies, and she ends "a Writer again, with nothing left to revenge". The ending is good drama but the card should own that her sabotage goes unpunished, or the deal ending and this one blur.
- Good: the enemy's revenge is to have the court check the copies she herself falsifies; the heart's copy is the one accused, so her friend's revenge sinks her; left-handed copying as a tell; "the master's face goes still". The old silence as the heart's past is carried and paid for.

**Creativity 4.** **Emotional pull 4.** Twenty years of silence on the other side of a hearing; the player as advocate for someone whose friend is the enemy.

## 7 · The Copy House Across the Street (7307, W3-B)

**Coherence 4.** Breaks:
- **(roll / flavour)** Flavour a "to go straight at last" sits beside a reveal that she sells each entry to the house it shames and a crisis where that house's carriage waits at her lane door. The spine says the ways read the same; the motive doesn't. Hypocrisy would work if one line said so.
- **(spine)** C1-3's young copyists bound for Market debts are introduced as a Rescue premise and never seen again; the crisis's "take her on with her bond paid" is about the forger, not them.
- **(spine / write-up)** The ally's way, "the binder swears his presses were bought under a false name", doesn't say why that voids a sealed commission (a seal to a false name is void). One clause.
- **(placement)** The half-blind heart rides to the Wayhouse (3.93, ~4 by road) with the letter and the ways act back in Openharbour.
- Good: the Night Market's own seat (Openharbour) hosting a Night Market copy house across from the reading room; margins ("who owes, who is weak") as the real stock; the bindery's empty press-room as an absence-plant that pays off in the ally. The shown *Left with the holder* is a clean loss: nobody wins, the heart reads aloud no more.

**Creativity 4.** **Emotional pull 4.** The concordance a man wants to finish before his eyes go, and the keeping he loses, carry it; the shown ending leaves the want hanging, which hurts in the right way.

## 8 · Who Is Poor (7308, W4-B)

**Coherence 3.** Breaks:
- **(placement + spine)** The heart copies by night at a bindery in Highcrossing (R1) and his town, his stall, his mother's creditor and the broker's yard are all at Oathford, 1.42 away; the R1 plant is "a moneylender's clerk buying ink at the stall bench", and Highcrossing has no stall. The spine lets the heart's workplace and the heart's town be different slots; the roller stretched the stall to 1.42 and the heart now commutes a day and a half each way. Put the bindery in the heart's town or the stall in the hall's.
- **(spine)** Ch2 opens with the Wayhouse holding the mother's survey journal "under seal against her debt". The debt was owed to a private creditor in Oathford until the clerk bought it; the door only later says he kept it for himself. Order of information is defensible, but the card as written reads as a hall taking collateral on a stranger's debt.
- **(spine)** The uncle appears at the blow unset, and "sells what the boy's letters said" to buyers who already have the letters. What he adds is shame, not information; say that.
- **(variety)** Second lost-surveyor son (see 5).
- Good: "no debt bought from anyone who can write" is the best absence-plant of the eight; "the patience list"; a tally that adds up to the debt's face; the code-break that frees every family and burns every honest debt with them; "the clerk is ruined, and free". Placing a Binders' broker in a Great House river town is right by canon (the lords buy).

**Creativity 4.** Letter-writers selling the poor's begging letters to moneylenders is the idea most specific to this guild.
**Emotional pull 4.** A boy paying his lost mother's debt while his poverty is sold, with his own tally saying it was paid long ago.

## The set

**Variety 3.** Eight enemies, eight main towns, eight lords, as the roller promises, but the player feels the sameness underneath:
- 6 and 7 are near twins: stake S7 (a court's copying commission), blow "player stripped", ch2 opening "court copyists laid off", an added record house 0.5–0.8 out, false lines or stolen margins in the halls' copies, a woman enemy inside the court's paper.
- 5 and 8: the same mother.
- *The ledger doesn't add up* in 7 of 8 routines; *Copy it before it burns* in 3; *Unwelcome cargo* ("now 'unclean' / 'green-touched' / 'the debt's flour', barred from…") opens 3 of 8 chapter twos; "the X closes the dig/camp/hall and turns people off" is the chapter-1 move in 2, 3, 5 and near enough in 1, 4.
- *Burn your copy* rolled in 5 of 8, shown in 2; "three days off" in 3, 4, 5.
- Two "estranged kin, one is the knower" reveals (2, 6), as the spine doc already knew.

**Averages (8 stories):** coherence **3.5** · creativity **3.9** · emotional pull **3.9** · variety **3**.

**Best three:** 5 *The Mother's Journal* (the strongest heart and the best use of a faction's wound; fix the clock and it is a 5), 2 *The Last Reader* (the rope and the paper; the sister), 6 *The Court's New Clerk* (the cleverest scheme and the sharpest choice).
**Weakest three:** 1 *The Truce Held for a Debt* (five breaks, the longest shuttle, and a shown ending its own card undercuts), 4 *The Copies Sent for Mending* (a contradiction at the root: the lord holds a copy), 8 *Who Is Poor* (a heart who lives a day and a half from his own card).

### Failure patterns, counts, cheapest fix

| # | Pattern | Where | Cheapest fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Deadline ignores distance** ("three days", "the night before", "noon the last hour") | 1, 3, 5 (hard); 2, 6, 7 (soft: unshown 4–5-day rides) | Make the deadline a roller slot: `{days}` = road distance from the giver's place to the action + 1, printed into the card. Or: "the hearing is {days} off" with the roller filling it. |
| 2 | **The Wayhouse pin pulls far centres apart** | 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 | Either bound centres by distance to Highcrossing (≤2.5), or let the crisis be *decided by the leader's letter / the eldest's reply* carried to where the giver is, and keep "the Wayhouse" for stories whose action is there. This one is Laz's call (faction-stories §6). |
| 3 | **A person or party arrives unset** | 1 (widow), 2 (Seated House court), 7 (bound copyists), 8 (uncle) | Roller check: every party named in a *dealt* ending or door must appear on a dealt card; a plant that lives only on an undealt door option fails. Write-up: one clause tying each to the card before. |
| 4 | **Spine logic holes** | 1 (burn vs memory; draft/reply/sealing), 2 (burn pages vs reading aloud), 4 (archivist's copy), 5 (shorthand nobody reads), 6 (why a fresh copy), 7 (why a false name voids a seal) | One sentence each in the spine's crisis or presuppositions. Pure polish. |
| 5 | **Repeated texture** | ledger ×7, *Unwelcome cargo* ×3, closed-and-turned-off ×3–5, lost-surveyor sons ×2, 6/7 twins | Roller memory keys on stake code, blow event, ch2-opening premise and heart *parent* (H5, not H5a/b) as well as spine, enemy, lord and town; swap routine signatures where the spine allows (the self-check already permits it). |
| 6 | **Write-up ambiguity** | 1 (widow), 2 ("the Vale" on the reef), 6 ("binder" / "lord's old keep"), 3 (father's word missing), 5 (journal hand-over) | A rendering glossary (bookbinder; "the Binders' head's keep"; a region word drawn from the centre's region); print the heart's load-bearing line, not only the past. |
| 7 | **Missed canon resonance** | 2 (the blind lord is Tuhuḍo), 3 (the Chainmarket is the Binders' seat), 1 (a cross-faction truce) | Let the write-up name registry holders where a card lands in their seat; a one-line "this town is X's" pass. |

### Judgment

**Not yet at 4+.** Coherence sits at 3.5, variety at 3; creativity and pull are at the line.

What remains, in order of weight:
1. **Clock versus map** (pattern 1) is the one thing a player will call broken rather than thin. It is small to fix in the roller (compute the deadline from placement) but it is not polish: it is a rule the spines don't have yet.
2. **The Wayhouse pin** (pattern 2) is structural. It is why the far stories spend half their length on unshown roads and why hearts who should be frail ride for a week. Either bound the centre or relax "decided at the Wayhouse" to "decided by the guild, wherever its word reaches". Worth a one-line decision from Laz before more spines are written.
3. **Variety memory** (pattern 5) is a roller change of an afternoon: more keys, and the signature-swap the doc already allows.
4. Everything else (patterns 3, 4, 6, 7) is spine and write-up polish, about one sentence per break, and would lift coherence to 4 on its own.

So: the spines are the right instrument, and the fixes are mostly polish plus two roller rules. The one structural question is where a Writers crisis is allowed to be decided. Settle that, fix the clocks, widen the memory, and this set is a 4.
