# Review of the Writers samples, v7c (spines draft 3, seeds 7401–7408)

Independent read of `samples-v7c.md` against `spines.md`, `rolls-7401-7408.txt`, `map-D.json`, `world-factions.md` and `leaders.md`. No earlier review or sample set was read. Scores use the anchors given (5: holds up as a hand-written guild questline; 3: works but feels assembled; 1: doesn't make sense). Each break is tagged **spine**, **roll/fill**, **placement** or **write-up**.

## The case against a high score

Before the scores, the strongest honest case that this set is not yet a 4:

- **One skeleton shows through all eight.** Three routine cards, a reveal that always reads "the leader, off the road at X … so 'plant', 'plant' and 'plant' all point at Y", three chapter-1 cards of which the third is always *an opposing proxy holds something + an ally who'll help for a price*, an opening, a blow, a door with a "code-bending way", and a crisis with three ways. A player who sees two of these will predict the third's shape card for card.
- **The leader is a bus.** She turns up at the reveal of seven stories in eight (the eighth is her own plot). The canon makes her a wanderer; the stories make her a timetable.
- **Five of eight pass through Highcrossing** (1 R3 and suit, 3 crisis, 4 R2, 5 centre, 8 crisis), and two hearts are the same person twice: a by-shape copyist who cannot read what she or he copies (3 and 8).
- **Payloads land on schedule whatever the player knows.** In 2 the player holds the fences' list of houses and dates before fair night; in 8 the player holds the watermark proof before the hearing; in 5 the player has known of the burning since the reveal. Each crisis still arrives as written.
- **Placement broke one story's geography** (1: the heart's stall sits in a *different* Free Lord's keep from the one whose men serve the summons) and put another's hoard in a hostile enclave's land (3).

Against that: the hearts' wants, pasts and tells are genuinely authored and every one of them lands; the eight schemes are distinct and all grow from the guild's canon ("they keep the records, so they know everyone's secrets and debts"); nothing is nonsense. The skeleton is a design choice, not a fault of these rolls. The scores below weigh both.

## 1 · Sold by the Wayhouse (7401 · W2-B)

**Coherence 3.** The scheme is tight (a false seal, the halls' own post carrying the sale, a heart who guessed and wrote on for the fee), but placement bent the geography under it.

Breaks:
1. **The stall is in the wrong lord's land** (placement, and a missing slot need in the spine). The stall is placed at Breakwall, Kotqot Wall-breaker's keep; the enemy is the archivist of Bogford's Free Lord, Hausethayai. The crisis has "the Free Lord's men bring [the summons] to the stall" and "the lord's men watch the roads", and the spine's own text says "where his land honours it". Bogford's men serving a Throne summons inside Kotqot's keep, and watching Kotqot's roads, is not something a Free Lord of a bog village can do; and if the heart is already in another lord's town, the whole flight to a "haven" loses its reason (Kotqot's hall could take his oath). The `{stall}` slot binds only "≤1.25 of the dig"; it needs "in `{lord}`'s land".
2. **The clock counts one leg of two** (spine rule 9). The suit is cried in Highcrossing with the hearing 4 days off; the summons must then travel Highcrossing→Breakwall (3.2 days) *before* the heart can run Breakwall→Longwall (2.1) and the lofts carry his oath (1). That is 6+ days against a 4-day clock, and the card has the summons arrive "that night".
3. **"Bogford's toll on the Highcrossing road"** (placement). Bogford touches no road on the map; the Marches reach Highcrossing by Breakwall→Longwall or Chainmarket→Carter's Rest. A track-toll is fine; "carts turning off the Highcrossing road" and a toll-book of "Wayhouse post" assume a real road.
4. **R3 swap is the weaker reading** (spine). A magistrate's steward "means to burn" a page of the *Wayhouse's own* lodging book, and the Wayhouse "wants it copied first" rather than refusing him the book. The unswapped *ledger doesn't add up* reads cleanly; the *copy it before it burns* swap doesn't.
5. **The confidant knows "the suit to come"** (write-up). A dead digger's friend who saw a seal ring cannot know a lawsuit is being prepared in Highcrossing; the leader could add it, the card doesn't say so.
6. Minor: a Free Lord of a Low-standing bog village wanting "an archive of the old empire's rolls of his own" is a tonal mismatch (roll/fill), and the rolls report writes the heart as "she" throughout while the write-up casts him a man (report only; the write-up is consistent).

**Creativity 4.** The guild's own post carrying its forged sale, the toll-book entry as the damning record, and the heart's guilt hidden in a paid-off debt are hand-written-grade ideas.

**Emotional pull 4.** A man who left the halls to write for the poor, who took the one fee that paid his stall off, and who must confess it to save the halls that won't take him back. The shown ending (he swears, fee and all, and is refused the roll) is the right cost. The want/past/tell triangle is the set's best-constructed.

## 2 · The Stalls' Oath (7402 · W4-A)

**Coherence 3.** Everything is placed where it should be (the fence's shop is Saltmarket's real one, the loft a day's climb from the clerk's hall), and the chain stall→loft→robbed merchant→keeper is sound. Two holes, one of them large:

1. **The player holds the list before fair night** (spine). C1-2 finds "a list of houses and dates in a stall's hand" in the fence's boy's tube; C1-3's ally asks for "a copy of the list" as his price, so the player has it; chapter 2's opening walks Saltmarket's streets with the sign-painter to see which houses are marked. Then "on the night of the horse fair every house on the list is robbed". Nothing on any card says why a player holding the houses and dates could not warn them or the watch. The spine's answer (blow a: a warning would look like a Writer selling secrets) was not dealt; blow c was. The code-bending way ("tell one family, privately") makes it worse by admitting the player could.
2. **The Sabotage door reaches the sign-painter by assertion** (spine/write-up). Looking into a girth cut in Openharbour's stable "brings the player to the old sign-painter" of Saltmarket, who then volunteers a ten-year-old order about the oath board. Two unrelated facts are stapled together; the card needs one clause (the painter saw who was in the stable; or the Hand is the painter's lodger).
3. Minor (write-up): the clerk's defence "the oath binds the writer at the stall, not the hall" is a confession that the halls read the stalls' letters, which the card treats as deniable until the founding-book way.

**Creativity 4.** Letter-writers as an informant network, lists by pigeon, the painted-out last line of a public oath, and the clerk's legalism at the end are all specific and fresh. The dispute-as-crisis (does the oath bind the halls?) is a good change of key from the set's acquisitions.

**Emotional pull 4.** The carter who cannot write told his daughter where the dowry chest is hidden "in case the road takes me", and that letter is what loses it. That is a real wound, and the shown ending (half the dowry back, the board repainted whole, the clerk sells his list one last time) is bittersweet in the right proportion.

## 3 · The Copies Sent for Mending (7403 · W6-A)

**Coherence 4.** The best-built plot in the set: the mending pretext, the courier's one-ink "returned" marks, the tower, the hall master who'd sooner copy than send, the valley's margin grants, the heart's shape-sheets. Seams, none fatal:

1. **The hoard's tower is in Last Wave land** (placement). (8.69, 2.28) is `t-wave`, 1.8 from both Longwall and the Proving Ground. The leader hides the Crown Crossing's land rolls in the territory of an armed Qotiro enclave "dangerous to enter" (canon), with a deaf caretaker buying lamp oil. The `{tower}` slot needs a territory constraint (the centre's, or Great House land).
2. **The reveal knows more than the roll-case shows** (spine/write-up). A pedlar's roll-case with a label "addressed to no hall" plus the old reader's word "makes known the leader's letters, her courier, her reason in her letter … and the guild's hidden side". How the tutor gets from a label to the leader's letters and their stated reason is not on the card.
3. **"A Sharp Hands buyer waiting" is unglossed** (write-up of a flavour). Printed bare, it reads as the leader selling the rolls to the Sharp Hands, which contradicts the enemy line ("keep the balance") and the leader's canon. One clause fixes it ("so no Sharp Hands buyer decides a valley by what he buys").
4. **"Because the player was on the road with the courier"** (spine, blow a). The player followed the courier's road to the tower on C1-1 and watched the courier leave Longwall on C1-3; the player never travelled *with* him. The frame needs a different hook or a sentence putting the player on that road.
5. **The door's knower is two days from the door** (placement/write-up). The door is at Longwall's reading room; "the heart's word" about the paper carters comes from the Wayhouse in Highcrossing. Say how it reaches.
6. Minor: why the Wayhouse's own land rolls are copied by night "for mending" when they aren't torn is the plant, but no card ever says who ordered those copies or why; and the roller's clock prints 4 for a 2.00 road rounded up plus one (should be 3).

**Creativity 5.** The guild's own head as the enemy, "lost" copies that were never burned because she keeps the code, a foundling who copies by shape the roll that holds her own family's name and cannot read it. This would hold up anywhere.

**Emotional pull 5.** "She asks to carry it herself, though she can't read it" is the best tell in the set. The shown ending (Seized: the valley lost, the heart sent to a far hall, the leader thanks the courier) is bleak and earned.

## 4 · The Copy House Across the Street (7404 · W3-B)

**Coherence 4.** Clean in its own town: a forger who goes legitimate to keep stealing, the margins as the real prize, the bookbinder whose presses she bought, the steward's own debt in the margins. Breaks:

1. **The copy house exists before it opens** (spine, field 7). The reveal, which comes before chapter 1, finds "a box of 'spoiled sheets' from the copy house's first week"; chapter 1's move then has her "open a copy house across the street". Either the reveal's box comes from her back room, or the move is the *bid*, with the house already open.
2. **The pawned sheets reveal too much** (write-up). Court-roll entries with hall margins show the trade; they don't name "the copyist and his wife". The leader must supply that, and the card should say so.
3. Minor (spine): the blow is "heart disgraced, cried down for taking a side" and the ending shown costs "the halls' oath (a forger is sworn in)"; no second shaming of the heart, so the rule holds, but the heart's standing in court after the disgrace is never restored on any card before she "asks the player to win the rolls back" with the court's letter in hand.

**Creativity 4.** A thief who buys the right to the thing she was stealing is a strong reversal; "while the halls are true" as the commission's wording is a good lever; the Dusk Road's carriage at the lane door is a fine tell.

**Emotional pull 4.** A half-blind reading master who knows the rolls by ear and wants her concordance done before her eyes go; the shown ending gives it to her with the forger copying beside her, watched. Quiet and satisfying. Slightly lower stakes to the heart than 1–3 (she loses her keeping, not her liberty).

## 5 · The Letters Never Collected (7405 · W5-A)

**Coherence 4.** The Holy House burning records of its grants being work-for-hire, in First Peoples land where its word holds, is canon-exact. Breaks:

1. **The cook's line doesn't travel** (write-up of flavour b). At a camp 3.4 days from Highcrossing that has just burned pages *here*, the cook says that before *any* burning "they sleep at Highcrossing's chapel, and burn at dawn". It should be "when they burn in Highcrossing".
2. **A nine-year-old pigeonhole in the two-hundred-year section** (spine). The heart's father's hole lies "among the Wall's oldest pigeonholes" with letters "two hundred years uncollected"; no card says why a surveyor lost nine years ago holds a hole there (an inherited family hole would do it in six words).
3. **The drafters' covering letter *to the Mount* sits in a Longwall desk** (write-up). A letter addressed to the Mount would be at the Mount; call it the drafters' draft or copy.
4. Minor: a Throne magistrate issuing a heresy writ to the Holy House against the Writers' HQ in his own capital is a stretch the spine spends its one writ on; the card should name the lever (the Mount's standing with the Throne, or the magistrate's piety).

**Creativity 4.** A dead-letter wall, heresy as a cover for a fee, silence serving as well as fire, a writ that names one decade's holes. "Open the ledgers" as the shown ending (read aloud before the writ is served; the inquisitor ruined and his writ stands) is a very good shape.

**Emotional pull 5.** A son writing every month for nine years to a father the green took, each letter filed unopened, who asks everyone he meets if they walked with his father. The month-on line ("hears a stranger read his father's hole's neighbours aloud") is the set's best closing image.

## 6 · The Wall Through the Spring (7406 · W8-A)

**Coherence 4.** The cairn fraud is well mechanised (a moved cairn scraped clean of lichen so the old stones look new; the Writers' own checker's mark laundering the readings; the old false certification underneath). Breaks:

1. **All four pre-chapter cards sit in one town** (placement). R1, R2, R3 and the reveal are all at the Ninth Ring. Legal under the bounds, but the routine reads as one afternoon, and the roller itself flags "1 distinct".
2. **The levy is voted before the maps are certified** (spine). "The lords' levy is voted on the season's maps and the diggers start the ditch", then "the lords' stewards want the Ninth Ring's reading room to certify the maps in 3 days". A levy voted on uncertified maps whose certification is still wanted is backwards unless the vote was provisional; say so.
3. **"No notch for last year's cairn" against "the newest was raised on midsummer night"** (write-up). The staff's missing notch is last year's; the moved cairn is this year's. One of the two words is wrong.
4. Minor: in First Peoples land the payers would be houses, not "lords" (write-up); and the Wardens' leaders "never lie to recruits" (canon), so a lying captain under them wants one clause of distance ("behind the Green Wall's back").

**Creativity 4.** A wall a mile inside the real edge, a spring that feeds the lowland town too, a carter hauling the timber for the wrong wall as his father hauled stones for the last wrong line.

**Emotional pull 4.** Debt, pasture, a son who takes the Wardens' shilling and calls his father a fool. The shown ending (the spring piped, the wall in the wrong place, the heart owing the Wardens for the pipe, the towers failing a mile further out) is bleak irony done well.

## 7 · The Truce Held for a Debt (7407 · W1-A)

**Coherence 4.** The most intricate plot and it mostly holds: draft, reply, sealing, lodging in order; the hold rule planted on C1-1; the door copy planted on C1-2; the key-book tell. Breaks:

1. **A cross-faction feud is placed as a local one** (placement). lord_a is a Seated House head (First Peoples), lord_b a Banner Lord (Great House), and both borrow from lenders who lodge bonds at the Ninth Ring, the Alliance's chief town, 2.5 days from Oathford against 1.4 to Highcrossing. A truce between those two is an inter-faction treaty, which "lodged at a Writers' hall" undersells; the spine allows different peoples, but the slot should prefer the same major faction or say why a Banner Lord's lenders sit in the Ninth Ring.
2. **The Ninth Ring's Wall sorter is at Oathford's gate, twice** (spine). R2 has him as the "will"'s recipient at the steward's gate; C1-3 has him there again contradicting the clerk. Consistent, so readable as a travelling Wall man, but no card says it, and a sorter posting a letter to himself 2.5 days away is a question a player will ask.
3. **A stripped player decides the crisis** (spine/write-up). Blow c strips the player of rank; at the crisis the heart asks the player, "with the master present", whether to let the hold stand. The master who stripped the player stands by while the player rules on her hold. One clause on standing is needed.
4. Minor: "her name kept off every copy" (the clerk's price) is unclear about whose name and which copy; the hidden truth (the steward took the sealed original away) is good but the write-up's crisis says the master "holds the sealed truce", which is then false on the card's own face without the tell's gloss.

**Creativity 4.** A master who keeps two lords at a standstill by leaking true debts; "balance is the Writers' oldest duty" as a sincere villainy the canon leader endorses; a ruling that reads the roll, not a clerk's memory.

**Emotional pull 3.** The heart's want (one apprentice) and past (copied the debt as a boy) are good texture but the crisis's cost falls on the ford's tenants and the miller, not on him, and the shown ending (the hold stands, the mill idle) is a shrug rather than a wound. The miller and his mother carry more feeling than the heart does.

## 8 · The Apprentice's Hand (7408 · W7-B)

**Coherence 3.** The scheme is cruel and clear (a teacher who uses her pupil's hand to make the Wayhouse repeat its wrong), but its status words contradict each other:

1. **Struck off a roll he was never on** (spine, fields 4 and 10). The heart's want is "to be sworn a copyist at the Wayhouse"; no hall would take him. The crisis then has the hearing "strike him off the roll", and the shown ending "restores" him, after which "the heart is sworn". He cannot be struck off, restored and then sworn. Make the hearing *bar his hand from every hall* (and the ending lift the bar), or put him on an apprentice roll on R1.
2. **She signs the list** (spine, move M5). The chapter-1 move nails up twelve false lines "signed with her old roll name". That is the publication the payload saves for *after* the striking-off ("then she publishes that the lines were hers"). As written she tells the Wayhouse who set the lines before it judges the boy; the hearing has to ignore its own door-list to strike him.
3. **The hearing ignores the proof the player holds** (spine). The door (dealt before the crisis) gives the hearing-scribe's watermark finding that the false lines came from the tower's paper. The hearing strikes the heart off anyway, and the card doesn't say the finding was withheld or refused.
4. **A shepherd boy knows the twenty-year-old wrong** (write-up). The reveal's knower carries sheets up and down a hill; the master's false line a generation ago is not his to know. The leader must add it.
5. Minor: the heart is "a letter-writer's apprentice" whose "twelve copies in one hand" are the *reading room's* copies; how an unsworn apprentice's copies go out under the hall's oath is never said. "A market woman of the Silver Road Counting-House" puts a market in a fortified counting-house (placement, `{mother_town}` needs a town with a market).

**Creativity 4.** The teacher who taught for nothing and then spends her pupil as a weapon; "the halls' next mistake"; a hearing-scribe who kept his draft against the rule.

**Emotional pull 4.** "He still copies in the hand his teacher gave him, and won't change it", his mother siding with the halls, and the month-on "sworn, and won't use the hand he was taught" are strong. The Reckoning twist (the wronged won't see her ruined) is the set's most adult choice.

## The set

**Variety 4.** Eight different enemies (a lord's archivist, a hall clerk, the guild's own head, a Night Market forger, an inquisitor, a Warden captain, a hall master, a struck-off copyist), eight different payloads, seven different crisis families, every shown ending different, hearts spread over five types. Deductions: two by-shape copyists who can't read (3, 8); two "kin turned" blows (6, 8) in consecutive-but-one stories; five of eight route through Highcrossing; the leader at seven reveals of eight; the C1-3 card has the same shape in all eight; "copy it before it burns" on five routines. The eight read as eight stories, with a visible common chassis.

**Averages.** Coherence **3.6** (3, 3, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 3). Creativity **4.1** (4, 4, 5, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4). Emotional pull **4.1** (4, 4, 5, 4, 5, 4, 3, 4).

Pattern 1 is the only one the roller can cause on its own; the rest are a line each in the spines or the renderer. No new spines are needed.

### Three best, three weakest

**Best.** **3 · The Copies Sent for Mending** (the guild's head as enemy, the foundling's own name in the margins she cannot read; the one story a player would quote). **5 · The Letters Never Collected** (the strongest heart and the strongest close). **4 · The Copy House Across the Street** (the cleanest mechanism; one sequencing slip).

**Weakest.** **1 · Sold by the Wayhouse** (a 4+ spine broken by placement: the wrong lord's town, a clock that counts one leg). **8 · The Apprentice's Hand** (struck off a roll he isn't on; a signed list that pre-empts its own payload). **7 · The Truce Held for a Debt** (intricate and sound, but the heart is a bystander to his own crisis, and the feud is misplaced across two major factions).

### Judgment

**Creativity (4.1) and variety (4) are at 4+. Coherence (3.6) is not yet.** Nothing here fails to make sense; every story works. What keeps coherence under 4 is not the spines' plots but their edges: slot tables that bind distance without ownership (pattern 1), reveals that know too much (2), payloads that ignore what the player has in hand (3), a clock that counts one leg (4), and a few status words (5). Fix those six patterns, at most two or three lines each, re-roll, and this set should score 4+ on all three. Then the remaining ceiling is structural, not local: the identical C1-3 shape, the leader at every reveal, and the formula reveal sentence. Those are rendering and variation choices for after the Writers are locked, not reasons to hold them.
