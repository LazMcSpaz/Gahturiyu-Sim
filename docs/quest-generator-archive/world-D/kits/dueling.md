# The Dueling Society: story kit (v6c draft)

**Replaces** `guild-kits.md` §5, *Story kit* (the Profile and signature premises above it are unchanged). Built to fixlist v6c: every part carries the throughline fields (§1), the pools are wider (§2), and chapter 1 has a move table (§4). New material is marked *(Claude)*. Enemies D1–D5 keep their numbers; their wording is revised where the throughline needed it.

**Why it changed.** In the four D worlds the Dueling story was the sister's blood bout three times (D5 by elimination twice). Her old killing is now one engine of nine: the old heads, a patron's son, the showman's debts, a fixed book, a rival inside the Ring, a lord's prize that kills champions, a feud the Society once settled unfairly, and a priest whose shrine the bouts emptied.

**How to read the needs columns.**
- `a | b` means either; `a + b` means both. Means: `paper`, `coin`, `men`, `inside`, `name`, `faith`, `kin`, `craft` (fixlist v6c §1).
- **Shapes** is a gate *(Claude)*: the part rolls only with an enemy of that shape. Means alone can't tell an enemy attacking the Ring from outside from one whose scheme runs inside it (see the note at the end).
- **Reaches** *(Claude)*: the stakes the enemy's scheme can threaten. A stake rolls only if the scheme reaches it **and** the enemy has one of the stake's `needs`.
- **The roll is legal when:** the stake is reached and its needs met; the move, the crisis and the blow each have their needs met by the enemy's means and pass their shape gate; the move's hits and the crisis's hits both include the stake's `hit` (so the story is about one thing, and the crisis decides it). That covers throughline tests 2–4; tests 1 and 5 are fixed per enemy entry; test 6 is the sentence (end of file).
- † marks a part whose main means is `paper` (or a house ban by `faith`) aimed at the guild. It counts toward *writ fatigue* (at most 2 per world).

- **D7** *(Claude)***:** *the cost:* the grand tournament's prize, a post in a Banner Lord's guard, has sent the ladder's last champions to his march, where sport-trained fighters die in the lord's feud. - **D8** *(Claude)***:** *the old debt:* in the Society's first year its ring settled the Mill feud between two villages with a yield bout, and the bout was not fair (variants). - **D9** *(Claude)***:** *the cost:* the Society's feast-day bouts (the licence granted for a shrine's feast days) draw the feast crowd and its offerings away from the parish shrine, and its soup line has closed. ## The throughline test (fixlist v6c §1.6)
**Method.** The kit is modelled in `D/kits/throughline_check.py` (output in `throughline_run.txt`). It enumerates every enemy × stake × move × blow × crisis that the needs, shapes and hits allow (**130 legal combinations, 26 enemy–stake–move–crisis spines**, all nine enemies used, no move, crisis or stake left unreachable). Then `random.Random(93)` rolls 8 in the roller's order: an enemy first, then one of its legal combinations. *(Sampling all combinations flat gave D2 five of eight picks, because he has 40 of the 130.)*

1. **D8 · S3 · M5 · heart disgraced · C8.** Because the headman of the village that lost the Society's first feud-bout wants desperation (the mill his village lost), he means to prove that bout was never fair and take the mill back; he cries the Ring down in the taverns as a killers' yard, then has the heart accused of throwing a bout, and it comes to a head when his village comes to the Ring for the old winners' blood, the heart's kin among them.

3. **D2 · S2 · M6 · leader struck down (the sister, wounded) · C2.** Because the council-house head who holds the Ring's lease wants ambition (his son at the top of the ladder), he has every fighter who beats his son fined, banned or pressed into his guard; he posts men at the Ring's gate on bout nights to turn the crowd away, then has the sister wounded, and it comes to a head when the tournament licence is revoked after a riot he paid for, and the heart is fined and barred.

**What the test changed before these eight passed:**
- **Chasing out nonsense.** A priest "put you down the ladder" (*stripped* now needs `inside`). A village headman "had you arrested", and an old master's students "took members" (both need an *Above* enemy's men now). The showman could "shut a tavern-yard ring" or "strike down his sister", and the master-at-arms could "get rings banned" though he recruits from them (both now have per-enemy blow lists).
- **Making the instruments legal.** D7 trades `coin` for `paper` (his lord's writs press men). D9 was moved from the Ring (S1) to the feast-day licence (S2*c*), because a house ban doesn't bind in the Ring's town but his shrine's own seal on its licence does.
- **One tag per stake.** The licence's hit became `trade`, so `thing` always means the ladder.

**Hardest coherence problem.** Means say what an enemy *can* do, not what it would *want* to do. Matched on means alone, the showman could pay for a riot to close his own tournament (C2), or the bookmaker could seal the Ring. Two small fields fixed it: **Shapes** on moves and crises (Within / outside), and **Reaches** on enemies (the stakes the scheme touches). Every remaining case was then caught by reading the 26 spines one by one, not by the tags.
