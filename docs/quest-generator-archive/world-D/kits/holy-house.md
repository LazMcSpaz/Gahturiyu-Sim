# The Holy House: story kit (v6c draft)

**Replaces** `guild-kits.md` §6, *Story kit* (the Profile, the two codes and the three signature premises above it are unchanged). Built to fixlist v6c: every part carries the throughline fields (§1), the pools are wider (§2), and chapter 1 has a move table (§4). New material is marked *(Claude)*. Enemies HH1–HH6 keep their numbers and their revelations; the fields around them are new.

**Why it changed.** The four D worlds rolled the patron's old grant (HH3 with C4) twice, and the high priest gave the reveal three times. The kit now draws on four engines, none of them the high priest's lost faith, **which stays unused** (the Mount's hook; still a question for Laz, guild-kits §7 note 12):
- **corruption:** fear sold as protection (HH1), the tithe (HH2), a fake relic (HH4), bought inquisitors (HH7);
- **militarism:** the patron's riders (HH3), a raid answered by raids (HH8);
- **the cost to the poor:** the penance debts (HH5), an old burning (HH6);
- **the sincere priests, and reform against the sword:** a priest who feeds the hungry from the house's own stores (HH9).

**How to read the needs columns** (the same conventions as the Dueling kit):
- `a | b` means either, `a + b` both.
- **Shapes** is a gate on the enemy's shape *(Claude)*.
- **Reaches** *(Claude)* lists the stakes the enemy's scheme can threaten. A stake rolls only if the scheme reaches it **and** the enemy has one of the stake's `needs`.
- The roll is legal when the move, the crisis and the blow each have their needs met and pass their shape gate, and the move's and the crisis's hits both include the stake's `hit`.
- † marks a part whose main means is `paper` (or a heresy writ by `faith`) aimed at the house's own. It counts toward *writ fatigue*.

**Revelations** (each names who is harmed if it comes out):
- **HH1:** the inquisitors' heresy lists are copied from the tithe-houses' arrears books: whoever stops paying is named a heretic. He is making fear pay the tithe, so the major powers need an armed house again. (Never states his lost faith.) *Harmed:* the clerks who copied the lists; every family already named.
- **HH2:** the protection tithe, taken town by town, has emptied the outer-ring kitchens: the hungry the priestess feeds are the house's own tithe-payers. He keeps the rolls rising, because the order must be fed. *Harmed:* the order's riders, fed from the hungry's grain.
- **HH3:** his house paid for the Mount's walls and the Armed Nave generations ago, for the house's blessing on its claims. The bargain's last term pledges the order's riders to his house's service, and he means to call it in for his own quarrel. He acts through his house's court, which binds only in its own lordship. *Harmed:* the riders first pledged.
- **HH4:** the Mount's procession relic is a copy from his workshop. He holds the true one, and sells the house "recovered pieces" of it, with a bought keeper to swear to each. *Harmed:* the keepers who swore.
- **HH5:** the penance fines the inquisitors set by the book have put poor families in debt to the tithe-houses. The broker buys the debts and takes the people who can't pay. No priest chose it; the book did. *Harmed:* the tithe-house keepers who sold the debts.
- **HH6:** years ago he burned a village shrine with its keeper inside, on a false heresy charge. The "heretics" he hunts now are the village's survivors, who know. *Harmed:* the priests who stood by, and the survivors he still holds.
- **HH7** *(Claude)***:** the inquisitors of one region have been taking a True Line elder's grain and horses. In return their heresy charges fall on clients of the Seated Houses, wherever they shelter, so the Seated Houses' standing frays and his line's older claim looks cleaner. The house's writ binds only in its own lordship or on its lands (*Event ceiling*). *Harmed:* the inquisitors who took the grain; the clients' families already judged.
- **HH8** *(Claude)***:** *the old debt:* years ago the order's riders burned a Last Wave enclave's smoke hall on a heresy raid, calling it a heathen house. The raid leader's attacks on wayside shrines and tithe-towns are the answer. The Mount has used each raid to raise more riders and a higher tithe, so the order and the raiders feed each other. *Harmed:* the riders who rode that night; the tithe-towns, who pay for both sides.
- **HH9** *(Claude)***:** a Low Shrine priest has been emptying the tithe-houses' grain into the outer ring's bowls for a year, and writing false receipts. The hungry the Low Shrine feeds are eating the order's grain. When the receipts are found, the Mount will close the Low Shrine, and the priestess with it. **The priestess doesn't know** (she is never the enemy). *Harmed:* the priestess, and everyone in the line.

  - otherwise the high priest gives the crisis when its variant names him, or else the reveal (faith is leverage, and he uses what he learns);
  - the priestess is a natural candidate for chapter 1's ally plant.

## The throughline test (fixlist v6c §1.6)
**Method.** As for the Dueling kit: `D/kits/throughline_check.py` enumerates every legal enemy × stake × move × blow × crisis (**160 legal combinations, 25 spines**, all nine enemies, eight moves, eight crises and six stakes reachable). `random.Random(93)` then rolls 8 in the roller's order: an enemy first, then one of its legal combinations. *(Flat sampling over-picks HH1 and HH6, which have 40 combinations each.)*

1. **HH8 · S4 · M6 · leader struck down (the high priest, wounded or killed) · C6.** Because a Last Wave raid leader wants revenge (the riders paid back for a burned smoke hall), he raids the wayside shrines and the towns that tithe to them; he cries in the markets that the house's shelter is worth nothing, then strikes down the high priest on the road, and it comes to a head when his raiders camp a night from the Mount, the tithe-towns' protection fails, and the heart's village is next.
2. **HH6 · S2 · M2 · heart captive · C2.** Because an inquisitor wants cover-up (an old burning kept buried), he hunts as heretics the survivors of the shrine he burned with its keeper inside; he has a man taken from the Low Shrine's line, then has the heart held, and it comes to a head when the inquisitors take the heart's kin from the Low Shrine's line on a charge he laid, to be judged at the Mount on the feast day.
3. **HH2 · S4 · M4 · heart captive · C3.** Because the Mount's tithe-master wants preservation (the order fed), he keeps the protection tithe rising town by town, though it has emptied the kitchens the priestess feeds from; he raises one valley's tithe and calls in its arrears at once, then has the heart held, and it comes to a head when the tithe debts are sold, the heart's family's among them, and the debtors are penned in the Mount's yard to march at dawn.
4. **HH3 · S1 · M1 · members taken · C4.** Because a Seated House patron wants ambition (the order's riders for his house's quarrel), he means to call in the old grant's last term, which pledges the order's riders to his house; he lays his house's claim to the Mount before his house's court, then has novices and penitents taken, and it comes to a head when, by his court's ruling, his men take the Mount's gate under the old grant, and the heart's company of riders is the first pledged.
5. **HH7 · S2 · M2 · members taken · C8.** Because a True Line elder wants ambition (his line above the Seated Houses), he buys the inquisitors' favour with grain and horses, so heresy charges fall on the Seated Houses' clients; he has a man taken from the Low Shrine's line by inquisitors he paid, then has novices and penitents taken on their charges, and it comes to a head when at the feast-day judgment his bought inquisitors find the heart's kin heretics, taken from the Low Shrine where they sheltered, and their fields pass to his house.
6. **HH3 · S5 · M1 · members taken · C4.** Because a Seated House patron wants ambition (the order's riders for his house's quarrel), he means to call in the old grant's last term; he lays his house's claim to the old grant before his house's court, then has novices and penitents taken, and it comes to a head when, by his court's ruling, his men take the Mount's gate under the old grant, and the heart's company of riders is the first pledged.
7. **HH1 · S3 · M2 · player stripped of rank · C2.** Because the high priest wants ambition (an armed house the powers must need), he has the heresy lists copied from the tithe-houses' arrears books, so whoever stops paying is named a heretic; he has a tithe-dodger dragged from a shrine's steps, then has you stripped of your rank in the order, and it comes to a head when the inquisitors take the heart's kin from the shrine where they had sheltered, on a charge he laid, to be judged at the Mount on the feast day.
8. **HH9 · S2 · M7 · heart disgraced · C7.** Because a Low Shrine priest wants faith (a house that feeds people again), he empties the tithe-houses' stores into the hungry's bowls and writes false receipts to hide it; he feeds the outer ring from a tithe-house's door and calls it the house's duty, then lets the heart be accused of shaming the house in public, and it comes to a head when his false receipts are found, the riders come to the Low Shrine for the grain, and the heart, who carried it, is named the thief.

All eight pass with no "and also". The sample rolled HH3 twice (picks 4 and 6), and they differ only in the stake. HH3 has two spines and HH4 one, so those enemies vary only through their variants and blows. That is a known weakness, kept because both are canon-bound to one crisis.

**What the test changed before these eight passed:**
- **Chasing out nonsense.** The True Line elder could "strike down the high priest" he courts, so struck-down targets are now per enemy. The raid leader could "have you arrested on the house's complaint" (men alone no longer arrest outside *Above*). The raider could "accuse the heart of shaming the house" (*disgraced* and *framed* need `inside` or `faith`). A Within tithe-master could get his own house's shrines outlawed (*outlawed* is Above or Beside only).
- **One crisis was illegal, not just odd:** under HH7, C2's captor would have been an inquisitor he bought, not the enemy, and the enemy must fill the crisis's opposing slot. HH7 got C8 (*Ruin from above*, shield *Bought*), which is exactly his scheme.
- **Two stakes got their own tags:** the tithe rolls became `trade`, and the relic became its own stake (S6, HH4 only), so the two `thing` stakes never meet.

**Hardest coherence problem.** As in the Dueling kit: means tell what an enemy can do, not what he would. The worst case here was the sincere priest (HH9). He has `inside` like every Within enemy, so on means alone he could shut the Low Shrine (C1) or lay a heresy charge (C2). Keeping him off them took giving him no `faith` and no `men`, and adding a move and a crisis (M7, C7) that only his `name + inside` reaches.
