# Review: coherence and creativity of the v6c sample (worlds 7101–7104)

Independent read of `guild-narratives-v6c.md` (24 stories), checked against `guild-kits.md` (v6c kits), `faction-stories.md` §6 and §11a, `assembly.md` §4 (chapter-2 doors), and the canon in `world-factions.md` / `leaders.md`. Nothing else under `test/world/` was read. The rolls files were used only to confirm ids, `means_used` and blow details behind each summary.

**Scores:** 5 = would hold up as a hand-written guild questline; 3 = works, but feels assembled; 1 = doesn't make sense. Before each score, the strongest case against a high one.

**Headline:** coherence **3.1**, creativity **3.3**. The throughline test is doing its job at the level it was built for (enemy → stake → move → crisis): almost every story's spine is a real cause-and-effect chain. The breaks are in what the test doesn't look at: the **heart's fitness** for the role the crisis gives her, the **heart's state** after a *captive* blow, the **door's premise** versus how the blow was struck, the **odd job's** relation to the scheme, and **variants** that quietly change the "what" of a scheme. None of these is a story-craft problem; each is one missing check.

## 1. The Writers

### 7101 · The Survey Hall's Simmer — coherence 3 · creativity 3
**Case against:** the throughline says the master leaks *debts*; the rolled variant (W1c) says she leaks *boundary readings, not debts*; the crisis is *a debt read out of the court rolls*. The story contradicts itself about what the enemy does. The leader is "wounded on the road, through her people inside": the kit's blow row says wounded needs `men`, disgraced needs `inside`; W1 has no `men`. The Sabotage door ("the blow came through something rigged") is a stretch for a road wounding.
**Breaks:**
- *variant / throughline wording:* W1c (readings) vs base scheme and C3 S7 reading (a debt). The reveal and the crisis don't agree.
- *blow:* leader wounded via `inside` (kit: wounded = `men`). Door Investigate *Sabotage* chosen by event alone.
- *heart:* H4c, the old clerk who knows the hall's copying book, has no stated tie to the toll lord's court rolls, and "the heart's village burns" asks for a heart with a village (H3 carter, H5 surveyor's son fit; a hall clerk barely).
**What works:** take the rolls in hand → a debt leaks → quarrel becomes feud → a village burns is a true *therefore* chain, and "keep the balance" as a crime is the Writers' hidden side done right. The relay loft with pigeons unfed a week is a good odd-job image.

### 7102 · The Mile That Isn't There — coherence 4 · creativity 4
**Case against:** a Warden captain (Wardens: a small order "always begging for help") with "men on every road" out of Highcrossing is more force than canon gives him; the Sabotage door for a road wounding is again the wrong premise; the heart's gender wobbles ("son … her debts" is right, his mother's, but reads as a slip).
**Breaks:**
- *door:* leader wounded by `men` → Investigate *Sabotage*. An ambush isn't sabotage; the table offers only this door for "wounded".
- *reach:* W8's "men on every road" strains the Wardens' canon strength.
**What works:** the best Writers story. Scheme (redraw the edge a mile nearer so lords pay for the wall) → close the camp and keep its journals → wound the leader → the surveyor's son smuggles the true readings out in pieces by relay-loft pigeon, carrying the last himself. Every step uses the enemy's `men`/`kin`; the heart (H5b, a lost surveyor's son) is exactly the person for the cargo; the old debt (the Wayhouse certified the false line a generation ago) gives the guild its own guilt. Memorable image and title.

### 7103 · The Letters at the Simmer — coherence 3 · creativity 2
**Case against:** this is 7101 again (W1, S7b, M3, C3a, nearly the same endings) with a different heart, odd job and blow; two of the four Writers stories tell one plot. The odd job (green samples, a third journal "lost"; the Hand sent word ahead to a headman) has nothing to do with leaking debts: an "and also". The player is jailed in a lord's cells "on her paper": a hall master's `paper` is records, not writs, and the summary gives no charge.
**Breaks:**
- *odd job:* O5c is unreadable against W1's scheme.
- *heart:* H5a (surveyor's son with an unread journal) has no tie to court rolls; his village burning is the only link.
- *blow:* arrested by a Within hall master, no stated instrument or charge. Allowed by the table; unmotivated in the story.
- *repetition:* near-copy of 7101.
**What works:** the Fugitive door after an arrest chains properly, and Schism judged by the leader's letter is a good Writers texture.

### 7104 · A False Copy for the Toll Court — coherence 2 · creativity 3
**Case against:** the heart is held captive in chapter 2 "under a charge she laid", and then at the crisis the heart is (a) the crisis giver and (b) the active party in a public hearing. A chain may assume a card was reached, never how it ended; nothing frees her. The heart (H5a) is accused of *falsely copying the court rolls*, but H5a is the son who *can't read his mother's journal*; H5b (copies at the reading room) or H4 would fit. The W1c/debts contradiction from 7101 recurs. Two charges in a row (captive under a charge; accused before the court) are the same lever twice.
**Breaks:**
- *heart state:* captive after the blow, then giver and party at the crisis (assembly §4 ordering clause not applied).
- *heart type:* C7 needs a copyist; H5a isn't one.
- *variant:* W1c readings vs debts.
- *climb:* blow and crisis are both "a charge she laid".
**What works:** M9 (dismissing the copyist who asked who kept the rolls) is a sharp, in-character Within move, and "the halls' commission rides on the hearing" is a real stake.

**Writers variety: 2.** W1 three times out of four, all on S7b (the toll lord's court rolls); M3 twice; C3a twice; the surveyor's-son heart three times; O4 three times; *take a side at last* in all four ending sets. W1 reaches three stakes and three crises and is the kit's most "legal" enemy, so without a ledger it dominates. Only 7102 is a different story.

## 2. The Lantern Company

### 7101 · The Oldest Contract — coherence 3 · creativity 4
**Case against:** the heart is the Lamp Room's old lamp-keeper (thirty years trimming lamps), yet the crisis has "the caravan the heart rides with" and "the heart's own old chapter". A lamp-keeper doesn't ride caravans. The odd job (three contracts "closed as lost" under one signature; the client's factor is the Hand) needs the Free Lord to be a client who abandons chapters, which his scheme doesn't explain. The ch1 move already binds the Company to his side; the crisis then sets a chapter against the Company's escort, which only reads if the escort's caravan is his feud's target, and the summary doesn't say so.
**Breaks:**
- *heart type:* H5a (veteran lamp-keeper) in a crisis (C7c) built for a caravan rider; "her old chapter" is invented to make it fit.
- *odd job:* O4c's abandoned chapters aren't readable against L1's scheme.
- *blow:* a lord's `name` putting the player down a rank at the board is allowed, but no one says why the board listens.
**What works:** "the Company on both sides" (a Lantern chapter under contract against a Lantern escort, and the code won't let either refuse) is a genuinely good situation, the quartermaster's dilemma from `leaders.md` made concrete. Code-break "hand the cargo over" is the right knife.

### 7102 · Rides at Cost — coherence 4 · creativity 3
**Case against:** standard "the creditor owns you" plot; the stake variant (S1c, Lanternhall's *right to hold its own people for judgment*) is never addressed; the heart (a Lantern's widow who keeps the inn by the lamp grave) has unspecified "kin" in the chapter held. The Contest door (*The ladder*) is a thin fit for a mercenary company.
**Breaks:**
- *heart:* H4c's kin among the chapter is asserted, not given (variant b, the son who wants to sign on, would have supplied it).
- *stake variant:* S1c unused.
**What works:** the cleanest Lantern chain: buys the debts → posts his terms on the Company's own board → the player is demoted on the biggest client's word → he calls the debts in and holds the chapter at Lanternhall to work them off. Every step is `coin`; the cost kind (borrowing against next season sold the Company's work) is honest. The odd job (three chapters his factor won't ransom) is the one O4a that actually fits its enemy.

### 7103 · False Calls from the Hills — coherence 2 · creativity 4
**Case against:** the heart is a client, a caravan master with three family wagons, and chapter 2 has her "cried down for abandoning a comrade": a Lantern code-break applied to someone who isn't a Lantern. The crisis then names "the heart's chapter". She has none. The door and the crisis are both Dispute *Public offense*, back to back. The odd job (O4a, a client's factor who won't ransom chapters) is a hill headman's proxy in a position he has no way to hold.
**Breaks:**
- *heart type:* H3 (client) under a code-break blow and in a crisis (C8) that needs a member's chapter. Two breaks from one roll.
- *door = crisis:* Dispute *Public offense* twice in a row.
- *odd job:* O4a unreadable against L6 (and the same odd job as 7102).
**What works:** the premise is the best in the Lantern kit: a chapter held a hill village for a lord under contract, the headman's son died at its gate, and now his false calls for help turn the villages against any chapter that comes. The accuser is right. With a member heart (H1, H2) this would be a 4.

### 7104 · Called In at Dawn — coherence 4 · creativity 3
**Case against:** same enemy and variant as 7101 (L1a); the odd job (a factor kidnapped to an empty road fort, captor linked to the Hand) is not something a Free Lord calling in a contract needs; the lord's men holding a chapter "in Lanternhall's own yard" is odd ground.
**Breaks:**
- *odd job:* O2b unreadable against L1.
- *repetition:* L1a twice in four worlds.
**What works:** calls in the old contract → frames the player for *abandoning a contract* (which is exactly what refusing his call-in looks like; the summary doesn't make the link, but the parts make it) → the seizure order names one chapter, it refuses, and is held, the widow pleading. The frame and the writ climb properly. The writ's casting need (the lordship holds Lanternhall) was recorded.

**Lantern variety: 3.** Four different crises and four heart types, but L1a twice, O4a twice (O4 three times), and *honor it and march* in every ending set. Two of four are "a lord calls in the oldest contract".

## 3. The Old Hands

### 7101 · The Brother at the Ford — coherence 3 · creativity 3
**Case against:** the throughline needs an "and": OH9's scheme (raid the crossings and leave Old Hands seals for the watch) never appears in the move, the blow or the crisis; everything that happens is the feud. The ch1 move (quarter barred for a night) and the ch2 blow (a back room shut) are the same act twice. A rival gang seizing a back room by force gets the Dispute *Broken agreement* door, a lease quarrel.
**Breaks:**
- *throughline:* scheme and feud are two engines; the scheme is decorative.
- *door:* hall seized by `men` → Dispute *Broken agreement*. The door assumes a landlord shut it; here a crew took it.
- *climb:* M8a then "a back room shut" is no escalation.
**What works:** the paired truce lands well: a poisoned cup, one dead each side, the Market blames the forger (the heart's father), and he must be got out through the Old Gate House. The drowned brother at the ford is a real grievance, and the forger's daughter who longs for a Writers' hall is a good heart.

### 7102 · Pages for the Gates — coherence 3 · creativity 4
**Case against:** the old don, whose faction's code is *never go to the law*, has the player arrested by the watch "on his word". `leaders.md` makes him a man who has never raised a blade or his voice; the Old Hands' whole character is that they settle things in back rooms. The stake variant (S2c, the cipher only he reads) sits oddly with pages nailed legibly to gates. The door (Dispute *Public offense*, before the town's judge) has the Old Hands arguing in a public court, which the code also forbids.
**Breaks:**
- *blow vs enemy:* OH1 using the watch breaks the code and the character; the kit's "per-enemy blows" should bar *arrested* under OH1.
- *stake variant:* S2c unaddressed.
**What works:** the strongest Old Hands idea in the sample: the don, ignored by the houses he served (canon: his loyalty is unreturned), will publish every favour he ever did them. The heart is the unknowing heir of a house the Old Hands forged into being; her existence is in the book. The forger's daughter as crisis giver, the letters already with a runner, the seat ending *take the gate*: all earned.

### 7103 · The Gate That Never Closed — coherence 3 · creativity 2
**Case against:** this is 7101's shape again (S1a, M8a, C5, a forger's daughter), with a Seated House lord instead of a crew boss. Why does a lord erasing records have the player demoted? The heart's kin "who saw the scheme at work" are unnamed; her father is dead (H3a), so the kin the crisis needs don't exist on the card.
**Breaks:**
- *blow:* stripped of rank is unmotivated for OH3's scheme (erase records and the Old Hands).
- *heart:* H3a's kin undefined; C5 needs them.
- *repetition:* near-copy of 7101's spine.
**What works:** the lord erasing the forgeries his own house's claims rest on is good canon (the blind archivist-lord), and "his men come into the quarter as a house's guests" is a nice Old Hands texture.

### 7104 · The True Will — coherence 3 · creativity 4
**Case against:** the heart, a house steward, is "cried down for going to the law", and the door is Passage *Unwelcome cargo*: a steward with a tarnished name carried past waypoint headmen. The door is in the amended table (main-quest §9), but as a story it's a non sequitur; nobody needs to smuggle a disgraced steward anywhere. The odd job (a gate officer in the widow's pay waves a barred musician through) gives a minor river house's widow a man at the Alliance's gates. The Betrayal link "by her kin by marriage" is asserted by the cast step; the summary doesn't show it.
**Breaks:**
- *door:* heart disgraced → Passage *Unwelcome cargo* reads as unrelated to the blow.
- *odd job:* O1b's gate officer is beyond OH6's reach (`kin`, `name`, local).
**What works:** a settled Qotiro widow destroying the Old Hands with their own thirty-year-old forgery, one betrothal at a time, with the oldest house's marriages as the stake: the kit's best fit of enemy to stake. The Old Hands' hatred of outsiders makes her the right enemy. Letters read at a feast is the right Old Hands crisis.

**Old Hands variety: 3.** Four different enemies, but two stories share S1a + M8a + C5 and a forger's-daughter heart; *sell to the lords* in all four ending sets, *make the houses need us again* in three.

## 4. The Night Market

### 7101 · The Trap Score — coherence 3 · creativity 3
**Case against:** the reveal is "the big score is a trap, the house's guards wait inside", and then nothing in the story is about the score: the move is a notes refusal, the blow is a crew marched off, the crisis is the truce poisoning. The sentence needs "and struck the feud's first blow". An Old Hands steward getting an exchange to refuse the Market's notes by `name` alone is thin; the heart (a Far Sails widow captaining her husband's boat) has no tie to the stake ("no one owns the Night Market").
**Breaks:**
- *throughline:* the scheme (the trap) never pays off; the feud replaces it.
- *heart:* H3b's tie to S2 and to the dead man is cast-step glue.
**What works:** the paired crisis is strong on this side too: the Market's dead was his kill, and the old don shields him (Blood debt under *Protected*). The Old Hands steward tipping the watch (the odd job) is the Old Hands' rot, as the kit notes.

### 7102 · The Toll Lord's Bill — coherence 3 · creativity 2
**Case against:** the heart is the lamed dockhand who brought you in, and the crisis names him *the forger of the Market's notes*; the kit has a note-forger heart (H5) for exactly this. The want is "blackmail the Market into service"; the crisis destroys the Market's credit, which is what you do to a thing you want gone, not a thing you want to use. The odd job (a wanted-back house seal with a fence's mark the Market never uses; the Hand fenced it) has a toll lord's man fencing stolen goods. The hostage-as-guest variant (N4b) is never used.
**Breaks:**
- *heart type:* a dockhand named as the forger (C4).
- *want vs crisis:* blackmail-into-service vs cry-down-the-notes.
- *odd job:* O1c unreadable against N4.
**What works:** list read out → crew arrested → notes cried down is a steady squeeze, and "cried down before the heart's own family" is a good variant detail.

### 7103 · Bounty by Pigeon — coherence 2 · creativity 3
**Case against:** the heart is held for a bought debt in chapter 2 and then gives the crisis. The door is Rescue *Kidnapped* for a debt-hold (the table's *Bound by debt* is the premise for that). The crisis has the bounty captain holding the fences' list "as surety for a debt the crews owe him that he bought": a bounty hunter who sells crew names for lords' coin has no reason to be the crews' creditor, and the scheme (bounties) never pays off. He buys debts twice (the heart's, then the crews').
**Breaks:**
- *heart state:* captive, then giver.
- *door:* debt-hold → *Kidnapped* instead of *Bound by debt*.
- *crisis vs enemy:* C5's creditor role doesn't fit N6's scheme; "and also".
- *climb:* the same lever (buy a debt) twice.
**What works:** the fence bought at the card table in front of the crews, the names by pigeon from a cove: good Market texture and a fun title.

### 7104 · The Carter's Son — coherence 4 · creativity 4
**Case against:** the leader is "wounded, through his people inside": N7 has `name`, `craft`, `inside` and no `men`; the kit's row says wounded needs `men`. The odd job is O1c for the third Night Market world in a row, and the heart is the lamed dockhand again (as 7102). The Sabotage door is at least plausible here (a rigged cart, say), but the summary doesn't say.
**Breaks:**
- *blow means:* wounded via `inside`.
- *repetition:* O1c three times; H1a twice.
**What works:** the one Night Market story whose scheme pays off end to end: the eldest carter shows the toll men the trails one at a time (scheme) → shows them the plain door (move, on the stake "the way in") → the leader is struck → a raid comes tonight with him riding along, and the whole market must move by dawn. A revenge that reads as grief (his son drowned fleeing the toll men over the Market's load), from the Road Crews, the faction that actually hauls. Memorable.

**Night Market variety: 3.** Four different enemies and crises, but O1c three times, the dockhand mentor three times, and *go straight* in all four sets. Two stories (7101, 7103) don't use their enemy's scheme.

## 5. The Dueling Society

### 7101 · The Prize That Kills — coherence 2 · creativity 3
**Case against:** the heart is taken and held in chapter 2 (door Rescue *Bound by debt*), and the crisis's last move is "his men take the heart and the top rungs to work them off" (Rescue *Bound by debt*): the same premise twice and the heart captured twice. The enemy's kind is *the cost* (nobody planned the harm; he keeps giving a prize that happens to kill), yet his Hand sabotages a practice blade with a real edge (odd job) and he buys up the ladder's entry debts to press fighters (crisis). Those are plots, not the protection of what pays him. The stake variant (S4a, the ladder board) is barely touched.
**Breaks:**
- *heart state / door = crisis:* captive blow, then re-captured by the same premise.
- *kind:* odd job O2a and crisis C6 contradict *the cost*; C6 should carry *not under the cost*.
- *blow motive:* why a master-at-arms seizes one sport fighter is never given.
**What works:** the idea (the tournament's prize, a post in a lord's guard, has sent the last champions to die in his feud, and the Society keeps offering it because he funds the tournament) is the best "cost" revelation in the sample. The execution buries it under debt-bondage.

### 7102 · The Book on the Ladder — coherence 2 · creativity 3
**Case against:** the heart is a farmer who comes to watch bouts because the Society settled his family's feud; the crisis names him the ladder's fixer "whose wins were bought without her knowing". He has no wins. The kit's own compatibility note gives C3 and C6 fighting hearts; C9 needed one too. The heart is captive after chapter 2 and gives the crisis. The summary switches the heart's gender twice. Why a bookmaker buys a farmer's debts is never said.
**Breaks:**
- *heart type:* H5a (client, farmer) in C9 (a fighter whose rung was bought).
- *heart state:* captive, then giver.
- *blow motive:* a fixed-book bookmaker seizing a spectator.
**What works:** the ladder-keeper in love with the bookmaker, who confesses and runs at the season's draw while she has already fled: a small human story worth keeping.

### 7103 · First Blood on the Card — coherence 3 · creativity 4
**Case against:** the player is framed for *throwing a bout*, and the door is Investigate *Theft*. Nothing was stolen; the table's other option (*Sabotage*) or the Contest family's *The fix* would be what a thrown bout is. The odd job (a fixed bout paid in wager slips) is a fixing scheme, not a selling one. The ladder-keeper heart is "struck off the ladder" (C5b) though keepers aren't on it.
**Breaks:**
- *door:* framed (thrown bout) → Investigate *Theft*.
- *odd job:* O1a is a bookmaker's job, not the showman's.
**What works:** the sister giving the reveal against her brother; the showman overruling the yield rule in the Ring itself to make fighters saleable on blood terms; his followers taking the Ring; Schism judged by the sister with the eldest ring-keepers; code-break "meet him on blood terms". This is the founders' story from `leaders.md` (the one who killed and chose to stop, against the one who loves the crowd), and the parts line up.

### 7104 · Sold to the Pit — coherence 3 · creativity 3
**Case against:** D3c again, with M7 again: two of four Dueling stories are the showman's first-blood card. "Fighters are bonded for debts he bought": the showman is himself the debtor (he sells fighters *to pay the Ring's debts*); the kit's per-enemy list says *members taken (his own fighters, sold)*, not bonded for bought debts. The enemy variant (c, a Free Lord's feast-night bouts) and the crisis variant (b, a toll-town pit) disagree about where fighters go.
**Breaks:**
- *blow detail:* "bonded for debts he bought" vs a debtor enemy and the kit's own note (sold).
- *variant agreement:* D3c (feast nights) vs C3b (toll-town pit).
- *repetition:* same enemy and move as 7103.
**What works:** sells fighters → first-blood bouts → fighters bonded → the heart (a war veteran who chose sport) sold into a pit and badly hurt. The veteran is the right heart for C3 and for the Society's whole argument. Betrayal lands.

**Dueling variety: 2.** D3c twice with the same move; Rescue *Bound by debt* three times across doors and crises; two captive hearts; *keep it sport* in all four sets, *teach the deadly half* in three. The kit widened to nine enemies, but this sample saw three.

## 6. The Holy House

### 7101 · Grain for the Line — coherence 4 · creativity 5
**Case against:** the stake variant (S2c, the Low Shrine's right to keep its doors open at night) is never in play; the crisis reading is just "the riders come to the Low Shrine". A half-deaf parish priest (H1b) as the one who carried a year's grain is a slightly odd body for the job. The Ruin from above door for a frame by a Middle priest needs a shield (*Bought* or *Protected*) the summary doesn't name.
**Breaks:**
- *stake variant:* S2c unused.
- *door detail:* shield unstated.
**What works:** the only story in the sample where the enemy is a good man. A sincere priest empties the tithe-houses into the hungry's bowls and writes false receipts; he feeds the line in the open and calls it the house's duty; when the player gets close he lets the parish think it was the player who cried theft; the receipts are found, the riders come at the evening meal, and the mentor who carried the grain is named the thief. Code-break: tell the parish where the grain went. Every ending is a real choice. This would hold up hand-written.

### 7102 · Plaster in the Case — coherence 4 · creativity 3
**Case against:** the player is framed for *shaming the house in public* and the door is Investigate *Theft*; the frame isn't a theft. Why the relic-seller frames the player at all is unsaid. *Keep the sword* as an ending to a relic fraud is tonally off (it fits by the `thing` hit, not by sense). Low stakes for a guild story: a con exposed.
**Breaks:**
- *door:* framed (shaming) → Investigate *Theft*.
- *ending fit:* *keep the sword* fits the tag, not the story.
**What works:** tight and complete: the procession relic is his copy, the true one under his stall; he shows a "recovered piece" with a bought keeper swearing; on the feast day a carver reads the maker's mark, he is gone, and the sincere parish priest who swore to it is cast out. A pilgrim who bought a piece gives the crisis. Small, clear, sad.

### 7103 · The Penance Yard — coherence 3 · creativity 4
**Case against:** the blow is *outlawed*: a Binder debt broker obtains a lord's writ shutting the house's shrines in his lordship. His living is the penance debts the inquisitors' book produces; shutting the shrines shuts his own supply. It also contradicts *the cost* (he only protects what pays). The roller recorded 0 writs for this story though a `paper` guild-target blow "always counts" (faction-stories §9), so writ fatigue was undercounted in 7103.
**Breaks:**
- *blow vs scheme and kind:* outlawed by HH5; "and also", and against his interest.
- *bookkeeping:* the writ not counted.
**What works:** the penance debts bought at the tithe-house door in front of the town; a novice on home leave, the third never to reach the Mount (a slaver's odd job that fits); the debts sold by letter, the order's own riders penning the parish's poor in the Mount's penance yard to march at dawn; the priestess giving the crisis; code-break "read the sale aloud on the Mount's steps". Canon-rich and grim in the right way.

### 7104 · Taken from the Steps — coherence 4 · creativity 4
**Case against:** the odd job (a big-town shrine's offering box opened with a second key; the elder's Hand did it) has a True Line elder's man robbing a poor box, which buys him nothing his scheme needs. The heart (an old rider turned beekeeper) acquires "kin with fields" by the cast step only.
**Breaks:**
- *odd job:* O2b unreadable against HH7.
- *heart:* H4b's kin supplied by casting, not by the heart's own spec.
**What works:** a True Line elder buys the inquisitors with grain and horses so heresy falls on the Seated Houses' clients and his older claim looks cleaner (canon: the True Line courts the Temple); his bought inquisitors take a man from a shrine's steps (the sanctuary stake, hit squarely); the player is put down a rank in the order; at the feast-day judgment the heart's kin are found heretics and "their fields' boundary stones are already moved for his house". That last image is the sample's best single line. Endings all fit.

**Holy House variety: 5.** Four different enemies, stakes, crises, heart types and odd jobs; only *break the house* recurs (it fits *any*). The one kit that read as four stories.

Answer to Laz's question: the stories mostly make sense as spines (16 of 24 at 3 or above), but only four would pass as hand-written, and seven have a break a player would notice (a heart doing something she can't do, or being in two places at once). Creativity is carried by the kits' premises (which are good) more than by the assembly, and a weak assembly can bury a good premise (Lantern 7103, Dueling 7101).

### Failure patterns, with counts and the fix

1. **Doors are chosen by target + event only, not by how the blow fell (8).** Framed for a thrown bout → Investigate *Theft* (D 7103); framed for shaming the house → Investigate *Theft* (HH 7102); a back room seized by a rival gang's men → Dispute *Broken agreement* (OH 7101); held for a bought debt → Rescue *Kidnapped* (NM 7103); a steward cried down → Passage *Unwelcome cargo* (OH 7104); a leader wounded on the road by men → Investigate *Sabotage* (W 7101, W 7102, NM 7104).
   *Fix:* key the door on (event, `uses`, detail). *Framed*: the door's premise must be the code-break's own family (a thrown bout → Investigate *Sabotage* or Contest *The fix*; shaming → Dispute/Reckoning; a theft only when the frame is a theft). *Captive* / *members taken* for a debt → always *Bound by debt*. *Hall seized* by `men` → Acquire *Under seal* (what reopens it), never a lease dispute. *Leader wounded*: add a second door for an ambush by `men` (Reckoning *Blood debt* is barred without a death; a Threat or Investigate *Suspicious death*-style "attempt" premise is needed) and keep *Sabotage* for `inside`.

2. **The heart's type isn't checked against the crisis or blow role (7).** A client caravan master disgraced under the Lantern code and given "her chapter" (L 7103); a lamp-keeper riding with a caravan (L 7101); a farmer named as a fighter whose wins were bought (D 7102); a dockhand named the note-forger (NM 7102); a surveyor's son who can't read accused of a false copy (W 7104); an old clerk's "village" (W 7101); kin the heart doesn't have (OH 7103, L 7102, HH 7104 by cast step).
   *Fix:* every crisis and every heart-target blow carries `heart_fits` (the Dueling kit already has four such notes; make it a tag in all six kits) and the roller enforces it. *Disgraced* and *framed* under the code need a member heart (never H3 client). A crisis that needs "the heart's kin" needs a heart whose spec has kin.

3. **A captive heart is then active at the crisis (4 of 5 captive stories).** Giver and/or party while still held: W 7104, D 7102, NM 7103; re-captured by the same premise: D 7101.
   *Fix:* when the blow is *heart captive*, bar crisis variants whose giver is the heart (reroll the variant or substitute the fallback, as assembly §4's ordering clause says), bar crisis premises equal to the door's, and bar crisis last moves that take the heart.

4. **The odd job's Hand does something the scheme doesn't need (9).** W 7103 (green samples), L 7101, L 7103, L 7104, OH 7104 (gate officer), NM 7102/7103/7104 (O1c, a fenced seal, three times), D 7101 (sabotage under *the cost*), HH 7104 (poor box).
   *Fix:* give odd jobs `fits` like endings (wants, kinds, or enemies), e.g. O1c needs an enemy with `craft` or `coin` who fences; sabotage odd jobs never under *the cost*; a slaver's odd job for HH5, a debt one for L8. Readability against the scheme is already the throughline's own test (point 6); extend it to the odd job.

5. **The scheme never pays off; the sentence needs an "and also" (5).** Both paired stories (OH 7101, NM 7101: the feud replaces the scheme), NM 7103 (a bounty hunter ends as a creditor), D 7101 (the prize that kills ends in debt bondage), HH 7103 (a broker outlaws the shrines that make his debts).
   *Fix:* for paired worlds, the first striker's scheme must *be* the feud's cause (F1–F4 chosen from the scheme, and the scheme line rewritten to it), or the paired enemies' scheme lines should be feud lines. Add `limits` on crises for kinds (Dueling C6 and Night Market C5 *not under the cost* / wants greed only) and per-enemy blows for HH5 (never *outlawed*).

6. **A variant changes the "what" and the readings don't follow (4).** W1c leaks boundary readings while the base scheme and C3's reading say debts (W 7101, W 7104); S2c (a cipher only the don reads) vs pages nailed to gates (OH 7102); D3c (feast-night bouts) vs C3b (a toll-town pit) (D 7104).
   *Fix:* a variant may change who/why/how, never *what is leaked, sold or held*; W1c should be its own enemy with its own stake (S6) and readings, or be dropped. Crisis variants that name a place must agree with the enemy variant's place, or the throughline sentence should be written from the variant, not the base line.

7. **Blow means read from the row, not the sub-event (2).** Leader *wounded* via `inside` under enemies with no `men` (W 7101, NM 7104); the kit's row gives wounded `men`, disgraced `inside`.
   *Fix:* the roller should read the per-how means in the kit's blow rows, not the cell's union.

8. **The blow repeats the move, or the crisis repeats the blow (4).** Quarter barred then back room shut (OH 7101); a fighter taken then fighters taken (D 7101); a charge laid then a charge laid (W 7104); a debt bought then a debt bought (NM 7103).
   *Fix:* a story's three steps use three different levers (Story craft already asks for two); bar a crisis premise equal to the door's.

9. **The enemy's code or character is contradicted (1).** The old don has the player arrested by the watch (OH 7102): the Old Hands never go to the law. *Fix:* per-enemy blows for OH1 (never *arrested*, never *members taken* by the watch).

10. **Repetition without the ledger.** W1/S7b three of four, D3c two of four, L1a two of four, O1c three of four, O4a two of four, the same heart twice in three kits. The `recent` list would catch the enemy repeats across worlds, but the within-kit skew is structural: W1 reaches three stakes and three crises and is the only robust Within Writers enemy, so it dominates even with a ledger. *Fix:* weight enemies inversely to their legal-pair count at the roll, or widen W4/W6/W7's reaches.

11. **Bookkeeping (1).** HH 7103's *outlawed* blow by `paper` was not counted as a writ.

### The three best
1. **Holy House 7101 · Grain for the Line** (4 / 5): a sincere priest as the enemy, every step his, the player's own mentor named the thief, and every ending a moral choice. The only story that reads as written by someone.
2. **Writers 7102 · The Mile That Isn't There** (4 / 4): the one Writers spine that isn't W1; the surveyor's son carrying the true readings out by pigeon is exactly who the crisis needs, and the old debt (the Wayhouse certified the false line) makes the guild complicit.
3. **Night Market 7104 · The Carter's Son** (4 / 4): the scheme pays off end to end (trails → the plain door → the raid), the grievance is real, and the Road Crews canon gives it weight. Holy House 7104 (*Taken from the Steps*) is a close fourth for the boundary stones.

### The three worst
1. **Dueling 7101 · The Prize That Kills** (2 / 3): the heart is captured twice by the same premise; the odd job and the crisis are plots under a "nobody planned it" revelation; a strong idea left unused.
2. **Dueling 7102 · The Book on the Ladder** (2 / 3): a spectator farmer named as the fixer of wins he never had, while still a captive, giving the crisis.
3. **Lantern 7103 · False Calls from the Hills** (2 / 4): the best Lantern premise in the kit, broken by a client heart put under the Company's code and given a chapter she doesn't have, with the same Dispute twice. Writers 7104 and Night Market 7103 are the next two down, for the captive-then-active heart and the "and also" crisis.
