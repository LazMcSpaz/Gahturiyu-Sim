# Review — v6d guild narratives (24 stories, worlds 7101–7104)

Independent read of `guild-narratives-v6d.md`, judged against `guild-kits.md`, `faction-stories.md` §6 and §11a, `assembly.md` §4, with canon from `world-factions.md` and `leaders.md`. Nothing else under `test/world/` was read. Scores: 5 would pass as a hand-written questline; 3 works but feels assembled; 1 doesn't make sense.

## The case against a high score (written before scoring)

Read cold, as a player would meet them, these 24 stories have one shape: a one-line villain, a public slight, a blow on someone, a showdown at headquarters. The villain's scheme is announced in a reveal and then, in about half the stories, never actually happens: the score that is a trap is never sprung, the deadly score is never attempted, the forged notes are never cried down, the headman's false calls for help never arrive. The chapter-2 blow is in most stories a reason-free act ("you are put down a rank on his word", "you are framed for abandoning a comrade") that the story never motivates and that changes nothing downstream. Three Writers stories are the same Warden captain closing the same survey camp; two Dueling stories are the same village coming for the same mill; two Old Hands stories are the same cache keeper writing the same letters; two Holy House stories end in the same yard with the same priestess speaking the same line. Several hearts cannot do what the crisis needs them to do (a surveyor's son who was never at the camp must "carry the true readings he witnessed"; a half-blind reading master is accused of "drawing the edge maps false"; a farm boy on your rung is marched off under a prize he never won). And in four stories the final move is something the enemy, as written, would never do: a man whose living depends on nobody knowing about the captives he moves writes letters to three houses telling everyone what the Old Hands carried. The tags all pass. The sentences all parse. A reader still has to supply the connective tissue, and in a quarter of the stories no tissue would hold.

That is the honest ceiling: nothing here is a 5, and the mean should sit at 3.

**W-7101 The Leader's Own Keeping** (W6a · S7b · H4a · O1a · M3 · stripped → Restore · C7a)
The leader lets every other copy of the land rolls go astray; she takes a toll lord's court rolls into her own keeping; you are put down a rank; she accuses the heart before a court of a false copy.
- *Break (detail disagreement):* the stake is a **toll lord's** court (S7b); the crisis is "before a **market lord's** court on market day" (C7a). The halls' commission for one lord's rolls can't ride on another lord's hearing. Cause: C7a's variant text fixes a court the stake variant contradicts.

- *Unmotivated blow:* why is the player put down a rank? Inferable (you were told the secret), never supplied.
- **Coherence 3. Creativity 3** — the leader as a patient villain is a good premise; the trial-by-copy crisis is fine; the blow is filler.

### The Lantern Company

### Recurring failure patterns, with counts and the fix that would stop each

1. **The heart's *variant* can't fill the crisis's role** (5 clear, 2 minor): W-7103 (a surveyor's son who was never at the camp is the witness-cargo), W-7104 (a reading master "drew the edge maps"), L-7103 (a recruit with a ransomed sister has "kin among the riders"), D-7101 (a low-rung farm boy is called up under a prize he never won), HH-7101 (a former-thief novice's "village"); minor: OH-7102 (a forger named "the carrier"), HH-7104. *Cause:* the `heart` tag is on entries; the role-relevant fact is in the variant. *Fix:* put heart fits on variants (the kit already allows "a variant letter where only that variant fits" — make it the rule wherever a crisis names what the heart did, holds or has), or forbid variants from changing anything a crisis can need (village, kin, trade, access).

2. **The last move is something this enemy would never do** (4): OH-7102 and OH-7104 (a cost enemy who lives on secrecy publishes his own trade), OH-7103 (a schemer who needs forgers silent hands one to a magistrate), HH-7104 (a cost enemy sells parishioners into bondage). *Cause:* moves carry `cost` and want limits; crises mostly don't, and throughline point 5 is in practice checked on the move only. *Fix:* give every crisis the same `cost` flag and `limits: wants …` moves have, and roll only cost-marked crises under cost enemies. Old Hands C1 in particular needs *never under the cost*.

3. **The scheme is announced and never happens** (4): N-7101 (the trap is never sprung), N-7103 (the deadly score is never attempted), N-7104 (the notes are never cried down), L-7101 (the false calls never come). *Cause:* nothing requires any step to *be* the scheme's payload; "the want survives the last move" guards against the opposite failure. *Fix:* each enemy entry names its payload event, and the roll fails unless the move, the blow or the last move is that event (or, for the paired syndicates, the first striker's payload is spent as the feud's first blow).

4. **A door assumes an event the story never supplied** (2): W-7102 and D-7101, "Captured in battle" with no battle. *Cause:* the kits' cells say "marched off" / "pressed"; assembly's `men (Above)` row says "taken in a fight". *Fix:* make the kit cell the authority and split assembly's row: pressed or marched off → Kidnapped, taken in a fight → Captured in battle, chosen by the kit's own words.

5. **A move or odd-job variant has nothing to do with this enemy** (5): N-7104 (a High broker daring dockhands at cards), W-7104 (a loan roll under the edge-map captain), W-7101 (a widow's letters under the land-rolls leader), D-7104 (a ring-keeper abducted under the uncle), D-7102/7103 ("killers' yard" for a man wronged by unfair terms). *Cause:* odd-job `fits` and "any stake" moves are per entry, with no reading per enemy or variant. *Fix:* `fits` on odd-job variants, not entries; no move takes `aims: any` without a per-enemy reading; move wordings that bake in an accusation ("killers' yard") take a brace filled from the enemy's grievance.

6. **The enemy's reach can't do what the move or crisis says** (5): L-7101 (a local hill headman closes and then raids the salt road out of the Company's HQ town), W-7102/7103 (a Warden captain seals every road out of Highcrossing), HH-7101 (a Last Wave raider cries in Roduro markets), OH-7103 (a True Line schemer dictates a house's public acts), N-7104 (a High broker at a dock game). *Cause:* readings don't carry reach or standing; several stakes (Lantern S6, Writers' Highcrossing, the Old Gate House) are pinned to HQ towns. *Fix:* a `reach` need on readings that act at a headquarters town or across a region, and a standing check (High enemies act through agents on Middle-tier moves).

7. **A detail a variant's prose takes for granted that the stake never supplied** (5): W-7101 (toll lord vs market lord), W-7104 ("the very court the copy was made for" with no court), N-7104 (who pledged the fences' list), HH-7103 and HH-7104 (whose riders; who buys), L-7103 (who ambushes). *Fix:* variant texts never name a specific the stake or enemy should supply; they take braces ({the stake's court}, {the enemy's men or the ambushers his means allow}), as assembly's door table already does.

8. **The blow and the crisis are the same event twice** (2): D-7101 (fighters taken for the guard, twice), HH-7102 (the heart publicly accused, twice). *Fix:* "no step repeats" compares who is acted on and how (taken, accused, shut), not family + premise alone.

9. **Stake variant unused or contradicted** (4, minor): N-7101 (S2a's toll lord never appears), N-7102 (the strongroom vs the way in), HH-7104 (one tithe-house vs one valley), W-7101. *Fix:* roll the stake variant last, among those the chosen readings touch.

10. **Giver fit** (3, minor): HH-7101 and HH-7104 (the militant high priest reveals his own order's or his own tithe's cost), HH-7102 (a sincere priest cries down a fellow of his own way). *Fix:* a reveal giver list per enemy, as the crisis variants already have.

Also one rule gap: Old Hands C1 names the heart but lacks the *herself* tag the sibling crises carry, so a captive heart was accused in OH-7104.

## Judgment: is it converging?

**On legality, yes; on sense, no — and the two are coming apart.** Every v6d join I could check held: no thrown bout became a theft, no captive heart gave a crisis, no Within syndicate enemy used the watch, hall seizures didn't follow a shut, doors matched how the blow fell. Those classes of break are gone from this sample. The mean did not move (3.04 against 3.08) because the breaks moved down one level of granularity, to exactly where the tags stop: entry-level fits hold, variant-level ones fail (patterns 1, 5, 7, 9); moves have want/cost limits, crises don't (pattern 2); the parts are checked for compatibility, never for whether the scheme is ever *spent* (pattern 3). Each fix is a new hand-written joint, and the number of joints grows as enemies × variants × stakes × hearts × crises, so the fix list grows with the kits. This is the "each fix moves the breaks" outcome.

Two kinds of fix would end whole classes rather than instances:

- **Push the tags down to the level where the facts live, or stop variants from changing facts.** The kit's rule "a variant changes who, why or how, never the tags" is now the main source of breaks: a variant changes the heart's village, the odd job's record, the court the crisis sits before, and the tags don't follow. Either variants carry their own `fits`/`heart`/reach tags, or variants are confined to colour that no other part can depend on. This closes patterns 1, 5, 7, 9 at once.

- **Check the enemy's line, not the parts' compatibility.** Two tests on the enemy as a whole: *the last move fits the want* (crises get `cost` and `wants` like moves) and *the scheme is spent* (one of the three acts is the payload the scheme line promises). This closes patterns 2 and 3. Pattern 4 is a one-line correction (the kit's cell wins over assembly's row).

Beyond that, the sample says where the 4s come from: the stories that work are the ones where the kit *already authored the line* — D7's own C6 reading, N7 with M4 and C2, D8 with C8, HH5 with M5 and C3, L1 with M4 and C1. In those the enemy's scheme, move and crisis were written to each other and the "assembly" was choosing among a few pre-fitted spines. The stories that fail are the ones assembled from pools of generic parts (M7 "any stake", C1 letters, M5 "killers' yard", C6 "any heart"). The approach would converge faster by leaning into that: give each enemy two or three authored spines (scheme → move → payload → crisis, with braces for stake and heart variants) and roll among spines, keeping the pools for colour (hearts, odd jobs, endings). That turns a five-dimensional joint space into a short list of lines a reviewer can read, and it is the shape the stories that scored well already have.

One structural note that no tag will fix: the paired syndicate stories can be legal and still leave both enemies' schemes unspent, because the shared truce is the last move for both. If the pairing stays, the first striker's scheme has to *be* the first blow (the trap sprung, the steward bought), or the pair will always read as two schemes abandoned for a poisoning.
