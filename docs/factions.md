# Factions (Laz, 2026-10-12)
Factions replace the regional leagues from the sim docs. Names were chosen by Laz on 2026-10-12 (see Names).

## Canon notes from this pass
- **The Qotiro came in waves.** Earlier arrivals came in small numbers and didn't found towns of their own; they live mixed into other towns. **The newest wave is different:** these newcomers do have towns, in isolated enclaves. Many earlier Qotiro resent them for undoing the trust they built over generations.
- **The Temple is strictly the Roduro church,** with a lot of overlap with the Horaro, since the two peoples mixed so much.
- **The Roduro and Horaro think of themselves as the first two peoples** (as in `languages.md`), which feeds the Alliance's pride.
- Earlier "decided" lines in other docs were working assumptions, not binding canon (Laz). This doc wins where they disagree.

## Major factions

### The First Peoples (Roduro–Horaro Alliance)
- **Who:** led by Roduro, with Horaro as honoured but junior partners. They call themselves the first two peoples.
- **Traits:** isolationist, stagnant, extremely hierarchical. The old families call the shots, and their power is generations deep. Racist towards the Qotiro and Ṭaḍoro.
- **Role:** the establishment.
  - Rich, safe, well-guarded towns.
  - A hard place for Qotiro or Ṭaḍoro squad members: suspicion, worse prices, closed gates.
  - Quests: old-family feuds, inheritance, keeping the wrong people out.
- **Cracks:** the Horaro half is quietly unhappy (some have already left as the Free Horaro).

### The Great House (the Empire)
- **Who:** the remains of a larger empire that once ruled the island. Mostly Roduro.
- **Traits:** feudal and opportunist. There's an emperor, but most lords do their own thing and ally when it's convenient. Less racist than the Alliance; they'll work with any people to get what they want.
- **Structure: a faction with factions inside it.**
  - **On the map it's one power:** shared borders, the same rules on who's let in, the same laws and customs, the same look (towns, guards, banners).
  - **Underneath:**
    - **The Throne (loyalists):** the emperor's court and soldiers, who want the empire restored for real.
    - **The lords:** each lord, or small alliance of lords, has their own standing with the Throne and with each other. Some are loyal, some ignore the emperor, some are quietly at war.
  - **Reputation works at both levels:** standing with the Empire decides entry to its towns; standing with a lord decides their quests, contracts, and who hunts you. Helping one lord can anger a rival without turning the whole Empire hostile.
  - Lords can rise, fall, switch sides, or be swallowed by the Throne, so the map's politics shift on their own.
- **Slavery:** the lords are the people who profited when the old order fell apart. Buying slaves is one of their habits (see the Binders).
- **Role:** the politics faction. The best employer for a mercenary squad: contracts, escorts, raids on a rival lord.

### The Open Hand (the Upstarts)
- **Who:** well mixed across all four peoples. An established faction, not a ragtag band.
- **Traits:** commercial, all about trade, energetic, growing. Led by a council.
- **Role:** **the people's faction.** They broke away from the old power structures and spread wealth by opening up trade.
  - Caravans, trade posts, the friendliest starting faction.
  - Their growth is a threat to the others: they buy up land, undercut old crafts, and squeeze the lords' tolls and the Alliance's old monopolies.

## Factions within the majors (Laz, 2026-10-12)
Each major has **three sub-factions**: enough that none feels like one block, few enough to keep loyalties readable. On the map each major is still one power (one border, one set of who's let in, one look). Underneath, reputation is held with each sub-faction too (see the engine notes).

### The First Peoples
| Sub-faction | Who | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| **The Seated Houses** | The Roduro houses currently listened to; they run the Alliance | Keep things exactly as they are |
| **The True Line** | A few old Roduro houses claiming an older, purer lineage than the Seated Houses | To be the ones in charge. They scheme, dig up old records, and court the Temple |
| **The Tide Houses** | The Horaro houses still inside the Alliance | Honoured but unheard. Some quietly envy the Free Horaro; some double down on loyalty |

### The Great House
| Sub-faction | Who | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| **The Throne** | The emperor's court and soldiers (the loyalists, the "true imperials") | Restore the empire for real; bring the lords to heel |
| **The Banner Lords** | Lords who still fly the Throne's banner, at least in public | Favour, land and titles from the Throne |
| **The Free Lords** | Lords who ignore the Throne and look after their own holdings | Power for themselves. They ally when it suits and buy slaves without asking |

- Mechanically **each lord is still their own entity**, with their own standing. The two lord groups are how the player reads them; a lord can move between them.

### The Open Hand
| Sub-faction | Who | What they want |
|---|---|---|
| **The Open Council** | The council houses and the trading mainstream; most of the faction | Open trade, growth, wealth spread around |
| **The Sharp Hands** | Cutthroat merchants who've done very well | To corner markets and become the new old money. Happy to screw a partner out of a deal |
| **The Road Crews** | Caravan masters, carters and small traders who do the actual hauling | Fair rates and safe roads. The faction's working heart, and the first to grumble about the Sharp Hands |

## Minor factions

### The Holy House (the Temple)
- The Roduro church (with heavy Horaro overlap), grown corrupt and arrogant.
- Highly militaristic, to stay powerful while people turn away from it.
- **Role:** zealot enforcers. Inquisitor patrols, shakedowns, raids on "heretics". Leans on the Alliance as a patron.

### The Last Wave (Qotiro newcomers)
- The latest and largest wave of Qotiro. They're meeting hostility the only way they know how: as warriors.
- They live in isolated enclaves, with their own towns (priestesses, mess halls, duels, smoke halls). Many see them as an imminent threat.
- The earlier, settled Qotiro often resent them for undermining hard-won trust.
- **Role:** a threat that fulfils itself. Their enclaves are dangerous to enter and rewarding to befriend. The player can be a bridge, or the spark for open war.

### The Far Sails (Free Horaro)
- Horaro who split off from the stagnant Alliance. Somewhere between the First Peoples and the Open Hand in outlook.
- **A naval power:** ships, ferries and sea lanes. Not many communities.
- **Role:** control of the sea, a refuge, and a sign the Alliance is cracking.

### The Binders (slavers)
- **Mainly Qotiro** (Laz, 2026-10-12). Slavery was always allowed in Qotiro custom, and slave-taking is how this group **bought its place in the old empire**: they supplied the lords with labour, and the lords gave them standing in return.
- They take people into servitude (debtors, captives, the unlucky) and sell them to the lords, mostly the Free Lords.
- They give the Qotiro a third face: the settled earlier waves, the Last Wave newcomers, and the Binders, who many Qotiro would rather not be associated with.
- **Role:** the main human predator on the roads. Slave raids, people to free, and the way a squad member can end up enslaved.

### The Wardens
- Like the Night's Watch: a small order holding the line against the Overgrowth.
- Present in certain towns, always trying to persuade the major powers to help defend the land.
- **Role:** gives the Overgrowth a human front line. Quests into dangerous country; a cause nobody funds.

### The Highfolk (Highland Clans, minor culture)
- Roduro mountain clans in the snowy and rocky mountains.
- The Empire counts them as its own. The clans see themselves as independent, and they're isolated enough that nobody bothers to correct them.
- **Role:** a self-contained culture to discover in the mountains.

### The Rooted (world threat)
- People sworn to Qotihìqì and the Overgrowth. Like Morrowind's Sixth House.
- **Joining is one-way:** a Rooted member can't belong to any other faction.
- **One goal:** cover the island with the Overgrowth.

## Guilds
Minor factions and quest-givers, present in basically every large town.

### The Writers (scholars' guild)
- Ṭaḍoro at the core, open to anyone. The mages' guild equivalent.
- **Offers:** spells, magic manuals, training; enchanting later.
- **Quests:** lost texts, dangerous experiments, studying the Overgrowth.
- **Flavour:** they keep the records, so they know everyone's secrets and debts.

### The Lantern Company
- Mixed-race swords for hire: road guards, caravan escorts, beast hunters, bounty work. The fighters' guild equivalent.
- **Offers:** contracts, combat training, hired guards.
- **Flavour:** they work for anyone who pays, so they're often caught between factions.

### The rival syndicates (Tong-style)
The thieves' guild slot is two rivals at war, like Morrowind's Thieves Guild and Camonna Tong. Join one and you're on the wrong side of the other.
- **The Old Hands (native):** a Roduro syndicate rooted in the old families. Blackmail, forged inheritances, smuggling across the Alliance's closed borders. Hates outsiders, and wants the Qotiro newcomers gone.
- **The Night Market (open):** a mixed smuggling and fencing ring from the Upstart ports. Tariff dodging, stolen goods, forged notes. Anyone can join.
- In Empire lands, both sell to the lords.

### The Dueling Society
- Combat as sport. Relatively new; their philosophy is that you can fight for fun too.
- The old heads dislike them, because they think fighting should be for killing.
- No presence on the world stage. Something the player can take part in now and then, like an arena.

## Voices (Laz, 2026-10-12)
For the assembled dialogue (`emergent-dialogue.md`). Each faction gets its own pool of greetings, remarks and farewells, tagged with the faction as a condition.

**Rules:**
- **Layered voice.** A person's **people** sets the rhythm: Roduro slow and formal, Qotiro clipped, Horaro warm and indirect, Ṭaḍoro quick and curious. Their **faction** sets the attitude, the topics and the pet words. A Roduro in the Open Hand still sounds Roduro, just warmer and more hopeful.
- **Sub-factions add a third, lighter layer** where they differ (the Throne versus a Free Lord).
- **Talk about other factions comes up only when it's relevant:** the squad is tied to that faction, the news involves them, or they're the topic at hand. It's not a constant background topic.
- The lines below are **examples of tone**, a starting set for each pool. Slots are in {braces}.

### Majors

**The First Peoples: cold courtesy**
- Polite on the surface; every line reminds you that you don't belong. They never raise their voice.
- **Topics:** lineage and houses, the world's decline since the old days, keeping order (outsiders, crime, the wrong sort moving in).
- **Pet words:** "house", "line", "proper", "in time", "one's place".
- **Examples:**
  - Greeting: "You may wait by the gate. Someone will see to you."
  - Remark: "The {house} has kept this hill for nine generations. Things were quieter then."
  - On outsiders: "We don't turn people away. We simply don't invite them in."
  - Farewell: "Mind the road. It is not ours past the bridge."
- **Seated Houses:** smooth and confident; everything is fine and will stay that way. "The houses have spoken. There's nothing more to say."
- **True Line:** aggrieved, robbed of their rightful place. "Our blood was here before theirs had a name."
- **Tide Houses:** quietly torn. Loyal in public, doubtful in private. "The elders listen... sometimes." In private, with trust: "My cousin sails with the Far Sails now. Don't repeat that."

**The Great House: mixed by sub-faction**
- **Shared base:** the habits of an empire that's no longer quite there. Titles, ranks, "by leave of".
- **The Throne:** glory and duty. They talk as if the empire still stands and will rise again. "The emperor's peace held this whole island once. It will again." "Kneel or don't. The Throne remembers both."
- **Banner Lords:** courtly and ambitious; they talk up their loyalty and their reward. "My lord flies the Throne's banner. That counts for something here."
- **Free Lords:** transactional; everything is a deal, and rivals are always on their minds. "What can you do for me, and what will it cost?" "Lord {rival} wants my valley. I want Lord {rival} to stop wanting it."

**The Open Hand: idealistic**
- Believers in the cause: open trade frees people and breaks the old powers.
- **Topics:** caravans and new routes, fairness, the old houses' monopolies, people who got out of bondage or poverty.
- **Pet words:** "free", "open", "fair", "everyone", "the road".
- **Examples:**
  - Greeting: "Welcome. Whoever you are, your coin's as good as anyone's, and so's your word."
  - Remark: "Trade frees people. Every caravan breaks a chain."
  - On the old powers: "The First Peoples sit on their hills counting ancestors. We count customers."
  - Farewell: "Open roads, friend."
- **Open Council:** the cause in its best form; earnest and hopeful.
- **Sharp Hands:** smooth flatterers. They use the cause's words, always angling. "For you, a special price. Between friends."
- **Road Crews:** the cause in practice: plain, tired, fair-minded. They grumble about the Sharp Hands. "Council talks about the road. We walk it."

### Minors

**The Holy House: sermonising**
- Every answer is scripture. Grand, heavy, a little threatening underneath.
- **Examples:** "The stone remembers the faithless." "Kneel at the rite, and the house will remember you kindly." "Many have left the shrine. The shrine has not left them."

**The Last Wave: proud, testing**
- Respect is earned, often by standing your ground or a fight. Short and blunt (Qotiro rhythm).
- **Examples:** "You want to talk? Show me you can stand first." "We came here. We stay here. That's all." Once you've earned it: "You stood. Sit. Eat."

**The Far Sails: free and easygoing**
- Warm, joking, unbothered by rank (Horaro warmth, without the Alliance's weight).
- **Examples:** "Titles don't float, friend." "The sea doesn't care whose house you're from." Farewell: "Fair wind. Or any wind. We're not fussy."

**The Binders: predatory**
- Always sizing you up as stock. Polite in a way that makes skin crawl.
- **Examples:** "You travel light. No one waiting for you, I'd guess." "Strong back on that one." To a squad member who looks weak: "Careful on the roads at night, friend. People go missing."

**The Wardens: gallows humour**
- Underpaid, outnumbered, and joking about it.
- **Examples:** "Pay's bad, food's worse, and the trees want to eat you. Interested?" "Another one of us went into the green. We'll drink his share." "Tell your lord the wall's still standing. For now."

**The Highfolk: old-fashioned**
- Archaic Roduro: old words, old courtesies, long sentences. No emperor up here.
- **Examples:** "Your road is long, lowlander." "Sit by the fire and give us your name, and your father's." "The Great House claims these peaks? Let it climb up and say so."

**The Rooted: hollow and strange**
- Not quite human any more. Odd phrasing, "we" instead of "I", slow and calm.
- **Examples:** "We are many. We are growing. You are... not yet." "Stone forgets. Root remembers." "Lie down a while. The green is soft."

### Guilds

**The Writers: curious, digressive**
- Every conversation becomes a lesson (Ṭaḍoro quickness, turned to study).
- **Examples:** "Fascinating. Did you know that word once meant 'twice-forged'?" "Wait, wait, say that again. I want to write it down." "We have three accounts of that battle. All of them disagree. Isn't that wonderful?"

**The Lantern Company: rough camaraderie**
- Soldiers' banter, dark jokes, loyalty to each other.
- **Examples:** "Another job, another scar." "Drinks are on the dead man." "You hold the lantern, we'll hold the line."

**The Old Hands: old and quiet**
- Respectable, coded, never saying the thing outright. They sound like a family business.
- **Examples:** "A friend of the house is a friend of ours." "That matter's been seen to. Don't ask by whom."

**The Night Market: slick**
- Fast, cheeky, street talk.
- **Examples:** "Looking for something you can't buy in daylight?" "Didn't see you, you didn't see me, everybody's happy."

**The Dueling Society: sporting and cheerful**
- Good sports who think fighting can be fun.
- **Examples:** "Good bout! Same time tomorrow?" "Nobody died. That's the point." To an old-school fighter: "I know, I know, real fights kill people. Ours don't. Come watch."

## Depth (Laz, 2026-10-12)
So no faction is one note. Each has a **public face** (what strangers see), a **virtue** (what they're right about), a **wound or flaw**, and a **private side** that shows only to people they trust.

**Public and private voice (Laz):** low trust gets the faction's public line. As trust grows, people start to show their hidden side: doubts, fears and what they actually believe. This uses the trust the dialogue system already tracks. Private lines are their own pool, gated by trust.

Items marked *(Claude)* were filled in to complete the set; change freely.

### The First Peoples
- **Virtue: they remember.** They hold the island's history, its old pacts and its dead. Nobody else does, and when the First Peoples go, that memory goes too.
- **Wound:** pride has turned to stagnation; they're shrinking and won't admit it *(Claude)*.
- **Private fear: the Overgrowth.** Their towns are old and rooted. They can't move, and they know it.
- **Private lines:**
  - "We have records of every house on this island. Do you know how many have gone quiet in my lifetime?"
  - "My grandmother's garden is three days from the green now. It was thirty when she planted it."
- **Sub-factions:** the Seated Houses fear change more than the green; the True Line say the Seated Houses are failing at the one thing that matters, remembering; the Tide Houses wonder if the Far Sails were right.

### The Great House
- **Virtue: a dream of unity.** The Throne genuinely wants one island at peace, as it was under the emperor.
- **Private wound: a weak emperor.** The Throne is hollow, and everyone pretends otherwise. The lords know it, which is why they do as they please.
- **Fear:** that the dream dies with this emperor and nobody even notices *(Claude)*.
- **Private lines:**
  - Throne: "You've seen him. Don't tell me what you saw. Just... help us keep the banner up."
  - Banner Lord: "I bow to the Throne because someone has to. If we all stop, there's nothing left."
  - Free Lord: "An emperor who can't hold a cup isn't going to hold my valley."

### The Open Hand
- **Virtue: they genuinely free people.** Trade has lifted families out of bondage and poverty, and the members believe in it *(Claude, from their idealism)*.
- **Flaw: growing too fast.** They're losing their soul as they get big, and the Sharp Hands are winning.
- **Fear: a crackdown.** The old powers uniting to crush them before they're strong enough.
- **Private lines:**
  - Open Council: "When I joined we knew every carter by name. Now there are ledgers I'm not allowed to read."
  - Road Crews: "If the Great House and the First Peoples ever stop hating each other, they'll come for us first."
  - Sharp Hands (they barely have a private side): "Ideals are lovely. They don't pay for guards."

### The Holy House
- **Public:** sermons and menace.
- **Hidden: true believers inside.** Some priests are sincere and ashamed of what the house has become. They're the house's conscience, and its most likely reformers.
- **Private lines** (from a sincere priest): "We used to feed people. Now we arm them. I pray for the house more than I pray for anyone."

### The Last Wave
- **Public:** proud and testing.
- **Depth: fierce family loyalty.** They're warmest to their own: children, elders and the wounded come first. Once you're counted as one of theirs, you're protected like family.
- **Flaw:** that loyalty runs one way; anyone outside the family is fair game *(Claude)*.
- **Private lines:** "My daughter was born here. This is her land, whatever the old houses say." "You stood by us. Now you eat at our fire, and no one touches you."

### The Far Sails
- **Public:** free and easygoing.
- **Shadow: smuggling.** Freedom pays badly, and many run goods for the Night Market.
- **Private lines:** "Free doesn't feed anyone. So yes, some cargo isn't on the manifest." "Ask me no questions about the hold, friend."

### The Binders
- **Public:** predatory.
- **Depth: reluctant.** Many Binders don't like what they do, but they see it as their **only way of being accepted**: it's what bought the Qotiro a place in the Great House, and they can't see another door.
- **Private lines:** "You think I wanted this? The lords didn't want Qotiro farmers. They wanted this." "My son will be a lord's man, not a Binder. That's what all this buys."

### The Wardens
- **Public:** gallows humour.
- **Underneath: almost every Warden lost someone** (family, or a whole town) to the green. The jokes keep the grief down.
- **Private lines:** "My village was where those trees are now. I can still find the well, if I want to." "Everyone here has a name they don't say. That's why we joke."

### The Highfolk
- **Public:** old-fashioned and proud.
- **Depth: a secret.** They guard something old in the mountains. **What it is stays open** (Laz to decide): a way through the peaks, an old place, something tied to the Overgrowth's past.
- **Private lines:** "Some paths up here we don't show lowlanders. Some we don't show each other." "The mountain keeps what the lowlands forgot."

### The Rooted
- **Public:** hollow and strange.
- **Terrible appeal: the dead return.** The green keeps something of those it takes, and they speak through it. People join to hear a lost child or a husband again.
- **Private lines:** "Your mother is in the roots. She asked for you." "We did not lose them. We kept them. Come and listen."

### The Writers
- **Public:** curious and digressive.
- **Hidden: knowledge is power.** Behind the curiosity, they trade secrets and quietly steer events. Their ledgers hold everyone's debts.
- **Private lines:** "We don't take sides. We just make sure the right side knows the right thing at the right time." "Curiosity is a fine reason. It's also an excellent cover."

### The guilds
- **The Lantern Company: a code.** No abandoning a contract or a comrade. Breaking it is the worst shame a Lantern can carry. Private line: "I've seen a man walk back into a fire for a contract he'd already been paid for. That's the Company."
- **The Old Hands: rotting.** The old families are using them less, and they're getting desperate, so more reckless. Private line: "The houses used to need us. Now they pretend they never did."
- **The Night Market:** slick, no added depth for now.
- **The Dueling Society: mocked, and dangerous.** Old fighters sneer at them, but their best fighters are genuinely deadly. Private line: "They laugh at us for fighting for fun. Then they fight one of us for real."

## Engine notes (for later)
- **Parent and child factions:** every major has sub-factions (three each; the Empire's lords are also individual entities). Relations and reputation are held at both levels. Entry to towns reads the parent; quests and hostility read the child.
- **Lords change over time:** standing between lords and the Throne shifts (alliances, betrayals, takeovers), clock-derived like other town life.
- **One-way faction:** joining the Rooted removes all other memberships and blocks new ones.
- **Rival guilds:** joining one syndicate sets the other hostile.
- **Guild presence:** guild halls or contacts in every large town.
- Faction relations replace the league layer. Save format bump (rule 12).
